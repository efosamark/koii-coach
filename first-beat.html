K.O. II / COACH

CHAPTER 1 / ABOUT 30–50 MINUTES

First beat

Your first beat

90 BPM, one bar, kick + snare + hats, bass and two sections. Hardware instructions follow the official guide; rhythm and mix choices are our teaching recipe.

You will make: a one-bar drum and bass loop at 90 BPM, kept as two scenes, DRUMS and BEAT, and a tiny performance with them.

What you need

Your K.O. II, set up. New K.O. II, or not updated yet? Do Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II first: it updates the unit, backs it up and chooses the empty project this chapter uses.

Your project: empty project 6. Every note you hear is one you placed.

Paper and pencil: you write down two scene numbers, DRUMS and BEAT. Chapter 2 needs them.

Part 1 · Load your kit

  1. Go home, and stop playback

    Press MAIN. If the K.O. II is playing, press PLAY to stop it.

    Why

    On a new K.O. II, projects 1–5 hold factory demos and projects 6–9 are completely empty. Working in project 6 keeps the demos safe, and every note you hear later is one you placed. We will make a one-bar, 90 BPM drum loop, then add bass and a second section. No finger-drumming skill required.

    Not working?

    Not on OS 2.5.1 yet, or music on the unit you care about? Do Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II first (update and backup).

  2. Open empty project 6

    Hold MAIN, then press pad 6 and keep both held while you watch the display; release both once it changes.

    Owners report the display briefly shows P 6; the official guide does not describe it, so trust the silent pads.

    Why

    The official guide says “hold main and one of the pads (1-9) to select an empty project”.

  3. Check that project 6 is silent

    Now tap A, tap pads 1, 2 and 3, then press PLAY.

    Check: The pads make no sound and PLAY gives silence.

    Press PLAY again to stop.

    Why

    Project 6 has no sounds or notes yet.

    Not working?

    Pads still play sounds? You picked a scene, not a project: holding MAIN and typing a number also selects a scene. Try again and keep pad 6 held longer while watching the display. Already used project 6 for something else? Use another empty project and write its number down, but later chapters plan 7 for Chapter 3, 8 for Chapter 5 and 9 for Chapter 6: see help “My project is not empty”. See help: “My project is not empty”.

    Official guide: Get started

  4. Set the tempo to 90 BPM

    Press TEMPO and turn X until the display shows 90 (or hold TEMPO and type 9, 0).

    Check: At 90 BPM one bar (four beats) lasts about 2.7 seconds.

  5. Set the grid to 16, quantized

    Press TIMING and turn X until it shows 16, the default 1/16 grid (not 16T). Press − to choose quantize. Press MAIN.

    Leave Y (swing) at no swing.

    Why

    The recipe uses ordinary 4/4 and one-bar patterns with 16 steps per bar, four per beat. Quantize snaps live playing to the grid; it matters when you record live in the optional practice at the end. Interval and swing apply when you record, not to notes already written.

    Not working?

    If you turned Y before, turn it back to no swing. If you changed the time signature before, MAIN + TEMPO opens it; this recipe needs 4/4. Keep the default one-bar pattern length.

    Official guide: Functions

  6. A1: load the kick

    Press SOUND, tap A, then tap pad 1. Hold SOUND, type 1 on the pads to load sample 001, and release SOUND.

  7. Hear the kick

    Tap pad 1 to hear it (audition).

    Check: Pad 1 makes a low drum sound.

    Why

    Pick a short, low thump, not a long bass note. Factory kicks are stored at 1–99.

    Not working?

    001 is our starting choice, not a guaranteed named sample. If your library differs, use − / + within 1–99 and choose the first short, clean kick you hear. Silent? See help: “A pad is silent”.

    Official guide: Modes

  8. A2: load the snare

    Stay in SOUND. With group A selected, tap pad 2. Hold SOUND, type 1, 0, 0, release SOUND and tap pad 2.

    Check: Pad 2 sounds sharper and brighter than the kick.

    Why

    Choose a crisp snare or clap-like backbeat. Factory snares are stored at 100–199.

    Not working?

    If 100 is unsuitable, browse 100–199 with − / + and pick a short snare. Do not use a long drum loop. See help: “A pad is silent”.

    Official guide: Modes

  9. A3: load a closed hi-hat

    Still in SOUND with group A, tap pad 3. Hold SOUND, type 2, 0, 0, release SOUND and tap pad 3.

    Check: Pad 3 makes a short, bright tick.

    Why

    Choose a short “ts” sound. Factory hi-hats are stored at 200–299; avoid a long open cymbal for this first beat.

    Not working?

    If 200 rings like a long cymbal or your library differs, browse 200–299 for a short closed hat. Your ear decides.

    Official guide: Modes

  10. Make the hats quieter

    Still in SOUND, tap A3. Turn X down gently until the hat is clearly quieter than the kick. Leave Y alone for now.

    Check: Tapping A1 then A3, the hat supports the kick rather than covering it.

    Why

    In SOUND, X is the selected pad’s amplitude (volume) and Y its pitch. Bright hats can dominate even at a lower level.

    Not working?

    There is no magic knob position. Knobs change jobs in different modes, so check you are in SOUND before turning.

    Official guide: Modes

Part 2 · Place the drums

  1. Learn the placement move once

    Press MAIN and make sure playback is stopped. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap A1 once, then release RECORD.

    Check: At 1.1.1 pad A1 lights.

    Why

    This writes the kick at bar 1, beat 1, step 1 (1.1.1). A lit pad means a note is written on this step. Tapping a pad without RECORD only auditions it; nothing is written.

    Not working?

    Use the display as your guide and keep the loop stopped while placing notes. Made a mistake? SHIFT + B is undo while the umbrella icon is lit. Taps not sticking? See help: “My taps do not repeat”.

    Official guide: Play and record

  2. Place all four kicks

    In stopped MAIN with group A, use − / + to reach each position and hold RECORD + A1 at 1.1.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.1 and 1.4.3.

    Check: A1 lights at all four positions, and the kick row matches the target rhythm.

    The placement helper lists them one by one.

    Why

    These are grid steps 1, 7, 9 and 15: a steady foundation with two off-beat pickups. 1.1.1 is already done; do not add it twice.

    Not working?

    Press PLAY to check, then stop before editing. One wrong note? Go to it and briefly press ERASE + that pad. Holding ERASE + a pad until TRK blinks clears all of that pad’s notes, so keep the press short. See help: “The beat has extra hits”.

    Official guide: Play and record

  3. Place the two snares

    In stopped MAIN with group A, go to 1.2.1, hold RECORD and tap A2. Repeat at 1.4.1.

    Check: A2 lights at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1; the snare lands between the main kicks.

    Why

    These are beats 2 and 4 (grid steps 5 and 13): the backbeat. Count one, TWO, three, FOUR.

    Not working?

    If the display shows a sample number instead of a position, you are still in SOUND: press MAIN. Use group A for both drums. See help: “The snare is in the wrong place”.

    Official guide: Get started

  4. Place the eight hats

    In stopped MAIN with group A, hold RECORD + A3 at 1.1.1, 1.1.3, 1.2.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.1, 1.3.3, 1.4.1 and 1.4.3.

    Check: A3 lights at all eight positions, and the hats tick steadily over kick and snare.

    Why

    These are the .1 and .3 of every beat: eighth notes, counted “1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and” (grid steps 1, 3, 5 … 15). The other eight steps stay empty.

    Not working?

    Some positions already hold a kick or snare: adding a hat there too is correct. Keep TIMING at 16 so every position exists. The placement helper below lists each one.

    Official guide: Play and record

  5. Listen to your finished drum beat

    Press PLAY on the K.O. II and listen to a few loops. Press PLAY again to stop before editing.

    Check: All three rows match the target rhythm above.

    “Hear this recipe” gives a rough reference. Kick at 1.1.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.1 and 1.4.3; snare at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1; hats on every .1 and .3. That is a complete drum beat.

    Why

    The web sounds are synthesised examples, not your factory samples. Compare the rhythm, not the exact sound.

    Not working?

    Taps that do not repeat were not written: see help “My taps do not repeat”. Extra notes? Press SHIFT + B (undo) while the umbrella is lit, or see help “The beat has extra hits”.

    Official guide: Play and record

Part 3 · Balance and keep the drums

  1. Tap the drums in SOUND

    Press SOUND and tap A1, A2 and A3 in turn.

  2. Tweak the balance, one sound at a time

    Keep the kick solid, turn the snare’s X down a little if it is harsh, and keep the hats lowest.

    Check: Kick and snare are easy to pick out; the hats are supporting detail.

    Why

    This is basic mixing: choosing how loud each part is compared with the others. Keep the original pitch while getting the rhythm right.

    Not working?

    In MAIN, X is input gain, not pad volume: always set pad volume in SOUND. Press MAIN when finished.

    Official guide: Modes

  3. Write down DRUMS

    Stop playback. Hold MAIN and read the scene number on the display; write it down as DRUMS.

    Why

    Commit saves the current patterns as a scene and creates a new scene, so you can add variations without overwriting the previous section. It is not an audio export or a backup file.

  4. Commit: make a copy

    Release MAIN, then press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN again.

    Check: The number has changed. You are now in a copy.

    Why

    The new number differs from DRUMS, and PLAY still gives your drum beat: the copy starts identical.

    Not working?

    Lost track of scenes? See help: “Which scene am I in?”.

    Official guide: Modes

Part 4 · Add a bass

  1. B: select pad 1 in SOUND

    Tap B (a short tap selects the group). Press SOUND and tap pad 1 (B1).

  2. Choose one bass sound

    Hold SOUND, type 4, 0, 0 and release, or browse 400–499 with − / + for a short, low bass.

    Check: B1 plays one low bass note, separate from the group A drums.

    Turn X down if it overwhelms the kick.

    Why

    Use one pitch only for this first attempt. KEYS (one sample at many pitches) is not needed.

    Not working?

    Custom libraries differ. Choose a bass, not a long melodic loop. If it clashes with the drums, try another bass sample. See help: “A pad is silent”.

    Official guide: Modes

  3. Place two bass pulses

    Press MAIN and make sure playback is stopped, with group B selected. Hold RECORD + B1 at 1.1.1 and at 1.3.1 only.

    Check: B1 lights at 1.1.1 and 1.3.1, and the bass row in the target rhythm matches.

    Why

    The bass reinforces beats 1 and 3 (grid steps 1 and 9). The same sample plays at its normal pitch both times.

    Not working?

    If the bass rings over the next note, shorten it in the next step. Do not add a melody until this simple layer works.

    Official guide: Play and record

  4. B1: open the trim page

    Never hold SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page unless you mean to crop: that overwrites the sample file and cannot be undone.

    Tap B1, then press SHIFT + SOUND and use − / + to reach the trim page.

  5. Give the bass a shorter tail if needed

    Leave X (start) alone; turn Y (length) down gently, tapping B1 between changes. Press MAIN when done.

    Check: The bass pulses sound separate and cleaner.

    Why

    A long bass tail can muddy a small loop. Trim changes how the sound plays; the stored sample file stays unchanged unless you crop it.

    Official guide: Modes

Part 5 · Perform your loop

  1. Optional: choose reverb for A

    Press PLAY on the K.O. II. Tap A, then press FX and choose reverb with − / +.

  2. Optional: a little room, not a wash

    Raise the fader only a little: in FX it sets group A’s send. X sets the reverb length; start short.

    Check: A subtle tail appears behind the drums while their attack stays clear.

    Why

    There is one shared effect; each group chooses how much to send to it. A small amount adds space without blurring the rhythm.

    Not working?

    Keep the bass send low. Do not hold FX while pressing a group button: that solos the group. Do not hold RECORD while moving the fader unless you want to record automation.

    Official guide: Effects

  3. Optional: make scene changes wait for the bar

    Press SHIFT + ERASE (system settings), type 4, 1, 1, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Why

    By default a new scene starts the moment you pick it. With setting 411, scene changes wait for the end of the bar, so in the performance steps that follow you can pick the next scene a little early.

    Not working?

    Type 410 the same way to switch back. See help: “Scene changes land at the wrong moment”.

    Official guide: System

  4. Write down BEAT

    Stop. Hold MAIN, read this scene’s number and write it down as BEAT (drums + bass).

  5. Keep BEAT safe, then start DRUMS

    Release, then press SHIFT + MAIN once so BEAT stays safe. Now hold MAIN, use − / + to select DRUMS, release and press PLAY.

  6. Turn the loop into a tiny performance

    Let it loop four times, switch to BEAT (hold MAIN, − / +) for eight loops, then back to DRUMS for four. Press PLAY to end.

    Check: You hear drums only, then drums + bass, then drums only again.

    Why

    A hand-made intro, main section and outro. You count the loops yourself; Chapter 2 teaches song mode, which plays scenes in order automatically.

    Not working?

    By default a new scene starts the moment you pick it. To make scene changes wait for the end of the bar, do the optional scene-change step first. See help: “Scene changes land at the wrong moment”.

    Official guide: Modes

Part 6 · Record live and wrap up

  1. Optional: make a practice copy

    Hold MAIN, select BEAT and release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once to make a practice copy; BEAT stays untouched.

    Why

    So far you placed every note by hand. Live recording is the faster way: you play pads while the loop runs. Practising first costs nothing.

  2. Optional: play along before recording

    Tap A. Press PLAY and, without touching RECORD, tap pad 2 (snare) once near the end of each loop, just before it starts again.

    Check: You hear your extra snare while you tap it; it disappears when you stop tapping.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Not working?

    If your taps keep repeating on their own, RECORD was on: stop and press SHIFT + B (undo) while the umbrella is lit.

    Official guide: Play and record

  3. Optional: arm recording with a count-in

    Stopped, in the practice copy with group A selected: tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY.

  4. Optional: tap the snare near each loop’s end

    After the four-beat count-in, tap A2 once near the end of each loop for two or three loops.

    Why

    Quantize (the − you chose in TIMING) snaps each tap to the nearest step, so a slightly early or late tap still lands on the grid. A metronome clicks while you record; TEMPO Y sets its volume.

    Not working?

    No count-in? Pressing RECORD and PLAY together starts without one.

  5. Optional: stop recording and listen

    Press RECORD to stop recording while the beat keeps playing, and listen.

    Check: The extra snare now repeats by itself near the end of every loop.

    Not working?

    Nothing recorded? See help: “My taps do not repeat”.

    Official guide: Play and record

  6. Optional: keep it or undo it

    Press PLAY to stop. Like the new snare? Keep it.

    Too messy? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella icon is lit.

    Check: The practice copy sounds the way you chose.

    Then press PLAY to check it is gone.

  7. Optional: or erase a single note

    To remove a single note instead, go to it in stopped MAIN and briefly press ERASE + A2.

  8. Optional: BEAT is still clean

    Hold MAIN and select BEAT.

    Check: It plays without the extra snare.

    Why

    Undo and erase are your safety net for live recording. Because you recorded in a copy, BEAT is still the clean version.

    Not working?

    If undo removes more or less than you expected, this is only a practice copy: select BEAT and make a fresh copy with SHIFT + MAIN. See help: “I erased or changed the wrong thing”.

    Official guide: Play and record

  9. Your first beat is done

    Read the six moves below, then try one from memory without reading this app.

    • Navigate: stopped MAIN, − / + (SHIFT + − / + jumps a bar).
    • Write: hold RECORD + pad.
    • Erase one note: brief ERASE + pad.
    • Undo: SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit.
    • Commit: SHIFT + MAIN.
    • Select a scene: hold MAIN + − / +.

    Check: You can build a drum beat, add bass, perform two sections and fix a mistake.

    Your scenes so far: DRUMS and BEAT.

  10. Try one move in a fresh copy

    In a fresh copy of BEAT (SHIFT + MAIN), move one kick or add one hat. Rebuild your change in the Rhythm lab and listen.

    Then mark this chapter complete.

    Not working?

    To keep your work outside the device, back it up with the official EP sample tool on a computer. This coach cannot capture hardware audio. See help: “How do I export or back up?”.

    Official guide: Get started
