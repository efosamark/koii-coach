K.O. II / COACH

CHAPTER 6 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Tech house

A full 128 BPM track

Build a complete tech-house track at 128 BPM: four-on-the-floor drums, a rolling bassline that ducks under the kick, moving percussion, a short vocal hook in your own recording, two different drops, a breakdown, a build and an ending. Song mode then plays it as 112 bars, about 3½ minutes. This is a step-by-step recipe, not a promise of a mastered release: your sound choices and your speaker still matter.

You will make: a tech-house track at 128 BPM, about 3:30 long, with two drops, a breakdown and a build: 28 song positions, 112 bars.

Before you start

Firmware: written and checked for OS 2.5.1. Song mode and sidechain need OS 2.0 or later; a few optional steps say “OS 2.5”. Older OS: update first (see the official OS notes).

You need: your K.O. II, headphones or a speaker, and for the vocal hook either your own voice (built-in microphone), a phone or computer connected with a USB-C cable (OS 2.5), or a line cable. No way to record? Load a short sound from 500–599 on C1 instead and skip the two recording steps in Part 7. Paper and a pen for scene numbers.

You should already know the moves from Chapter 1 · First beat (placing notes with RECORD, a brief erase, undo, commit) and Chapter 2 · Make it a song (the song list). Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample and Chapter 4 · Play & perform explain sampling and live playing in more depth; this chapter only uses what it needs.

Safety: stop playback before every position-by-position edit. A brief ERASE + pad removes the one note at the current step. Holding ERASE + pad until TRK blinks clears every note of that pad in this pattern: this chapter uses both, on purpose. SHIFT + B undoes while the umbrella icon is lit.
Never use ERASE + SOUND here: it deletes the sample from the device.

Your destination

Tempo
128 BPM, 4/4, 16 grid
Main loop
4 bars / 64 steps
Roles
A drums · B bass · C vocal hook and synth stab · D optional riser
Song
28 song positions / 112 bars / about 3:30

The secret is not maximum sounds all the time. Kick and bass repeat to make people dance. Changes happen at the ends of bars, between sections, and around the vocal.

Part 1 · Start in an empty project and set the grid

  1. Press MAIN. If music is playing, press PLAY to stop it. Hold MAIN, hold pad 9 as well, then release both. The official guide says “hold main and one of the pads (1-9) to select an empty project”, and on a new unit projects 6–9 are empty. This course keeps project 6 for Chapters 1, 2 and 4 (First beat, Make it a song, Play & perform), 7 for Chapter 3 (Sample, chop & resample) and 8 for Chapter 5 (Full hip-hop beat). Project 9 was your scratch project in Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II and now becomes this track. If 9 already holds work you want to keep, back it up first with the EP sample tool (Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II, Part 4) and see help “My project is not empty” in Troubleshooting. Write the project number on your paper.

  2. Press PLAY: you should hear nothing. Press PLAY again to stop. Tap A, then tap a few pads: in an empty project the pads have no sounds yet. Pads 1, 2 and 3 may still play the kick, snare and hat you picked in the tour in Chapter 0; that is fine, because Part 2 puts new sounds on them. If music plays, you are not in an empty project. On OS 2.0+ MAIN + a number can also select a scene, and the guide does not say how the device tells the two apart, so check your display and try step 1 again.

  3. Press TEMPO. Turn X until the display shows 128, or hold TEMPO and type 1, 2, 8 on the pads. Press MAIN.

    Check: the display shows a position such as 1.1.1 again.

  4. Press TIMING. Turn X until the display shows 16 (not 1 and not 16T): 16 steps per bar. Press − to choose quantize (+ would be free time). Leave Y (swing) at no swing. Press MAIN.

Checkpoint: you are in an empty project at 128 BPM with a 16-step grid, and PLAY is silent.

If it doesn’t: sound on PLAY means a project with music: repeat step 1, holding MAIN first and then pad 9. Tempo will not reach 128? Hold TEMPO and type it.

Part 2 · Choose every sound before writing notes

  1. Press SOUND, tap A, then tap pad 1. Hold SOUND, type 1 on the pads and release: that loads sound 001. Tap A1 to hear it. The guide says factory kicks are stored at 1–99: use − / + to browse until you find a short, punchy kick, solid but without a long boom.

  2. Still in SOUND with group A, tap pad 2. Hold SOUND, type 1, 0, 0 and browse with − / + for a crisp, short clap or snare (snares are stored at 100–199; the guide does not list claps separately, so if you want a clap and find none there, try the percussion range 300–399). Then tap pad 3, hold SOUND, type 2, 0, 0 and choose a short closed hat (hi-hats: 200–299). Turn X down on A3 so it is quieter than the kick and clap.

  3. Load four more short sounds in group A the same way: A4 a short open hat (200–299); A5 a dry rim or woodblock click (try 300–399, percussion); A6 a soft shaker (300–399); A7 a short tom or percussion hit (300–399). SHIFT + − / + jumps ten sound numbers at a time. Do not chase perfection: usable and short beats perfect.

    Check: tapping A1 to A7 gives seven different short sounds.

  4. Tap B, then pad 1. Hold SOUND, type 4, 0, 0 and browse 400–499 (bass) for a clean, short bass. Turn X down so it is quieter than the kick. Now copy it to B2: with B1 selected press SHIFT + C once, tap pad 2, then press SHIFT + D. Tap B2: it plays the same bass. B2 becomes the answer note at the end of bar 4. Press MAIN.

Checkpoint: A1–A7 are kick, clap, closed hat, open hat, rim, shaker and tom; B1 and B2 are the same short bass.

If it doesn’t: a silent pad may be an empty sound slot: press SOUND, tap the pad and press + to move on. Swap any sound you dislike now. Pad sounds belong to the project, so changing one later changes it in every scene.

Official guide: sound mode and sound numbers · copy sounds

Part 3 · Write the four-on-the-floor foundation

  1. Press MAIN and make sure playback is stopped. Tap A. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap A1, release. Use + to reach 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1, writing A1 at each.

    Check: A1 lights at all four positions. This is “four on the floor”.

  2. Go to 1.2.1, hold RECORD and tap A2; repeat at 1.4.1. The clap sits on beats 2 and 4, on top of the kick.

    Check: at 1.2.1 both A1 and A2 light.

  3. Write A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3: the off-beat closed hats between the kicks. Press PLAY for a few loops, then stop. The kick should feel steady and the hats smaller than the clap.

  4. Write A6 (shaker) at 1.1.4 and 1.3.4 only. The next step copies bar 1 into every bar, so two notes here become eight.

  5. Stopped, with A selected: tap RECORD to arm it, hold SHIFT and press + once. The pattern doubles to two bars and its notes are copied. Hold SHIFT and press + once more for four bars. Tap RECORD again to leave record arm.

    Check: press PLAY and the same one-bar beat repeats four times before the loop restarts. Stop.

Checkpoint: a four-bar loop with the kick on every beat, the clap on 2 and 4, a closed hat between the kicks and a quiet shaker tick.

If it doesn’t: a missing hit was never written (tapping a pad without RECORD only plays it): go to its position and write it. Bars 2–4 silent? + was pressed without SHIFT, which adds empty bars: press SHIFT + B (undo) while the umbrella is lit, then double again with SHIFT held.

Official guide: step sequence · pattern length and duplicate

Part 4 · Make the percussion move across four bars

  1. Stopped, with A selected, write A3 at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4. Shortcut: hold SHIFT and press + to jump a whole bar, or − to jump back. Only do this with record arm off (armed, SHIFT + + doubles the pattern) and without holding a pad (SHIFT + pad + − / + nudges that note).

  2. Write A4 (open hat) at 2.4.3 and 4.4.3. At both positions also briefly press ERASE + A3, so the open hat replaces the closed hat instead of stacking on it.

    Check: at 2.4.3 A4 lights and A3 does not.

  3. Write A5 (rim) at 1.2.4, 2.3.4, 3.2.4 and 4.3.3. Press SOUND, tap A5 and turn X down until it is a tiny click you feel more than hear. Press MAIN.

  4. Now play one part live. Stopped, hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY: you hear a four-beat count-in, then recording starts. Count the bars with the loop: “ONE two three four, TWO two three four, THREE…, FOUR two three four”. On that very last “four”, tap A7 twice quickly. Then press RECORD: recording stops but the beat keeps playing. Listen to one more loop and press PLAY to stop. Quantize (the − you chose in TIMING) snaps your taps to the nearest step.

  5. Press PLAY.

    The tom fill now plays by itself at the end of every four bars, turning the phrase back to bar 1. Stop.

    Too many hits or in the wrong place? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit, or, stopped, hold ERASE + A7 until TRK blinks to clear every A7 note, then record again. Prefer exact placement? Write A7 at 4.4.2 and 4.4.4 with RECORD instead.

  6. Optional, OS 2.5: push the shaker slightly late for a looser groove. Stopped, press SHIFT + TIMING to open timing correct. Check X shows 16 and leave Y alone. Hold A6 and press + three times: every shaker note moves three ticks later (one 16th step is 24 ticks). Press MAIN, play and compare. To undo, open timing correct again, hold A6 and press − three times.

Checkpoint: four bars that move: extra hat ticks in bars 2 and 4, open hats at the ends of bars 2 and 4, a quiet rim, and a tom fill that turns bar 4 back to bar 1.

If it doesn’t: cluttered? Lower the rim or shaker with X in SOUND. A closed hat still under an open hat? Briefly erase A3 at that position. A hit missing? Go to its position, stopped, and write it.

Official guide: live record with count-in · erase and undo · timing correct

Part 5 · Build the rolling bassline

  1. Press MAIN, stopped. Tap B (a short tap selects the group; holding B and pressing − / + would switch its pattern). Hold RECORD, press + once, then release both. B’s pattern is now two bars long and still empty.

    Check: the display shows the length change from 1 to 2.

  2. Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Write B1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.2, 1.3.3 and 1.4.2. These notes land after the kick, not on top of it.

  3. Hold SHIFT and press + to jump to bar 2. Write B1 at 2.1.3, 2.2.3, 2.3.2 and 2.4.3. Press PLAY: a two-bar bass phrase bounces between the kicks. Stop.

  4. Stopped, with B selected: tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + once, then tap RECORD again to leave record arm. B is now four bars long, and bars 3 and 4 repeat bars 1 and 2.

  5. Go to 4.4.3. Briefly press ERASE + B1, then hold RECORD and tap B2. Bar 4 now ends on B2, the answer before the loop restarts.

    Check: at 4.4.3 only B2 lights.

    Optional: press SOUND, tap B2 and turn Y (pitch) up a little so the answer is a different note; turn it back if it clashes.

Checkpoint: kick and bass take turns: the bass fills the gaps between kicks, and bar 4 ends with B2.

If it doesn’t: if kick and bass blur into one boom, shorten B1: press SOUND, tap B1, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND (sound edit), use − / + to reach the trim page and turn Y (length) down a little while tapping B1. Do not hold SHIFT + SOUND there: on the trim page that crops the file permanently. Still muddy? Lower B1 and B2 with X in SOUND.

Official guide: pattern length and duplicate · trim

Part 6 · Make kick and bass breathe

  1. Press PLAY and listen to at least four loops. You should pick out the kick on every beat, and the bass should bounce, with B2 answering at the end of bar 4. If not, change one thing only: the bass level (X in SOUND), its length (trim, as in the checkpoint above) or a different bass sound. Stop.

  2. Sidechain (OS 2.0+) ducks the bass each time the kick plays. Press PLAY. Press SHIFT + FX to open the output settings (master compressor), then press + once to reach sidechain. Hold the A group button and press pad 1: the kick A1 becomes the source. Hold SHIFT and press B: the bass group becomes the destination. Turn X (length of the duck) to short and Y (shape) to about the middle. Stop adjusting when the bass dips under each kick and recovers before its next note. Press MAIN, then PLAY to stop.

  3. Press PLAY and stand up for 20 seconds. If your body wants to move, continue. If it feels stiff, do not add layers: lower one loud percussion sound or shorten B1, then listen again. Stop.

  4. Stopped. Hold MAIN and read the scene number; write it as GROOVE (drums and bass). This finished section stays as it is. Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN again: the number has changed. This new scene is your working copy; write its number as DROP A. Release MAIN. PLAY still sounds exactly like GROOVE.

Checkpoint: GROOVE is saved and you are in DROP A’s working copy, which still sounds identical.

If it doesn’t: no audible duck? Make X longer; pumping too hard? Make it shorter. If the scene number did not change, press SHIFT + MAIN once more while stopped. Lost track? Hold MAIN and step through the scenes with − / +, playing each briefly.

Official guide: sidechain · commit

Part 7 · Record the vocal hook and add the synth answer

  1. Tap C, then press SAMPLE: the pads blink. Use − / + to choose the source: the built-in mic for your own voice; usb (OS 2.5) for sound from a phone or computer connected with a USB-C cable, with K.O. II chosen as its audio output; or line in (in) for a cable. Make a test sound and turn X (input level) up until it is strong but not distorted. Keep Y (threshold) low so recording starts as soon as you do.

  2. Hold C1, sing or say two to four words, then release. Keep it under about 1.5 seconds (one bar at 128 BPM lasts about 1.9 seconds). Tap C1: you hear your words. Not happy? Press SAMPLE again and record C1 again. Need both hands, for example to start audio on your phone? Instead of holding C1, press SHIFT + pad 1 for hands-free sampling (OS 2.0+), then press SAMPLE to stop. Press MAIN when done. Using a line from someone else’s record? Fine for practice; for public release of music containing someone else’s record, check and obtain the necessary permissions.

  3. Do not hold SHIFT + SOUND here: on the trim page that crops the file permanently.

    Press SOUND, tap C, then tap pad 1 to select C1. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit, then use − / + to reach the trim page. Turn X (start) a little at a time, tapping C1, until playback begins right at the first word. Turn Y (length) until it ends just after the last word.

  4. Still in sound edit, use − / + to find the page where X sets the play mode, and turn X to oneshot (the other choices are key and legato). In oneshot one trigger plays the whole trimmed phrase. Press SOUND, then turn X (level) until the words are clear but not louder than the clap. Press MAIN.

  5. Stopped in MAIN, tap C. Press SHIFT + A once: this gives the selected group, C, a fresh empty pattern in this scene, so GROOVE keeps its own. Hold RECORD and press + three times, then release both: C is now four bars long and still empty.

    Check: PLAY still gives drums and bass, and nothing from C yet.

  6. Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap C1 once, release. Press PLAY: the hook plays once at the start of every four-bar loop. Stop.

  7. Stopped. Hold MAIN and check the number is DROP A’s: this finished section (drums, bass and hook) stays as it is. Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN again: the new number is your working copy; write it as DROP B.

  8. In DROP B, press SOUND, tap C and tap pad 2. Hold SOUND, type 5, 0, 0 and browse 500–599 (melodic sounds) for a short synth or pluck stab. Turn X down so it is quiet. Press MAIN, stopped, and write C2 at 2.3.3 and 4.3.3 (hold SHIFT and press + to jump a bar). It answers the voice; it is not a full melody.

  9. Press PLAY and listen. If C1 fights the clap or hats, lower the hats (A3) first, then C1. If C2 clashes with the bass, try another sound or turn its Y (pitch) a little in SOUND. Do not fix a clash by adding another sound. Stop. DROP B is finished.

Checkpoint: hold MAIN and play GROOVE, DROP A and DROP B in turn: no vocal; the hook; the hook plus the synth answer.

If it doesn’t: if GROOVE has the hook, select GROOVE, tap C, go to 1.1.1 and briefly press ERASE + C1. If DROP A has the stab, briefly erase C2 there at 2.3.3 and 4.3.3. Words cut short? Set C1’s play mode to oneshot. Recording silent? Check the source and the X input level.

Official guide: sampling and hands-free sampling · sample over USB audio · sound edit, trim and play mode

Part 8 · Make the other sections

  1. Every new section starts as a copy of a finished one. Stopped, hold MAIN and use − / + to select GROOVE, then release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as OUTRO. Tap A. Hold ERASE + A4 until TRK blinks, then release: every open hat in this pattern is gone. Do the same with A5 (rim) and A7 (tom).

    Cleared the wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives kick, clap, closed hats, shaker and bass. Stop.

  2. OUTRO is finished. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as INTRO. Tap B. Hold ERASE + B1 until TRK blinks, then do the same with B2.

    Cleared the wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives drums only, no bass. Stop.

  3. Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP A, then release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as BUILD. Tap B and clear B1 and B2 (hold ERASE + pad until TRK blinks). Tap A and clear A3 (closed hats) and A4 (open hats) the same way.

    Cleared the wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives kick, clap, rim, shaker, tom fill and the hook, with no bass and no hats. Stop.

  4. Still in BUILD, stopped, with A selected: write A2 (clap) at 4.3.1, 4.3.3 and 4.4.3. With the claps already at 4.2.1 and 4.4.1, bar 4 now rolls into the next section. Press PLAY to hear it, then stop.

  5. BUILD is finished. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as BREAK. Tap A and clear A1 (kick), A2 (clap) and A7 (tom) with the same TRK hold.

    Cleared the wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives just the rim, shaker and your hook. It should feel empty: that is what makes the next drop hit. Stop.

  6. Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP B, then release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as PEAK. Tap A, go to 4.4.1 and briefly press ERASE + A1.

    Check: PLAY sounds like DROP B with one kick missing at the very end, a tiny breath before the loop restarts. Stop.

Checkpoint: eight named scenes: GROOVE, DROP A, DROP B, OUTRO, INTRO, BUILD, BREAK and PEAK, each matching the scene table below.

If it doesn’t: TRK never blinks? Playback is running: stop first, and keep holding until it blinks (a brief press only removes the note at the current step). A finished scene changed too? You erased before committing: press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit, or write the missing notes back. A working copy is a mess? Select its finished source scene and commit a fresh copy; unused scenes do no harm unless you put them in the song.

Official guide: erase and undo · select scene and commit

Part 9 · Add space and a riser

  1. Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP B, release, and press PLAY. Tap C, then press FX and use − / + to choose reverb. Raise the fader a little: in FX it sets group C’s send. Turn X down for a short reverb. Keep the bass dry: do not raise B’s send. Do not hold RECORD while moving the fader, or you record fader movement. If the words blur, lower the fader before changing anything else. Press MAIN, then PLAY to stop.

  2. Optional, OS 2.5 reverse: press SOUND, tap C, tap pad 1 and press SHIFT + C once to copy C1’s sound. Tap D, tap pad 1 and press SHIFT + D to paste it: D1 now plays your hook. With D1 selected, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, press + until the display shows tim, and turn X until it shows rev. Tap D1: your hook plays backwards. C1 is unchanged. Press MAIN.

  3. Optional: hold MAIN, use − / + to select BUILD and release. Stopped, tap D and press SHIFT + A once (a fresh empty pattern for D in this scene only). Hold RECORD and press + three times for four bars. Go to 4.1.1 (hold SHIFT and press + to jump bars), hold RECORD and tap D1.

    Press PLAY: the reversed hook swells through bar 4 into the next section.

    If it runs past the end of bar 4, briefly erase it with ERASE + D1 and place it at 4.2.1 or 4.3.1 instead. Stop.

  4. Listen to INTRO, GROOVE, DROP A and DROP B in that order (hold MAIN and use − / + between them). Something new should arrive each time: bass and percussion, then the hook, then the synth answer. If two scenes sound the same, check that section’s clears against the scene table below.

Checkpoint: the hook has a little space without blurring the kick and bass, and each section adds something new.

If it doesn’t: everything washed out? Lower C’s send. The riser hides the drop? Place it later or leave it out. The reversed D1 cuts off? Set its play mode to oneshot, as for C1.

Official guide: effects and reverb · time page and reverse · OS 2.5 reverse

Part 10 · Build the 112-bar song list

  1. A song position lasts as long as its scene’s longest pattern, so every scene must loop every four bars. Spot-check INTRO, BREAK and PEAK: play each and count; it should restart after four bars (16 beats). Groups you never lengthened stay one bar and simply repeat underneath; that is fine.

  2. Stopped, hold MAIN and press ENTER to open the song list editor. In this fresh project the list starts with one position. Hold SHIFT and press − / + to choose INTRO’s scene number for position 1.

  3. Hold SHIFT and press A to add a position, then, still holding SHIFT, use − / + to choose its scene. Add while you are on the last position. Repeat for positions 2 INTRO; 3–4 GROOVE; 5–8 DROP A; 9–12 DROP B.

    Check: release SHIFT and step through with − / +: exactly 12 positions so far.

  4. Add the rest the same way: 13–14 BREAK; 15–16 BUILD; 17–20 DROP A; 21–24 DROP B; 25–26 PEAK; 27–28 OUTRO. Scene numbers and song-position numbers are different things: check each one against your paper. Extra position? Select it and press SHIFT + C to cut it.

  5. Press SHIFT + PLAY to play the song from the start. 28 positions × 4 bars = 112 bars, about 3 minutes 30 seconds at 128 BPM, and the song stops by itself at the end. Ends much too soon? A position is missing. Drags on? A pattern is longer than four bars. Keeps looping? Song loop is on: with the unit on, press SHIFT + ERASE (system settings), type 4, 2, 0, press ENTER, then MAIN.

Part 11 · Speaker mix check

  1. Play the whole song without touching anything. Write down only three things you dislike. Do not try to repair every detail at once.

  2. First fix, levels: in SOUND, use X so the kick A1 is your loudest drum, the clap A2 slightly below it, hats and percussion lower, the bass B1/B2 under the kick, and the vocal C1 clearly audible but not shouting. Pad levels apply in every scene of this project. Check at low volume too.

  3. Second fix, low end: if it is muddy, shorten B1’s tail first (SOUND, tap B1, briefly SHIFT + SOUND, − / + to trim, Y down; never hold SHIFT + SOUND there), or make the sidechain duck a little longer (SHIFT + FX, +, then X). If it feels empty, raise the shaker or rim slightly rather than adding a constant melody. If it feels crowded, lower the rim (A5) or shaker (A6): one level change fixes every scene.

  4. Third fix: test on the speaker you will actually use, at a sensible volume. Keep the K.O. II’s output level conservative: a distorted sound does not get better by turning it up.

Checkpoint: at low and normal volume the kick leads, the bass sits under it and the words are clear.

If it doesn’t: change one level at a time and listen to the whole song again before the next change.

Official guide: sound level and pitch · output and master compressor

Part 12 · Record, save and share

  1. In the song list, use − / + to select position 9 (DROP B) and press PLAY to play from there through BREAK and BUILD into DROP A. This is your main payoff. The break should create anticipation; the returning kick, bass and hook should feel bigger without being louder. Stop.

  2. Record one full pass before experimenting again. With OS 2.5, connect the K.O. II to a phone or computer with a USB-C cable, choose K.O. II as the input in a recording app, start recording, then press SHIFT + PLAY in the song list. Or record the headphone output with a recorder or audio interface. Optional: hold FX and press pads at the transitions for punch-in effects (more in Chapter 4 · Play & perform). Commit makes scene copies; it is not an audio export.

  3. Write down the project number and the scene numbers of GROOVE, DROP A, DROP B, OUTRO, INTRO, BUILD, BREAK and PEAK. Keep a copy of your samples on a computer with the official EP sample tool. Your ticks on this page are saved only in this browser. For public release of music containing someone else’s record, check and obtain the necessary permissions; do not assume sampling automatically grants rights.

  4. Optional: play the sections by hand instead of song mode. With the unit already switched on, press SHIFT + ERASE (system settings), type 4, 1, 2, press ENTER, then press MAIN: scene changes now wait for the end of the pattern, so you can pick the next scene early with hold MAIN + − / +. Type 410 the same way to switch back. Never hold SHIFT + ERASE while switching the unit on: that formats the drive.

  5. You are done when the whole track plays, kick and bass stay clear, the vocal appears only where it matters, and the two drops feel different. Resist adding sounds at the last minute: try new ideas in a fresh copy (SHIFT + MAIN) so the finished scenes stay safe.

Checkpoint: you have a recording of the full track and a written list of scenes.

If it doesn’t: recording silent? Check that K.O. II is chosen as the input in your recording app, or record from the headphone output instead.

You want to…Do this
Move one stepStopped MAIN → − / +
Jump a whole barSHIFT + − / + (record arm off; not while holding a pad)
Write an exact noteStopped MAIN → go to the position → hold RECORD + tap pad
Remove one wrong noteStopped MAIN → go to it → brief ERASE + that pad

UndoSHIFT + B while the umbrella icon is lit
Longer pattern (empty bars)Hold RECORD and press + (− makes it shorter)
Double a pattern with its notesStopped → tap RECORD → hold SHIFT and press + → tap RECORD
Live record with count-inStopped → tap RECORD → PLAY. RECORD stops recording; PLAY stops playback
Choose a soundSOUND → group → pad → − / + (SHIFT + − / + jumps ten), or hold SOUND and type the number. X level, Y pitch
Copy a sound to another padSOUND → select pad → SHIFT + C once → select target pad → SHIFT + D
Copy one pad’s notes to another pad (OS 2.5)Hold the pad + SHIFT + C → hold the target pad + SHIFT + D
Copy and paste a barMAIN → SHIFT + C once (twice copies the whole pattern) → go to the target bar → SHIFT + D
Trim a sampleSelect pad → brief SHIFT + SOUND → − / + to trim → X start, Y length
Copy a scene (commit)SHIFT + MAIN. You move into the new copy; the scene you left is the finished one
Select a sceneHold MAIN + − / +. The guide also documents typing a scene number while holding MAIN (OS 2.0+), but MAIN + pad 1–9 is documented for changing project too, so check the display
Clear the current sceneHold ERASE + MAIN for 2 seconds: CLR, or DEL if it is empty and not in the song. Check the scene number first
Song listMAIN + ENTER → SHIFT + A adds a position → SHIFT + − / + picks its scene → SHIFT + C cuts → SHIFT + PLAY plays from the start

Scene changes at pattern endSHIFT + ERASE → type 412 → ENTER → MAIN (410 = change immediately)

Safety: ERASE + SOUND permanently deletes the selected sample. Holding SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page permanently crops it. SHIFT + ERASE with the unit already on only opens system settings, but holding SHIFT + ERASE while switching the unit on formats the drive: the official guide warns this “will remove all your work as well as all factory sounds. once removed factory sounds can not be recovered!” It is not a casual reset; copy anything you want to keep with the official EP sample tool first.
