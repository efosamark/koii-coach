K.O. II / COACH

CHAPTER 6 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Tech house

A full 128 BPM track

Build a tech-house track at 128 BPM with two drops and a breakdown.

A kick on every beat, a rolling bass that dips under it, and a vocal hook you record yourself. Two drops, a break and a build turn it into a full track.

You will make: a 3½-minute, 112-bar tech-house track.

Before you start

  • Empty project 9, OS 2.0 or newer
  • Headphones or a speaker
  • Your voice, or a phone and a cable
  • Paper and a pen

What you need

Firmware: written for OS 2.5.1. Song mode and sidechain need OS 2.0 or newer, and a few optional steps need OS 2.5. On an older OS, update first: see the OS notes.

For the vocal hook: your voice and the built-in mic, a phone or computer on a USB-C cable, or a line cable. No way to record? Load a short sound from 500–599 on C1 and skip the two recording steps in Part 7.

You know: writing notes, erase, undo and commit from Chapter 1 · First beat, and the song list from Chapter 2 · Make it a song. Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample and Chapter 4 · Play & perform go deeper into sampling and live playing.

  • Stop playback before you edit notes.
  • Never use ERASE + SOUND: it deletes a sample.
Safety: stop playback before editing notes. A brief ERASE + pad removes the one note at the current step. Holding ERASE + pad until TRK blinks clears every note of that pad in this pattern. This chapter uses both. SHIFT + B undoes while the umbrella icon is lit.
Never use ERASE + SOUND here: it deletes the sample from the device.

Your destination

Tempo
128 BPM, 4/4, 16 grid
Main loop
4 bars / 64 steps
Roles
A drums · B bass · C vocal hook and synth stab · D optional riser
Song
28 song positions / 112 bars / about 3:30

Less is more: kick and bass repeat to keep people dancing. Changes come at the ends of bars, between sections and around the vocal.

Your pad plan

Part 1 · Start in an empty project and set the grid

Goal: open empty project 9 at 128 BPM, with a 16-step grid and no swing.

  1. Open empty project 9

    Project 9 holds work you want to keep? Back it up first with the EP sample tool, as in Chapter 0, Part 4.

    Press MAIN. If music is playing, press PLAY to stop it. Hold MAIN, hold pad 9 as well, then release both.

    Check: While both are held, the display shows P 9.

    Write the project number on your paper.

    Why

    Chapters 1–5 use projects 6, 7 and 8. Project 9, your scratch project from Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II, becomes this track.

    Not working?

    Not sure what 9 holds? See help “My project is not empty”.

  2. Check that project 9 is empty

    Press PLAY. Press PLAY again to stop. Tap A, then tap a few pads.

    Check: You hear nothing.

    Not working?

    Music plays? You are not in an empty project: a quick MAIN + number press selects a scene instead. Open project 9 again, keeping pad 9 held until the display shows P 9. A1–A3 play the kick, snare and hat from Chapter 0? That is fine: Part 2 replaces them.

  3. Set the tempo to 128 BPM

    Press TEMPO. Turn X until the display shows 128, or hold TEMPO and type 1, 2, 8 on the pads. Press MAIN.

    Check: The display shows a position such as 1.1.1 again.

  4. Set the grid to 16

    Press TIMING. Turn X until the display shows 16, not 1 or 16T.

    Check: The display shows 16.

  5. Choose quantize, no swing

    Press − for quantize, not + for free time. Leave Y (swing) at no swing. Press MAIN.

    Check: The display shows a position such as 1.1.1 again.

Checkpoint: you are in an empty project at 128 BPM with a 16-step grid, and PLAY is silent.

If it doesn’t: music on PLAY? Open project 9 again, holding MAIN first and then pad 9. Tempo will not reach 128? Hold TEMPO and type it.

Part 2 · Choose every sound before writing notes

Goal: load seven drum sounds on A1–A7 and one short bass on B1 and B2.

  1. A1: load sound 001

    Press SOUND, tap A, then tap A1. Hold SOUND, type 1 on the pads and release: that loads sound 001. Tap A1 to hear it.

    Check: A1 plays a kick, sound 001.

  2. Browse for a punchy kick

    Use − / + to browse until you find a short, punchy kick, solid but without a long boom.

    Check: A1 plays a short, punchy kick.

    Why

    Factory kicks sit at 1–99.

  3. A2: choose a clap or snare

    In SOUND with group A, tap A2. Hold SOUND, type 1, 0, 0 and browse with − / + for a crisp, short clap or snare.

    Check: A2 plays a crisp, short clap or snare.

    Snares sit at 100–199, claps at 300–316.

  4. A3: choose a closed hat

    Tap A3, hold SOUND, type 2, 0, 0 and choose a short closed hat from 200–299.

    Check: A3 plays a short closed hat.

  5. Turn the hat down

    Turn X down on A3 so it is quieter than the kick and clap.

    Check: A3 sounds quieter than the kick and clap.

  6. A4: an open hat

    In group A, load a short open hat on A4 the same way, from 200–299.

    Check: A4 plays a short open hat.

    SHIFT + − / + jumps ten sound numbers at a time.

  7. A5 and A6: a rim and a shaker

    Load A5 and A6 the same way: a dry rim or woodblock click on A5, a soft shaker on A6, both from 300–399.

    Check: A5 clicks and A6 plays a soft shaker.

  8. A7: a tom

    Load A7 the same way: a short tom or percussion hit from 300–399.

    Check: Tapping A1 to A7 gives seven different short sounds.

    Short and usable beats perfect.

  9. B1: choose a bass

    Tap B, then B1. Hold SOUND, type 4, 0, 0 and browse the basses at 400–499 for a clean, short one.

    Check: B1 plays a clean, short bass.

  10. Turn the bass down

    Turn X down so it is quieter than the kick.

    Check: B1 sounds quieter than the kick.

  11. Copy B1 onto B2

    Copy it to B2: with B1 selected, press SHIFT + C once, tap B2 and press SHIFT + D. Tap B2, then press MAIN.

    Check: B2 plays the same bass as B1.

    Why

    B2 becomes the answer note at the end of bar 4.

Checkpoint: A1–A7 play kick, clap, closed hat, open hat, rim, shaker and tom, and B1 and B2 the same short bass.

If it doesn’t: a silent pad may be an empty slot: in SOUND, tap the pad and press +. Swap any sound you dislike now: a change later reaches every scene.

Part 3 · Write the four-on-the-floor foundation

Goal: write a kick on every beat, with claps and hats, in group A, then double it to four bars.

  1. Go to 1.1.1 with group A

    Press MAIN and check that playback is stopped. Tap A. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1.

    Check: The display shows 1.1.1.

  2. A1: four kicks

    Hold RECORD, tap A1, release. Use + to reach 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1, writing A1 at each.

    Check: A1 lights at all four positions.

    This is “four on the floor”.

  3. A2: claps on 2 and 4

    Go to 1.2.1, hold RECORD and tap A2. Repeat at 1.4.1.

    Check: At 1.2.1 both A1 and A2 light.

    Why

    The clap sits on beats 2 and 4, on top of the kick.

  4. A3: off-beat hats

    Write A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3. Press PLAY for a few loops, then stop.

    Check: The kick feels steady and the hats sound smaller than the clap.

  5. A6: a shaker in bar 1

    Write A6 (shaker) at 1.1.4 and 1.3.4 only.

    Check: A6 lights at 1.1.4 and 1.3.4.

    Why

    The next step copies bar 1 into every bar, so two notes here become eight.

  6. Double A to two bars

    Stopped, with A selected: tap RECORD to arm it, hold SHIFT and press + once.

    Check: The display shows Ln.2: bar 2 plays bar 1’s notes.

  7. Double again to four bars

    Hold SHIFT and press + once more for four bars. Tap RECORD again to leave record arm.

    Check: With PLAY, the one-bar beat repeats four times before the loop restarts.

    Stop.

Checkpoint: a four-bar loop with the kick on every beat, the clap on 2 and 4, a closed hat between the kicks and a quiet shaker tick.

If it doesn’t: a hit missing? Go to its position and write it with RECORD held. Bars 2–4 silent? Press SHIFT + B (undo) while the umbrella is lit, then double again with SHIFT held.

Part 4 · Make the percussion move across four bars

Goal: make the four bars move with hat accents, open hats, a quiet rim and a tom fill you record live.

  1. A3: two hat accents

    Stopped, with A selected, write A3 at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4. Hold SHIFT and press + to jump a bar, or − to jump back.

    Check: A3 lights at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4.

    Jump bars only with RECORD unarmed and no pad held. Otherwise SHIFT + + doubles the pattern or nudges a note.

  2. A4: open hats instead of closed

    Press ERASE + A3 briefly: a long hold clears every closed hat.

    Removes only those two A3 notes.

    Write A4 (open hat) at 2.4.3 and 4.4.3. At both, also briefly press ERASE + A3, so the open hat replaces the closed hat.

    Wrong note? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: At 2.4.3 and 4.4.3 A4 lights and A3 does not.

  3. A5: rim clicks

    Write A5 (rim) at 1.2.4, 2.3.4, 3.2.4 and 4.3.3.

    Check: A5 lights at all four positions.

  4. Turn the rim down

    Press SOUND, tap A5 and turn X down until it is a tiny click you feel more than hear. Press MAIN.

    Check: Tapping A5 gives a tiny, quiet click.

  5. Arm recording with a count-in

    Stopped, hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY.

    Check: You hear a four-beat count-in, then recording starts.

  6. A7: tap twice on the last “four”

    Count the bars with the loop. On the last “four” of bar 4, tap A7 twice quickly. Then press RECORD.

    Check: Recording stops but the beat keeps playing.

    “ONE two three four, TWO two three four, THREE…, FOUR two three four”.

  7. Listen, then stop

    Listen to one more loop and press PLAY to stop.

    Check: Your tom hits play back at the end of bar 4.

    Why

    Quantize, set in Part 1, snaps your taps to the nearest step.

  8. Check the tom fill

    Press PLAY.

    Check: The tom fill now plays by itself at the end of every four bars.

    Stop.

    Why

    It turns the phrase back to bar 1.

    Not working?

    Too many hits, or in the wrong place? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit. Or, stopped, hold ERASE + A7 until TRK blinks to clear every A7 note, then record again. Or write A7 at 4.4.2 and 4.4.4 with RECORD held.

  9. Optional: open timing correct

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Stopped, press SHIFT + TIMING to open timing correct. Check X shows 16 and leave Y alone.

    Check: The display shows 16 for X.

    Why

    A shaker slightly late loosens the groove.

  10. Optional: push the shaker later

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Hold A6 and press + three times. Press MAIN, play and compare.

    Check: The shaker now lands a touch later, for a looser groove.

    To undo, open timing correct again, hold A6 and press − three times.

    Why

    Every shaker note moves three ticks later. One step is 24 ticks.

Checkpoint: the four bars move, and a tom fill leads bar 4 back to bar 1.

If it doesn’t: cluttered? Lower the rim or shaker with X in SOUND. A closed hat still under an open hat? Briefly erase A3 at that position. A hit missing? Go to its position, stopped, and write it.

Part 5 · Build the rolling bassline

Goal: write a rolling bass in group B that fills the gaps between the kicks, with B2 answering at the end of bar 4.

  1. Make B two bars long

    Press MAIN, stopped. Tap B. Hold RECORD, press + once, then release both.

    Check: The display shows Ln.2: B’s pattern is two bars long.

    Tap B briefly: holding it while pressing − / + switches its pattern.

  2. B1: bass in bar 1

    Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Write B1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.2, 1.3.3 and 1.4.2.

    Check: B1 lights at all four positions.

    Why

    These notes land after the kick, not on top of it.

  3. B1: bass in bar 2

    Hold SHIFT and press + to jump to bar 2. Write B1 at 2.1.3, 2.2.3, 2.3.2 and 2.4.3. Press PLAY.

    Check: A two-bar bass phrase bounces between the kicks.

    Stop.

  4. Double B to four bars

    Stopped, with B selected: tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + once, then tap RECORD again to leave record arm.

    Check: The display shows Ln.4: bars 3 and 4 repeat bars 1 and 2.

  5. B2: the answer at 4.4.3

    Press ERASE + B1 briefly: a long hold clears the whole bassline.

    Removes only the B1 note at 4.4.3.

    Go to 4.4.3. Briefly press ERASE + B1, then hold RECORD and tap B2.

    Wrong note? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: At 4.4.3 only B2 lights.

    Optional: in SOUND, tap B2 and turn Y (pitch) up a little for a different note. Turn it back if it clashes.

    Why

    Bar 4 now ends on B2, the answer before the loop restarts.

Checkpoint: kick and bass take turns: the bass fills the gaps between kicks, and bar 4 ends with B2.

If it doesn’t: kick and bass blur into one boom? Shorten B1: in SOUND, tap B1, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, go to the trim page with − / + and turn Y (length) down a little. Never hold SHIFT + SOUND there: it crops the file for good. Still muddy? Lower B1 and B2 with X in SOUND.

Part 6 · Make kick and bass breathe

Goal: make the bass dip under each kick, then keep the loop as GROOVE and copy it as DROP A.

  1. Checkpoint: kick and bass

    Press PLAY and listen to at least four loops.

    Check: Kick on every beat, the bass bouncing, B2 answering at the end of bar 4.

    Stop.

    Not working?

    Change one thing only: the bass level (X in SOUND), its length on the trim page, or the bass sound.

  2. Open the sidechain page

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press PLAY. Press SHIFT + FX to open the output settings, then press + once to reach sidechain.

    Check: The beat plays on while the sidechain page is open.

    Why

    Sidechain turns the bass down for a moment each time the kick plays.

  3. Kick as source, bass as destination

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold the A group button and press pad 1: the kick A1 becomes the source. Hold SHIFT and press B: the bass group becomes the destination.

    Check: The beat keeps playing while you set source and destination.

  4. Set the duck

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Turn X (length) to short and Y (shape) to about the middle.

    Check: The bass dips under each kick and recovers before its next note.

    Press MAIN, then PLAY to stop.

  5. The stand-up test

    Press PLAY and stand up for 20 seconds.

    Check: You hear a groove your body wants to move to.

    Stop.

    Not working?

    If it feels stiff, do not add layers: lower one loud percussion sound or shorten B1, then listen again.

  6. Write down GROOVE

    Stopped, hold MAIN and write the scene number down as GROOVE.

    Check: While MAIN is held, the display shows the scene number.

    Why

    This finished section stays as it is.

  7. Commit, and name the copy DROP A

    Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN again and write the new number as DROP A.

    Check: The number has changed. PLAY still sounds exactly like GROOVE.

    Release MAIN.

Checkpoint: GROOVE is kept, and you are in DROP A, which still sounds the same.

If it doesn’t: no audible dip? Make X longer. Pumping too hard? Make it shorter. Same scene number? Press SHIFT + MAIN once more while stopped.

Official guide: sidechain · commit

Part 7 · Record the vocal hook and add the synth answer

Goal: record your vocal hook on C1 and place it in DROP A, then make DROP B with a synth answer on C2.

  1. C: open SAMPLE

    Tap C, then press SAMPLE.

    Check: The pads blink.

  2. Choose the source

    Use − / + to choose the source.

    Check: The display names the source, such as usb or in.

    mic for your voice, usb for a phone or computer on a USB-C cable, in for a line cable. Over USB, choose K.O. II as the phone’s audio output.

  3. Set the input level

    Make a test sound and turn X (input level) up until it is strong but not distorted.

    Check: The pads keep blinking: you are still in SAMPLE.

  4. Set the threshold low

    Keep Y (threshold) low so recording starts as soon as you hold a pad.

    Check: The pads still blink.

  5. C1: record your hook

    Hold C1, sing or say two to four words, then release.

    Check: Recording stops as you release. C1 now holds your words.

    Keep it under about 1.5 seconds: one bar at 128 BPM lasts about 1.9. Someone else’s record is fine for practice, but a public release needs permission.

    Not working?

    Need both hands? Press SHIFT + C1 to sample hands-free, then SAMPLE to stop.

  6. Listen to C1

    Tap C1. Press MAIN when done.

    Check: You hear your words.

    Not working?

    Not happy? Press SAMPLE again and record C1 again.

  7. C1: select it in SOUND

    Press SOUND, tap C, then tap C1 to select it.

    Check: C1 plays your hook as you select it.

  8. Open the trim page

    Do not hold SHIFT + SOUND here: on the trim page that crops the file permanently.

    Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit, then use − / + to reach the trim page.

    Check: The display shows TRI: the trim page.

  9. Trim the start

    Turn X (start) a little at a time, tapping C1, until playback begins right at the first word.

    Check: Tapping C1 starts right on the first word.

  10. Trim the end

    Turn Y (length) until it ends right after the last word.

    Check: C1 now stops right after your last word.

  11. C1: set oneshot

    Still in sound edit, use − / + to find the page where X sets the play mode, and turn X to oneshot.

    Check: A single tap of C1 plays the whole trimmed phrase.

    The other choices are key and legato.

    Why

    In oneshot one trigger plays the whole trimmed phrase.

  12. Set the vocal level

    Press SOUND, then turn X (level) until the words are clear but not louder than the clap. Press MAIN.

    Check: C1 is clear but no louder than the clap.

  13. C: a fresh pattern

    Stopped in MAIN, tap C. Press SHIFT + A once.

    Check: PLAY still gives drums and bass, and nothing from C yet.

    Why

    C gets a fresh, empty pattern in this scene only, so GROOVE keeps its own.

  14. Make C four bars long

    Hold RECORD and press + three times, then release both.

    Check: The display shows Ln.4: C’s pattern is four bars long, still empty.

  15. C1: the hook at 1.1.1

    Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap C1 once, release. Press PLAY.

    Check: The hook plays once at the start of every four-bar loop.

    Stop.

  16. Check you are in DROP A

    Stopped. Hold MAIN and check the number is DROP A’s.

    Check: The display shows DROP A’s number from your paper.

    Why

    DROP A, with drums, bass and hook, stays as it is.

  17. Commit, and name the copy DROP B

    Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN again and write the new number as DROP B.

    Check: The new number differs from DROP A’s.

  18. DROP B: select C2

    In DROP B, press SOUND, tap C and tap C2.

    Check: The display shows C2’s sample number.

  19. Choose a short synth stab

    Hold SOUND, type 5, 0, 0 and browse 500–599 (melodic sounds) for a short synth or pluck stab. Turn X down so it is quiet.

    Check: C2 plays a short, quiet synth stab.

  20. C2: write the answer

    Press MAIN and, stopped, write C2 at 2.3.3 and 4.3.3. Hold SHIFT and press + to jump a bar.

    Check: C2 lights at 2.3.3 and 4.3.3.

    Why

    It answers the voice. It is not a full melody.

  21. Listen to DROP B

    Press PLAY and listen.

    Check: Drums, bass, the hook and the synth answer in bars 2 and 4.

    Stop. DROP B is finished.

    Not working?

    The hook fights the clap or hats? Lower the hats (A3) first, then C1. C2 clashes with the bass? Try another sound or turn its Y (pitch) a little in SOUND.

Checkpoint: GROOVE has no vocal, DROP A adds the hook and DROP B adds the synth answer.

If it doesn’t: GROOVE has the hook? There, tap C and briefly erase C1 at 1.1.1. DROP A has the stab? Briefly erase C2 there at 2.3.3 and 4.3.3. Words cut short? Set C1 to oneshot. Silent recording? Check the source and the X input level.

Official guide: sampling and hands-free sampling · sample over USB audio · sound edit, trim and play mode

Part 8 · Make the other sections

Goal: copy your scenes and strip them down into OUTRO, INTRO, BUILD, BREAK and PEAK.

  1. Copy GROOVE as OUTRO

    Stopped, hold MAIN and use − / + to select GROOVE, then release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as OUTRO.

    Check: The new number differs from GROOVE’s.

    Why

    Every new section starts as a copy of a finished one.

  2. Clear the open hats, rim and tom

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be OUTRO.

    Clears every note of A4, A5 and A7 in OUTRO.

    Tap A. Hold ERASE + A4 until TRK blinks, then release. Do the same with A5 and A7.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives kick, clap, closed hats, shaker and bass.

    Stop.

  3. Copy OUTRO as INTRO

    OUTRO is finished. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as INTRO.

    Check: The new number differs from OUTRO’s.

  4. Clear INTRO’s bass

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be INTRO.

    Clears every note of B1 and B2 in INTRO.

    Tap B. Hold ERASE + B1 until TRK blinks, then do the same with B2.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives drums only, no bass.

    Stop.

  5. Copy DROP A as BUILD

    Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP A, then release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as BUILD.

    Check: The new number differs from DROP A’s.

  6. Clear the bass and hats

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be BUILD.

    Clears the bass and both hats in BUILD.

    Tap B and clear B1 and B2 (hold ERASE + pad until TRK blinks). Tap A and clear A3 and A4 the same way.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives kick, clap, rim, shaker, tom fill and hook, no bass or hats.

    Stop.

  7. A2: a clap roll in bar 4

    Still in BUILD, stopped, with A selected: write A2 (clap) at 4.3.1, 4.3.3 and 4.4.3. Press PLAY to hear it, then stop.

    Check: Bar 4 ends with a roll of claps.

    Why

    With the claps already at 4.2.1 and 4.4.1, bar 4 now rolls into the next section.

  8. Copy BUILD as BREAK

    BUILD is finished. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as BREAK.

    Check: The new number differs from BUILD’s.

  9. Clear the kick, clap and tom

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be BREAK.

    Clears every note of A1, A2 and A7 in BREAK.

    Tap A. Hold ERASE + A1 until TRK blinks, then do the same with A2 and A7.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY gives only the rim, shaker and your hook.

    Stop.

    Why

    It should feel empty: that is what makes the next drop hit.

  10. Copy DROP B as PEAK

    Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP B, then release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once, hold MAIN and write the new number as PEAK.

    Check: The new number differs from DROP B’s.

  11. Remove the last kick

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be PEAK. Press briefly.

    Removes only the A1 note at 4.4.1.

    Tap A, go to 4.4.1 and briefly press ERASE + A1.

    Wrong note? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: PLAY sounds like DROP B with one kick missing at the very end.

    Stop.

    Why

    A tiny breath before the loop restarts.

Checkpoint: eight scenes, each matching the scene table below.

If it doesn’t: TRK never blinks? Stop playback, then keep holding until it blinks. A finished scene changed too? You erased before committing: press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit, or write the missing notes back. A copy is a mess? Select the scene it came from and commit a fresh copy.

Part 9 · Add space and a riser

Goal: give the hook a short reverb and, if you like, build a reversed riser for BUILD on D1.

  1. Play DROP B

    Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP B, release, and press PLAY.

    Check: DROP B plays: the hook plus the synth answer.

  2. C: add a short reverb

    Do not hold RECORD while moving the fader, or you record fader movement.

    Tap C, then press FX and use − / + to choose reverb. Raise the fader a little. Turn X down for a short reverb.

    Check: The hook gains space; the words stay clear.

    Keep the bass dry: add no reverb to B. Press MAIN, then PLAY to stop.

    Why

    In FX, the fader sets how much of group C goes to the reverb.

    Not working?

    If the words blur, lower the fader before changing anything else.

  3. Optional: copy C1’s sound

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Press SOUND, tap C, tap C1 and press SHIFT + C once to copy C1’s sound.

    Check: C1 plays your hook as you tap it.

  4. Optional: paste it onto D1

    Tap D, tap D1 and press SHIFT + D to paste it.

    Check: D1 now plays your hook.

  5. Optional: reverse D1

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    With D1 selected, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, press + until the display shows tim, and turn X until it shows rev. Tap D1. Press MAIN.

    Check: Your hook plays backwards. C1 is unchanged.

  6. Optional: a fresh D pattern in BUILD

    Hold MAIN, use − / + to select BUILD and release. Stopped, tap D and press SHIFT + A once.

    Check: The New pattern icon lights as D gets an empty pattern.

    Why

    A fresh empty pattern for D in this scene only.

  7. Optional: D1 at 4.1.1, four bars

    Hold RECORD and press + three times for four bars. Go to 4.1.1, holding SHIFT and pressing + to jump bars. Hold RECORD and tap D1.

    Check: Press PLAY: the reversed hook swells through bar 4 into the next section.

    Stop.

    Not working?

    If it runs past the end of bar 4, briefly erase it with ERASE + D1 and place it at 4.2.1 or 4.3.1 instead.

  8. Checkpoint: the sections in order

    Play INTRO, GROOVE, DROP A and DROP B in order. Hold MAIN and use − / + to switch.

    Check: Something new arrives with each scene.

    Why

    Bass and percussion, then the hook, then the synth answer.

    Not working?

    If two scenes sound the same, check that section’s clears against the scene table below.

Checkpoint: the hook has a little space without blurring the kick and bass, and each section adds something new.

If it doesn’t: everything washed out? Lower C’s reverb fader in FX. The riser hides the drop? Place it later or leave it out. The reversed D1 cuts off? Set it to oneshot, as for C1.

Part 10 · Build the 112-bar song list

Goal: check that every scene is four bars, then build the 28-position, 112-bar song list.

  1. Check every scene is four bars

    Spot-check INTRO, BREAK and PEAK: play each and count.

    Check: Each one restarts after four bars (16 beats).

    Groups you never lengthened repeat their one bar underneath. That is fine.

    Why

    A song position lasts as long as its scene’s longest pattern, so every scene must loop every four bars.

  2. Open the song list

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Stopped, hold MAIN and press ENTER to open the song list editor.

    Check: The display shows the current song position.

    Why

    In this fresh project the list starts with one position.

  3. Set position 1 to INTRO

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold SHIFT and press − / + to choose INTRO’s scene number for position 1.

    Check: Song position 1 now holds INTRO’s scene.

  4. Add position 2: INTRO

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold SHIFT and press A to add a position, then, still holding SHIFT, use − / + to choose its scene. Choose INTRO for position 2.

    Check: Song position 2 now holds INTRO’s scene too.

    Add while you are on the last position.

  5. Add positions 3 to 12

    Add positions 3–4 GROOVE, 5–8 DROP A and 9–12 DROP B the same way.

    Check: Release SHIFT and step through with − / +: exactly 12 positions so far.

  6. Add positions 13 to 28

    Add the rest the same way: 13–14 BREAK, 15–16 BUILD, 17–20 DROP A, 21–24 DROP B, 25–26 PEAK, 27–28 OUTRO.

    Check: The display shows song position 28, holding OUTRO.

    Scene numbers and song-position numbers are different things: check each one against your paper.

    Not working?

    Extra position? Select it and press SHIFT + C to cut it.

  7. Checkpoint: play the song

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SHIFT + PLAY to play the song from the start.

    Check: About 3:30, then the song stops by itself at the end.

    Why

    28 positions × 4 bars = 112 bars: about 3 minutes 30 seconds at 128 BPM.

    Not working?

    Ends much too soon? A position is missing. Drags on? A pattern is longer than four bars. Keeps looping? Song loop is on: with the unit on, press SHIFT + ERASE (system settings), type 4, 2, 0, press ENTER, then MAIN.

Part 11 · Speaker mix check

Goal: listen on a speaker, list three things you dislike, and fix them one at a time.

  1. List three things you dislike

    Play the whole song without touching anything. Write down only three things you dislike.

    Check: The song stops by itself, and your paper lists three dislikes.

    Do not try to repair every detail at once.

  2. First fix: the drum levels

    In SOUND, use X so the kick A1 is your loudest drum, the clap A2 slightly below it, hats and percussion lower.

    Check: The kick is loudest, the clap slightly below it.

    Why

    Pad levels apply in every scene of this project.

  3. Bass and vocal levels

    Set the bass B1/B2 under the kick, and the vocal C1 clearly audible but not shouting.

    Check: The bass sits under the kick, and the words are clear.

    Check at low volume too.

  4. Second fix: shorten the bass tail

    Never hold SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page.

    Low end muddy? Shorten B1’s tail: SOUND, tap B1, briefly SHIFT + SOUND, − / + to the trim page, Y down.

    Check: B1 ends sooner and the low end clears.

  5. Or lengthen the sidechain duck

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Or make the bass dip a little longer: SHIFT + FX, +, then X.

    Check: The bass ducks a little longer under each kick.

  6. If it feels empty or crowded

    If it feels empty, raise the shaker or rim slightly rather than adding a constant melody.

    Check: The shaker or rim is slightly louder in every scene.

    If it feels crowded, lower the rim (A5) or shaker (A6): one level change fixes every scene.

  7. Third fix: your real speaker

    Test on the speaker you will actually use, at a sensible volume.

    Check: On your speaker the kick leads and nothing distorts.

    Keep the K.O. II’s volume moderate: distortion does not improve when louder.

Checkpoint: at low and normal volume the kick leads, the bass sits under it and the words are clear.

If it doesn’t: change one level at a time and listen to the whole song again before the next change.

Part 12 · Record, save and share

Goal: play into the second drop, record the whole track, write down your scenes and back up your samples.

  1. Checkpoint: into the second drop

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    In the song list, use − / + to select position 9 (DROP B) and press PLAY.

    Check: It plays from there through BREAK and BUILD into DROP A.

    Stop.

    Why

    This is the payoff: after the break, the returning kick, bass and hook feel bigger without being louder.

  2. Connect a recorder

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Connect the K.O. II to a phone or computer with a USB-C cable. Choose K.O. II as the input in a recording app.

    Check: The recording app shows K.O. II as its input.

    Or record the headphone output with a recorder or audio interface.

    Why

    Record one full pass before experimenting again.

  3. Record the song

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Start recording, then press SHIFT + PLAY in the song list.

    Check: The song plays from the start while the app records.

    Optional: hold FX and press pads at the transitions for punch-in effects. Chapter 4 · Play & perform has more.

    Why

    Commit copies scenes. It does not save audio.

  4. Write down your scene numbers

    Write down the project number and the scene numbers of GROOVE, DROP A, DROP B, OUTRO, INTRO, BUILD, BREAK and PEAK.

    Your ticks on this page are saved only in this browser.

  5. Back up your samples

    Back up your samples to a computer with the EP sample tool.

    Check: A copy of your samples is on your computer.

    Releasing a track that samples someone else’s record? Get permission first: sampling does not grant rights.

  6. Optional: scenes wait for the pattern (412)

    Never hold SHIFT + ERASE while switching the unit on: that formats the drive.

    With the unit on, press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 1, 2, press ENTER, then MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Why

    Play the sections by hand instead of song mode.

  7. Optional: pick each next scene early

    While a scene plays, hold MAIN and press − / + before its pattern ends to pick the next scene.

    Check: The new scene starts when the current pattern ends.

  8. Optional: instant scene changes again (410)

    Type 410 the same way to switch back.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

  9. Checkpoint: the finished track

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    In the song list, press SHIFT + PLAY and listen to the whole track.

    Check: Kick and bass stay clear, the vocal sits where it matters, the drops differ.

    New ideas? Try them in a fresh copy with SHIFT + MAIN, so the finished scenes stay safe.

  10. Your tech house moves

    Read the seven moves below, then try one from memory.

    • Load by number: hold SOUND, type the number on the pads, release.
    • Double a pattern: arm RECORD, hold SHIFT and press +, then disarm.
    • A fresh pattern for a group: tap it, then SHIFT + A.
    • Sidechain: SHIFT + FX, +; hold a group button and press a pad for the source, SHIFT + a group to duck.
    • Sections: SHIFT + MAIN, then clear what you don’t need with ERASE + the pad until TRK blinks.
    • Reverb: tap the group, FX, − / + to reverb, raise the fader.
    • Song list: hold MAIN and press ENTER; SHIFT + A adds a position, SHIFT + − / + picks its scene.

Checkpoint: you have a recording of the full track and a written list of scenes.

If it doesn’t: recording silent? Check that K.O. II is chosen as the input in your recording app, or record from the headphone output instead.
