CHAPTER 08 / ABOUT 90–150 MINUTES

Tech House / party-ready track

Make a complete 112-bar, four-bar tech-house track at 128 BPM: punchy drums, rolling bass, moving percussion, a short vocal hook, two different drops, a breakdown and a proper ending. This is an exact recipe—not a promise of a professionally mastered release. Your sample choices, balance and party speaker still matter.

Important: Use a disposable, empty project. Stop playback before every position-by-position edit. This recipe assumes 16 timing, not 1 or 16T. Wrong note? Navigate to its position, then use a brief ERASE + its pad. Never use ERASE + SOUND for a wrong sequenced hit: that deletes the audio sample.

Your destination

Tempo
128 BPM
Main loop
4 bars / 64 steps
Roles
A drums · B bass · C vocal/stab · D transition
Song
28 song positions / 112 bars / about 3:30

The secret is not maximum sounds all the time. Your kick and bass repeat to make people dance. Changes happen at the ends of bars, between sections, and around the vocal.

0 / 48 steps checked

01 / Protect your old music and set the grid
  1. Choose a disposable project. Press MAIN. If PLAY is lit or music runs, press PLAY once to stop it. Do not begin in yesterday’s hip-hop project.
  2. Press TEMPO. Turn X until the display shows 128. Press MAIN again. This is the speed for this recipe.
  3. Press TIMING. Turn X until the display shows 16: not 1 and not 16T. Press minus to select quantized recording. Leave swing neutral. Press MAIN.
  4. Press MAIN, select group A, and confirm its pattern is empty and one bar. Do the same for B, C and D. If a pattern contains important music, select another empty pattern or project.
02 / Choose every sound before writing notes
  1. Press SOUND, select A1, and use minus/plus to choose a short punchy kick. Factory kicks are commonly in 001–099. Tap A1 to audition. Choose one that is solid but not a huge long boom.
  2. Still in SOUND, choose A2. Find a crisp short clap or snare, commonly 100–199. Then choose A3: a short closed hat, commonly 200–299. Turn X on A3 down so it is quieter than the kick and clap.
  3. Choose A4: a short open hat. Choose A5: a dry rim or woodblock. Choose A6: a soft shaker. Choose A7: a short tom or percussion hit. Do not chase perfection—pick usable short sounds.
  4. Select B1 in SOUND and choose a clean, short bass from around 400–499. Select B2 and choose the SAME bass sound or a closely matching one with a slightly longer tail. Keep B1 and B2 quieter than the kick for now.
03 / Write the four-on-the-floor foundation
  1. Press MAIN, select A and stop playback. Go to 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap A1, release. Repeat A1 at 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1. These are the four kick hits.
  2. At 1.2.1, hold RECORD and tap A2; repeat at 1.4.1. The clap sits on beats 2 and 4, on top of the kick.
  3. Write A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3. These are the first offbeat closed hats. Press PLAY for one loop, then stop. Kick should feel steady; hats should feel smaller than the clap.
  4. With A selected and stopped, tap RECORD to arm. Hold SHIFT and press plus once to double the one-bar drum pattern to two bars. Repeat once more to double two bars to four. Disarm RECORD. Check that A now plays four bars with the original notes copied.
04 / Make the percussion move across four bars
  1. Add A3 closed-hat accents at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4. These two extra ticks make the phrase less mechanical without filling every gap.
  2. Write A4 open hat at 2.4.3 and 4.4.3. At both exact positions, briefly erase A3 so the open hat replaces—not stacks with—the closed hat.
  3. Write A5 rim at 1.2.4, 2.3.4, 3.2.4 and 4.3.3. Make A5 quiet in SOUND. It should be felt as a tiny click, not heard as a loud lead instrument.
  4. Write A6 shaker at 1.1.4, 1.3.4, 2.1.4, 2.3.4, 3.1.4, 3.3.4, 4.1.4 and 4.3.4. Write A7 tom at 4.4.2 and 4.4.4. Play the full four bars. Bar 4 should feel like it turns you back toward bar 1.
05 / Build the rolling bassline
  1. Select B. Stop playback. Arm RECORD and press plus until B is four bars long. Unlike A, B began empty, so this only makes room; it does not write notes.
  2. Write B1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.2, 1.3.3 and 1.4.2. These notes land after the kick, not directly on top of it.
  3. Write B1 at 2.1.3, 2.2.3, 2.3.2 and 2.4.3. Write B1 at 3.1.3, 3.2.2, 3.3.3 and 3.4.2.
  4. Write B1 at 4.1.3, 4.2.3 and 4.3.2. At 4.4.3, write B2 once. Press PLAY. If kick and bass become one muddy sound, stop: lower B1/B2 X in SOUND and shorten their trim length with Y before adding anything else.
06 / Make kick and bass breathe
  1. Listen to only A and B for at least four full loops. The kick must be easy to identify on every beat. The bass must feel bouncy, with the B2 note acting as a final-bar answer. If not, change one thing only: bass level, bass length, or bass sample.
  2. If your installed OS has sidechain: press SHIFT + FX, use plus to reach sidechain, set A1 as the source and B as the destination. Use a short X length and a moderate Y shape. Stop when the bass dips under each kick then recovers before its offbeat note. If you cannot find sidechain, skip it today.
  3. Press SHIFT + MAIN once to commit this four-bar core groove into a new scene. Write the current scene number as DROP A. Check that it plays drums and bass for four bars before continuing.
  4. Press PLAY and stand up for 20 seconds. If your body naturally wants to move, continue. If it feels stiff, do not add more layers yet: remove one loud percussion part or make B1 shorter, then listen again.
07 / Add the vocal hook and synth answer
  1. Select C. Stop playback. Arm RECORD and press plus until C is four bars. Press SAMPLE and select LINE input to sample a record, or choose a short vocal sample already available. For a record: capture two to four sung words on C1, trim silent lead-in with X and shorten the tail with Y. Do not use an entire verse.
  2. In SOUND, set C1 to oneshot on the sound play-mode page. Lower C1 X until the words are clear but not louder than the clap. Return to MAIN. At 1.1.1, hold RECORD and tap C1 once. This phrase happens only once per four-bar loop.
  3. In SOUND choose C2: a short synth/pluck stab from around 500–599. Keep it quiet. Return to MAIN and write C2 at 2.3.3 and 4.3.3. It answers the voice; it is not a full melody.
  4. Play all groups. If C1 competes with clap or hat, lower the hats first, then adjust C1. If C2 clashes with bass, change its sound or Y pitch in small amounts. Do not solve a clash by adding another sound. Press SHIFT + MAIN and write the new scene number as DROP B.
08 / Create real section differences
  1. Select DROP A. Press SHIFT + MAIN to make a copy for INTRO. In INTRO erase all B1/B2 bass events, erase C1/C2, erase A4/A5/A7. Keep kick, clap, closed hats and shaker only. Check the earlier DROP A scene remains full.
  2. Select DROP A again. Commit a copy for GROOVE. Erase C1 at 1.1.1 and C2 at 2.3.3 and 4.3.3. GROOVE is drums plus bass without the hook.
  3. Select DROP B. Commit a copy for BREAK. Erase every A1 kick event and every B bass event. Keep shaker/rim and C1. If too empty, keep one quiet closed hat—not the full drum kit. This contrast makes the next drop hit harder.
  4. Select BREAK. Commit a copy for BUILD. Put A1 kick back at 1.1.1, 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1 only. Add A2 claps at 4.3.1, 4.3.3 and 4.4.3. Commit DROP B once more and call this copy PEAK; in PEAK erase A1 only at 4.4.1 for a tiny breath before the next loop.
09 / Add controlled space and final movement
  1. Play DROP B. Select C, press FX, choose a short reverb with minus/plus, then raise C’s fader only a little. Keep B bass send low. If words become blurry, lower the fader before changing any other setting.
  2. Optional D group: make D four bars. Put a short crash or impact on D1 at 1.1.1 only in BUILD and PEAK. If you cannot find a good impact in two minutes, skip it. A weak extra sound is worse than no extra sound.
  3. Listen from INTRO into GROOVE, then DROP A and DROP B. You should hear new elements arrive in this order: bass, then hook, then extra answer details. If every scene sounds equally busy, go back and remove one role from INTRO or GROOVE.
  4. For OUTRO, select GROOVE and commit a copy. Erase C if any is present, then remove A5 rim, A7 tom and A4 open hats. Keep drums plus bass simple. Write this scene number as OUTRO.
10 / Build the 112-bar song list
  1. Every scene in this recipe must have a longest pattern of exactly four bars. Check groups A, B, C and D in each scene before arranging. A song position plays for the longest pattern in its selected scene.
  2. On OS 2.0 or later, hold MAIN then press ENTER to open the Song list. Start only in an unused song list; do not overwrite a valuable arrangement. Assign INTRO to position 1. Use SHIFT + A to add each position. Hold SHIFT with minus/plus to choose the scene for the selected position.
  3. Assign positions 1–2 INTRO; 3–4 GROOVE; 5–8 DROP A; 9–12 DROP B; 13–14 BREAK; 15–16 BUILD; 17–20 DROP A; 21–24 DROP B; 25–26 PEAK; 27–28 OUTRO. Check every number: scene numbers and song-position numbers are different.
  4. Select Song position 1 and press SHIFT + PLAY to start from the beginning. At 128 BPM this is 112 bars, about 3 minutes 30 seconds. If it ends too soon, a group may be one bar. If it drags, check for an accidental longer pattern.
11 / Party-speaker mix check
  1. Play the entire arrangement without touching controls. Write down only three things you dislike. Do not try to repair every detail at once.
  2. First fix: in SOUND set kick A1 as your loudest drum, clap A2 slightly below it, hats/percussion lower, bass B under kick, then vocal C clearly audible but not shouting. Check at low volume too.
  3. Second fix: if the low end is muddy, shorten B1/B2 tail before lowering everything. If it feels empty, bring up shaker or rim a small amount—not another constant melody. If it feels crowded, remove an extra closed hat or rim hit.
  4. Third fix: test on the actual speaker you will use if possible. Keep the master level conservative; distorted party sound will not become better by adding more level on the K.O. II.
12 / Save, record and play it tonight
  1. Listen to the DROP B → BREAK → BUILD → DROP A transition. This is your main payoff. The breakdown should create anticipation; the returning bass and vocal should feel bigger without being louder than everything else.
  2. Record one full pass before experimenting again. Use a suitable recorder/audio interface from the K.O. II output, or supported USB-audio workflow on your installed OS. COMMIT is a scene copy, not a finished audio export.
  3. Write down project number plus INTRO, GROOVE, DROP A, DROP B, BREAK, BUILD, PEAK and OUTRO scene numbers. If song mode misbehaves tonight, you can perform the sections manually from that list.
  4. You are done when the whole track plays, kick and bass stay clear, the vocal appears only where it matters, and the two drops feel different. Do not ruin a finished dance track by endlessly adding sounds five minutes before people arrive.
