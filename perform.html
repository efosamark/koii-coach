K.O. II / COACH

CHAPTER 4 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Play & perform

Play the K.O. II live: feel, scales, rolls, effects and scene changes.

Until now you placed notes one step at a time. Now you play the K.O. II like an instrument, and every part starts from a practice copy, so your finished scenes stay safe.

You will make: a live performance of your own beat, and a recording of it.

Before you start

  • Project 6 from Chapters 1–2
  • Headphones or speakers
  • Paper and a pencil
  • Part 9: a USB-C or 3.5 mm cable
Written for OS 2.5.1. On an older OS, update first.

What you need

Your Chapter 1 and 2 project, with the scenes DRUMS, BEAT, INTRO, MELODY and BREAK and the 10-position song list. No project yet? Start with Chapter 1 · First beat and Chapter 2 · Make it a song. For Part 9, a USB-C cable to a phone or computer, or a 3.5 mm stereo cable to a recorder.

Your project
Project 6, from Chapters 1–2
Starting settings
90 BPM, TIMING 16, quantize
Time
15–25 minutes per part
Order
Any. Parts 6 and 7 use your Chapter 2 scenes and song
  • Clearing and deleting a scene (hold ERASE + MAIN, Part 6) can’t be undone.
  • Work only in practice copies.
Safety. Work only in practice copies: every part starts by making one, and the ERASE moves here touch only those copies.
  • Never use ERASE + SOUND: it deletes a sample from the device.
  • Press SHIFT + ERASE only with the K.O. II already on: it opens system settings. Held while you switch on, it formats the drive, and factory sounds cannot be recovered.
  • Holding work you care about? Back it up first with the EP sample tool on a computer.
  • SHIFT + B undoes the last change while the umbrella icon is lit.

How every part starts

To get back to project 6, press MAIN and stop playback. Hold MAIN, hold pad 6 as well (or your own project’s pad), then release both. Press PLAY to check it is your own beat, then stop.

Then make the part’s practice copy. Stopped, hold MAIN, use − / + to reach the scene the part names (usually BEAT) and release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once to commit: this copies the scene and moves you into the copy. Hold MAIN and write the new number down with the part’s name for it. The original stays as it was.

To change a system setting, press SHIFT + ERASE, type the three-digit code on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN. The table at the end lists every code.

Your pad plan

Part 1 · Record live with feel

Goal: replace your placed snare with one you play live, soft “ghost notes” included.

  1. Copy BEAT as a practice scene

    Stopped, hold MAIN, select BEAT with − / + and release. Press SHIFT + MAIN to copy it.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not BEAT’s.

  2. Name it LIVE and listen

    Hold MAIN and write the new number as LIVE. Press PLAY.

    Check: You hear the same drums and bass as BEAT.

    Press PLAY to stop.

  3. Turn on pad velocity (301)

    Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 3, 0, 1 on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Why

    301 is velocity high, for a soft touch. 300, the default, switches velocity off.

    Not working?

    Hit hard and everything still sounds loud? Use 302, made for hard playing.

  4. Play A2 softly, then firmly

    Tap A, then tap A2 very softly and then firmly.

    Check: You hear a quiet snare, then a loud one.

  5. Turn on the practice click (400)

    Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 0, 0, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Why

    The default, 401, clicks only while recording. 400 clicks while you practise too.

  6. Set the click volume

    Press TEMPO only once, and leave X alone: both change the tempo.

    Press PLAY: the click plays with the beat. Press TEMPO once and turn Y until the click is clear but not harsh.

    Check: The click gets louder or softer with Y.

    Press MAIN, then PLAY to stop.

  7. Clear the snare in LIVE

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be LIVE.

    Clears every note of A2 in LIVE.

    Stopped, in MAIN with group A selected, hold ERASE + A2 until TRK blinks, then release.

    If anything else disappeared, press SHIFT + B straight away.

    Check: Press PLAY: kick, hats and bass, but no snare.

    Stop.

  8. Set quantize on the 16 grid

    Press TIMING. Check that the display shows 16 (turn X if not), press − to choose quantize, then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 16. The MAIN icon is lit again.

    Why

    Quantize snaps every hit you record to the nearest of the 16 steps in the bar.

  9. Practise the backbeat on A2

    Press PLAY and, without touching RECORD, tap A2 on beats 2 and 4 (count “one, TWO, three, FOUR”).

    Check: Your snare joins the beat on 2 and 4.

    Nothing is recorded yet.

  10. Add soft ghost notes

    Then add one or two very soft taps right before beat 4. Practise until it feels steady, then stop.

    Check: The ghost notes sound much quieter than the backbeat.

    Why

    These quiet in-between hits are called ghost notes.

  11. Get ready to record live

    Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY.

    Check: You hear a four-beat count-in, then recording starts.

  12. Record one bar of snare

    After the count-in, play your snare part for one bar. Then press RECORD: recording stops and the beat plays on.

    Check: Your backbeat and ghost notes repeat by themselves, on the grid.

    Press PLAY to stop.

  13. Not happy? Undo, or erase while it plays

    Only in LIVE, your practice copy: hold MAIN to check.

    Erases only the A2 hits held through.

    Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again. Or, while it plays, hold ERASE + A2 as the unwanted hits sound.

    Too much gone? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Only the unwanted hits are gone.

    Why

    Erasing while the beat plays is called playback erase.

  14. Fix a ghost note that is too loud

    This changes every note on that step: use it only where no hat or kick shares the step.

    Is one ghost note too loud? Stopped, go to its position, hold SHIFT and turn X.

    Check: The ghost note on that step now plays quieter.

  15. Switch to free time

    Press TIMING, press + (free time), then MAIN.

    Check: The MAIN icon is lit again.

  16. Overdub loose ghost notes

    Press PLAY, hold RECORD, tap two or three soft A2 notes during one bar, then release RECORD.

    Check: The new notes sit slightly off the grid, as you played them.

    Stop.

    Why

    Holding RECORD while the beat plays records on top of what is there (an overdub).

    Not working?

    Sounds sloppy? Press SHIFT + B.

  17. Optional: turn on swing

    Press TIMING, press − (quantize), check the display shows 16, turn Y a little and press MAIN.

    Check: The SWING icon lights.

    Why

    Swing gives the hats a shuffle. It only shapes notes you record after setting it, and only on the 8 and 16 grids.

  18. Optional: clear the hats in LIVE

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be LIVE.

    Clears every note of A3 in LIVE.

    Stopped, with group A selected, hold ERASE + A3 until TRK blinks.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: no hats, everything else plays. Stop.

  19. Optional: record swung hats

    Tap and release RECORD, press PLAY. After the count-in, tap A3 on every “and” and every beat (eight even taps) for one bar, then press RECORD.

    Check: The hats lean into a shuffle.

    Hold MAIN and select BEAT to compare: its hats stay straight.

    Not working?

    Too much or too little? Press SHIFT + B, change Y and record again.

  20. Set quantize back, without swing

    Press TIMING, press − (quantize), turn Y back to no swing, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SWING icon goes out.

    Why

    Later chapters expect quantize and no swing.

  21. Set the metronome back (401)

    Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 0, 1, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Keep velocity on (301) if you like it, or type 300 the same way to switch it off.

    Why

    With velocity on, notes you write with held RECORD + pad also come out quieter when you press softly.

Checkpoint: LIVE plays your own snare backbeat with a few soft ghost notes, and BEAT still plays the snare you placed.

If it doesn’t: no count-in, or nothing kept? Tap RECORD, release it, then press PLAY. Every hit equally loud? Type the velocity code again and confirm it with ENTER. Hits land off the grid? You are still in free time: press TIMING, then −.

Part 2 · Play a bassline in a scale with KEYS

Goal: turn one bass sound into a 12-note keyboard in a scale, and record a two-bar bassline live.

  1. Copy DRUMS as BASSLINE

    Stopped, hold MAIN, select DRUMS, your drums-only scene, with − / + and release. Press SHIFT + MAIN to copy it.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, the copy’s.

    Not working?

    No DRUMS scene? Copy BEAT instead.

  2. Name it, and listen

    Write the new number as BASSLINE. Tap B and press PLAY.

    Check: Drums only, no bass.

    Stop.

  3. Copied BEAT? Clear its bass

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be BASSLINE, not BEAT.

    Clears every note of B1 in BASSLINE.

    Stopped, with B selected, hold ERASE + B1 until TRK blinks.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: drums only, no bass. Stop.

  4. Make group B two bars long

    Stopped in MAIN with group B selected, hold RECORD, press + once and release both.

    Check: The display shows Ln.2: B’s pattern is two bars long.

    Why

    A stays one bar long and repeats underneath.

  5. B1: switch on KEYS

    Tap B1, your Chapter 1 bass, to select it. Press KEYS, then tap all twelve pads.

    Check: Each pad now plays B1’s sound at a different pitch.

    Not working?

    Pads still play different sounds? KEYS is not on: tap B1 and press KEYS again.

  6. Drop an octave

    For a deeper bass, hold KEYS and press − once.

    Check: Each pad plays B1’s bass an octave lower.

    Not working?

    Too boomy on small speakers? Hold KEYS and press + to go back up.

  7. Choose a key

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Hold KEYS and slowly turn X to choose a key, the note the scale is built on.

    Check: The display shows the key, changing as X turns.

    Why

    Over drums alone any key works, so pick one.

  8. Choose the blues scale

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Still holding KEYS, turn Y until the display shows BLU, the blues scale.

    Check: The display shows BLU.

  9. Play the scale

    Release KEYS and tap the pads.

    Check: Only notes from that scale play now, so a “wrong” note is hard to find.

    Why

    The key and scale are saved with the project.

  10. Count along for two bars

    Press PLAY. Count “1, 2, 3, 4” for two bars.

    Check: The drums play under your count.

  11. Play a two-bar bassline

    Play the lowest-sounding pad on beat 1 of each bar, and two or three other pads anywhere else.

    Check: You play the same two-bar line twice in a row.

    Stop.

  12. Record your bassline

    Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap RECORD, press PLAY, and after the count-in play your two-bar line once. Then press RECORD.

    Check: Your bassline repeats every two bars over the drums.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Not working?

    Messy? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again.

  13. Switch KEYS off

    Press KEYS again to switch it off. Tap A, then A1 and A2.

    Check: You hear kick and snare, not one sound at different pitches.

    The piano icon (KEYS MODE) should now be off. Hold MAIN and select BEAT: it still has its two original bass pulses.

    Why

    How KEYS switches off isn’t documented, so this step tries KEYS again. The piano icon and the pads show whether it worked.

    Not working?

    Piano icon still lit, or one sound at many pitches? Try MAIN, then tap A again, though this isn’t documented either. Whatever works, report it with “This step doesn’t match my K.O. II”.

Checkpoint: BASSLINE plays the drums plus your own two-bar bassline, and every note fits the scale.

If it doesn’t: the bass repeats after one bar? B is still one bar long: repeat the two-bars step with B selected. X or Y changed volume or pitch? Keep KEYS held while you turn.

Part 3 · Note repeat, hat rolls and the arpeggio

Goal: let the K.O. II repeat a pad for you: steady hats, a roll, a hands-free repeat and an arpeggio, held notes played one at a time.

  1. Copy BEAT as ROLL

    Hold MAIN, select BEAT, release and press SHIFT + MAIN. Write the new number as ROLL.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not BEAT’s.

  2. ROLL: clear the hats

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be ROLL.

    Clears every note of A3 in ROLL.

    Stopped, tap A and hold ERASE + A3 until TRK blinks.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: kick, snare and bass only.

    Stop.

  3. Set the repeat to eighth notes

    Press TIMING, turn X until the display shows 8, not 8T, and press − for quantize. Press MAIN, then PLAY.

    Check: Kick, snare and bass play, with no hats.

  4. Hold TIMING + A3

    Hold TIMING, then press and hold A3. Release A3 and it stops.

    Check: The hat repeats by itself in even eighth notes.

    Stop.

    Why

    With velocity on (Part 1), pressing harder makes the repeats louder.

  5. Get ready to record the hats

    Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap RECORD, press PLAY.

    Check: You hear a four-beat count-in, then recording starts.

  6. Record one bar of repeated hats

    After the count-in, hold TIMING + A3 for exactly one bar, let go, then press RECORD.

    Check: You hear steady eighth-note hats again, this time played by you.

    Stop.

    Not working?

    Uneven? Press SHIFT + B and try again.

  7. Set a fast roll (32)

    Press TIMING, turn X to 32, then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 32. The MAIN icon is lit again.

  8. Record a roll on beat 4

    Tap RECORD and press PLAY. After the count-in, count the bar, and on beat 4 hold TIMING + A3 for that one beat only.

    Check: The hat rolls fast through beat 4.

  9. Let go and stop recording

    Let go before the next “one”, then press RECORD.

    Check: You hear a fast “trrrr” leading into every new loop.

    Stop.

  10. Set the grid back to 16

    Press TIMING, turn X back to 16, then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 16, not 16T. The MAIN icon is lit again.

  11. Set the repeat to one per beat

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press PLAY. Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 4.

    Check: The beat plays and the display shows 4.

    Why

    One hat per beat.

  12. Latch the hat

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    While still in TIMING, hold SHIFT and tap A3.

    Check: The hat repeats once per beat, hands-free.

    Play A1 and A2 on top for fun. RECORD is off, so nothing is written.

  13. Stop the latch and reset

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    To stop it, hold SHIFT and tap A3 again while in TIMING. Stop playback, press TIMING, turn X back to 16 and press MAIN.

    Check: The hat repeat stops, then playback stops.

  14. C2: select an unused pad

    Tap C, press SOUND and tap C2, an unused pad.

    Check: The SOUND icon lights. C2 plays its current sound, if any.

    Why

    The arpeggio only works when the sound’s play mode is oneshot or legato.

  15. Load a short melodic sound

    Hold SOUND, type 5, 1, 0 and release, or browse 500–599 with − / + for a short melodic sound.

    Check: The display shows a number in 500–599: melodic sounds.

  16. Find the play mode page

    Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit. Use − / + to find the page where X changes the play mode.

    Check: The display changes with each page.

    Why

    It is the sound page: X sets the play mode, Y the pan.

  17. Set C2 to oneshot or legato

    Turn X until the display shows oneshot or legato, not key. Press MAIN.

    Check: C2 now plays one note at a time.

    Why

    Legato may show as LEG. This changes C2 only.

  18. Switch on KEYS at the 16 grid

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Tap C2, then press KEYS. Press PLAY. Press TIMING and turn X to 16, which sets the arpeggio’s speed.

    Check: The piano icon lights and the beat plays.

  19. Play an arpeggio

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Now hold TIMING and press and hold three pads, one after another.

    Check: The notes play one at a time, in the order you pressed them.

    Let go to stop. Try 8 for a slower arpeggio, then stop playback.

  20. Set quantize back

    Press TIMING, set X to 16, press − (quantize), then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 16, not 16T. The MAIN icon is lit again.

  21. Switch KEYS off

    Press KEYS again to switch it off, then tap C1.

    Check: C1 plays your Chapter 2 melody sound at its normal pitch.

    Not working?

    Piano icon still lit? See Not working? in the first Switch KEYS off step.

Checkpoint: ROLL plays your finger-drummed hats, with a roll into every loop.

If it doesn’t: the pad plays once instead of repeating? Hold TIMING first, then the pad. The arpeggio plays all notes together, or only one? Set C2’s play mode to oneshot or legato. A latch won’t stop? In TIMING, hold SHIFT and tap the same pad again.

Part 4 · Punch-in FX and fader moves

Goal: play effects live with FX and the pads (punch-in FX), then record a fader move and erase only that move.

  1. Copy BEAT as FXJAM

    Hold MAIN, select BEAT, release and press SHIFT + MAIN. Write the new number as FXJAM. Press PLAY and leave it running.

    Check: The copy plays the same beat as BEAT.

  2. Beat repeat: FX + pad 1

    Use only the 12 pads here. FX + a group button (A–D) solos that group instead.

    Near the end of a loop, hold FX and press pad 1 for one beat. Let go on the next “one”.

    Check: The beat stutters, then carries on normally when you let go.

    Why

    Pad 1 is the pad marked 1, where A1 sits. FX + 1 is beat repeat.

  3. Try the other punch-in FX

    Still playing, hold FX and try the other pads one at a time, for a beat or two each. Write down your two favourite pad numbers.

    Check: Each pad changes the beat differently while held.

    Stop.

    Why

    Punch-in FX respond to how hard you press, and two held pads combine. Owners agree on 2 tape stop, 4 low-pass, 5 high-pass, 6 effect send and 9 bit crush, and differ on the rest (unofficial cheat sheet, Elektronauts), so trust your ears.

  4. Read the fader labels

    Stopped, tap A (drums). Hold FADER and read the labels printed above the pads: they name the fader functions.

    Check: The labels include level, pitch and pan.

    Why

    Each one is something the fader can control instead of volume.

  5. Assign the fader to pitch

    Still holding FADER, press the pad labelled for pitch, then release. Press PLAY and move the fader slowly.

    Check: The whole drum group bends up and down.

  6. Find normal pitch again

    Find the fader position where the drums sound normal again, then press PLAY to stop.

    Check: The drums sound normal again, then playback stops.

  7. Record a fader move

    Keep your fingers off the pads while RECORD is held, or they record notes too.

    Press PLAY. At the start of a loop, hold RECORD and slide the fader down and back up during one bar, then release RECORD.

    Check: You hear your move repeat by itself every loop.

    Stop.

  8. See which function has moves

    Now hold FADER.

    Check: The pitch pad blinks: it holds a recorded move.

  9. Erase the pitch moves only

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be FXJAM.

    Erases the recorded pitch moves only. The notes stay.

    Stopped, hold ERASE + FADER and press the pitch pad.

    Umbrella lit? SHIFT + B undoes it. Otherwise record the move again.

    Check: Press PLAY: the drums no longer bend.

    Stop. To erase every fader move in this group and pattern at once: stopped, hold ERASE + FADER for 2 seconds, until FDR blinks and then DEL shows.

    Why

    Other fader functions keep their recorded moves.

  10. Reset the fader functions

    Stopped, press SHIFT + FADER.

    Pressing it again undoes the reset (SET shows).

    Check: RES shows, and every fader function of this group returns to its default position.

    Why

    It only works while stopped.

  11. Set the fader back to level

    Hold FADER and press the pad labelled for level.

    Check: The fader controls drum volume again, not pitch.

Checkpoint: FX + pad 1 makes the beat stutter, and your pitch move replayed by itself until you erased it.

If it doesn’t: punch-in FX do nothing? They only work while the beat plays: press PLAY first. Fader move not kept? Hold RECORD the whole time the fader moves, while the beat plays. Drums still detuned after erasing? Press SHIFT + FADER while stopped.

Part 5 · Edit by pad: copy, paste and shift

Goal: copy the kick’s notes to a second sound, then shift that layer and one snare a little later to change the feel.

  1. Copy BEAT as EDIT

    Hold MAIN, select BEAT, release and press SHIFT + MAIN. Write the new number as EDIT. Tap A.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not BEAT’s.

  2. A4: choose a free pad

    Press SOUND and tap A4, which Chapters 1–2 leave unused.

    Check: The SOUND icon lights. A4 plays its current sound, if any.

    Not working?

    A4 holds a sound you use? Pick another free pad and use it for A4 below.

  3. Load a short click on A4

    Hold SOUND, type 3, 0, 0 and release, or browse 300–399 with − / + for a short click, rim or clap.

    Check: The display shows a number in 300–399: percussion.

  4. Make it quieter than the kick

    Turn X so it is quieter than the kick. Press MAIN.

    Check: A4 now sounds quieter than the kick.

  5. Copy A1’s notes onto A4

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Stopped in MAIN: hold A1, press SHIFT + C, and release everything. Now hold A4, press SHIFT + D, and release.

    Check: Tap A4: it still plays its own sound, not the kick.

    Why

    This copies A1’s notes only. Without a pad held, SHIFT + C copies a whole bar or pattern instead.

  6. Check the copied layer

    Go to 1.2.3 with − / +. Press PLAY.

    Check: A4 lights there, like A1. Every kick now has the percussion stacked on top.

    Stop.

  7. Open timing correct

    With playback stopped, press SHIFT + TIMING to open timing correct.

    Check: The display changes to timing correct. No note moves yet.

  8. A4: move the layer six ticks later

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Careful: in timing correct, a plain tap on a pad while stopped quantizes all its notes back onto the grid.

    Hold A4 and, still holding it, press + six times, then release.

    Check: The display still shows timing correct.

    Holding a pad while the beat plays quantizes its notes as they pass, so keep playback stopped.

    Why

    Each press moves every A4 note one tick later. A step is 24 ticks, so six ticks are a quarter step.

  9. Hear the flam

    Press MAIN, then PLAY.

    Check: The layer lands slightly after the kick, a thicker double hit called a flam.

    Stop.

  10. Free time, at 1.4.1

    Press TIMING, press + (free time), then MAIN. Stopped, go to 1.4.1.

    Check: The display shows 1.4.1, and A2 lights there.

  11. Nudge the beat-4 snare

    Hold A2 before you press +, because SHIFT + + on its own jumps a whole bar.

    Hold SHIFT and A2, keep both held, press + six times, then release. Press PLAY.

    Check: The snare on beat 4 drags slightly behind, while the beat 2 snare stays put.

    Stop.

    Why

    In free time each press nudges the note one tick. In quantize, the same move jumps a whole step.

  12. Set quantize back and compare

    Press TIMING, press − (quantize), then MAIN. Compare: hold MAIN and switch between EDIT and BEAT while playing.

    Check: EDIT sounds thicker and slightly laid-back.

    Stop.

Checkpoint: EDIT has a flam on every kick and a late snare on beat 4, and BEAT is unchanged.

If it doesn’t: the paste did nothing, or pasted a whole bar? Keep the pad held while you press SHIFT + C or SHIFT + D. The snare jumped a whole step? You were in quantize: press SHIFT + B, switch to free time and nudge again. The layer snapped back onto the grid? You tapped A4 in timing correct: hold it and press + again.

Part 6 · Scenes as a live performance

Goal: perform your Chapter 2 scenes live, with every change landing on time, and clear a scene safely.

  1. Make scene changes wait (412)

    In project 6, stop playback. Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 1, 2, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Why

    The default, 410, changes scene the moment you pick it.

  2. Write a running order

    On paper, write a running order using your Chapter 2 scene numbers: INTRO 4 passes, BEAT 4, MELODY 2, BREAK 2, MELODY 2, BEAT 2.

    Why

    INTRO and BEAT are one bar long; MELODY and BREAK are two, so one pass of them lasts two bars.

  3. Play INTRO and count the passes

    Hold MAIN, select INTRO, release and press PLAY. Count the passes.

    Check: INTRO plays, repeating once per bar.

  4. Pick the next scene early

    During the last pass, hold MAIN, press − / + until the display shows the next scene’s number, and release.

    Check: The change lands at the end of the pattern, though you chose it early.

    Work through your running order, then press PLAY to stop.

  5. Try a direct jump to a scene

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Stopped, hold MAIN and type a scene number on the pads. Press PLAY.

    Check: The scene whose number you typed plays.

    Why

    A quick MAIN + number switches to any scene from 1 to 99. Holding MAIN and a pad 1–9 until the display shows P and a number changes project instead, as owners report.

    Not working?

    Music suddenly not yours? You changed project. Hold MAIN and press your project’s pad (6 from Chapter 1) to go back. When performing, switch scenes with MAIN + − / +.

  6. Perform your running order

    Perform your running order from start to finish, without stopping. Stop at the end.

    Check: Each scene change lands at the end of a pattern.

    Add a punch-in FX (Part 4) in the last beat before a change, or a latched hat repeat (Part 3) during BREAK.

  7. Copy a throwaway scene: TRASH

    Stopped, press SHIFT + MAIN to copy the scene you are in, then hold MAIN and write the new number as TRASH.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows the copy’s new number.

  8. Clear TRASH

    Needs OS 2.0.1 or newer

    Only ever do this on a scene whose number you checked a moment ago.

    Clears everything in this scene.

    Hold ERASE + MAIN for 2 seconds, until CLR shows.

    No undo: treat the cleared scene as gone for good.

    Check: Press PLAY: TRASH is silent. Stop.

  9. Delete the empty TRASH scene

    Needs OS 2.0.1 or newer

    Hold MAIN first: the number must still be TRASH.

    Deletes the scene itself.

    Hold ERASE + MAIN for 2 seconds again.

    No undo: a deleted scene cannot be brought back.

    Check: DEL shows: the empty TRASH scene is deleted.

    Then select the scene you copied and press PLAY: it should be unchanged.

    Why

    The scene is now empty, so if it is not in the song list, DEL blinks and it is deleted.

  10. Optional: start in lock mode

    Hold only MAIN: SHIFT + ERASE at power-on formats the drive.

    Switch the K.O. II off, then hold MAIN while you switch it on.

    Check: LOK shows for about a second.

    Why

    In lock mode, every change resets when you restart, so you can perform without changing your project.

  11. Optional: leave lock mode

    Before you build anything to keep, switch off, then on again holding nothing, to leave lock mode (LOK).

    Check: The K.O. II starts without showing LOK.

  12. Set instant scene changes (410)

    Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 1, 0, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Or keep 412 if you like performing this way.

Checkpoint: every scene change landed on a pattern boundary, and TRASH showed CLR and then DEL.

If it doesn’t: changes still happen instantly? Type the code again and confirm it with ENTER. Nothing changed? You released MAIN on the same number: watch the display while you press − / +. DEL never appears? The scene is in the song list, which is fine: leave it.

Part 7 · Song mode, deeper

Goal: make the breakdown in your Chapter 2 song longer, then rehearse with song loop and move a position.

  1. Copy BREAK as BREAK2

    In project 6, stop playback. Hold MAIN, select BREAK, release, then press SHIFT + MAIN. Write the new number as BREAK2.

    Check: Press PLAY: melody, snare and hats, two bars long.

    Stop.

  2. Double C’s pattern in BREAK2

    In BREAK2, stopped, tap C. Tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT, press + once and release. Tap RECORD again if it still blinks.

    Check: Press PLAY: the melody plays twice before the scene repeats.

    Stop.

    Why

    This doubles C’s pattern to four bars, copying its notes into the new bars. The RECORD icon blinks while armed, ready to record.

  3. Open the song list

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold MAIN and press ENTER to open the song list.

    Check: The display shows the current song position.

  4. Put BREAK2 at position 7

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Use − / + to go to position 7. Hold SHIFT and press − / + to set it to BREAK2’s number.

    Check: Position 7 now holds BREAK2’s number.

    Why

    The first BREAK in the Chapter 2 song map.

  5. Play the longer song

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SHIFT + PLAY to play from the start.

    Check: The breakdown at position 7 now lasts four bars.

    Why

    A song position lasts as long as the longest pattern in its scene. Position 10 still uses BREAK, so the ending stays short.

  6. Make the song loop (421)

    Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 1, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

  7. Rehearse with the loop

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Open the song list again (hold MAIN, press ENTER) and press SHIFT + PLAY.

    Check: After position 10 the song starts again.

    Practise punch-in FX at the section changes. Press PLAY to stop; the song goes back to the position you started from.

  8. Switch the loop off (420)

    Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 0, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

  9. Optional: cut position 10

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    In the song list, go to position 10, hold SHIFT and press C to cut it.

    Check: The song list now has 9 positions.

    Why

    Cutting and inserting is how you move a position.

  10. Optional: insert it at position 1

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Go to position 1, hold SHIFT and press D to insert it. Press SHIFT + PLAY and listen for where the BREAK landed.

    Check: BREAK now plays at or near the start.

    Prefer the old order? Go to the moved BREAK and cut it again with SHIFT + C. Then go to the last position, add one with SHIFT + A and set it to BREAK with SHIFT + − / +.

    Why

    Insert puts it into the selected song position.

Checkpoint: the song plays INTRO, BEAT, MELODY, a four-bar BREAK2, MELODY and a short BREAK, then stops.

If it doesn’t: breakdown still two bars? Position 7 still holds BREAK, or C in BREAK2 is still two bars long. Song never stops? Song loop is still on: set 420. No Chapter 2 song yet? Build it in Chapter 2 · Make it a song first.

Part 8 · Make the mix pump with sidechain

Goal: make the bass and melody dip on every kick (sidechain), so the kick punches through. It affects every scene in this project.

  1. Play MELODY

    In project 6, hold MAIN, select MELODY, release and press PLAY. Leave it running for the whole part.

    Check: MELODY plays: drums, bass and melody.

  2. Open the sidechain settings

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SHIFT + FX to open the output settings. Press + once for the sidechain settings.

    Check: The display shows OUT, then S.Ch after +.

    Do not turn X or Y yet.

  3. A1: make the kick the trigger

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold A, press pad 1 (A1), then release.

    Check: MELODY plays on unchanged: nothing dips until you choose groups.

    Why

    Sidechain on the K.O. II reacts to notes, so every A1 note now starts a dip.

  4. Choose B and C to dip

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Do not add A, or the drums would dip under their own kick.

    Hold SHIFT and press B, then hold SHIFT and press C.

    Check: The drums play on, undipped.

    Why

    Every group you add dips under the kick.

  5. Shape the dip with X and Y

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Turn X (the length of the dip) and Y (its shape) a little at a time.

    Check: The kick sits clearly on top, and the melody swells back between kicks.

    Why

    A short X gives a quick dip right under the kick, a long X a big, breathing pump. The display shows LEN for the length and ShP for the shape.

  6. Leave the settings and listen

    Press MAIN to leave the settings, then stop. Play BEAT and INTRO too, and compare them.

    Check: BEAT’s bass pumps too. INTRO, drums only, stays steady.

    Why

    Sidechain belongs to this project, so your other projects never pump. To soften it later, open sidechain again (SHIFT + FX, then +) and shorten X.

    Not working?

    Want B or C to stop dipping? How to remove a group isn’t documented. Hold SHIFT and press B or C again, the likeliest way, and listen.

Checkpoint: the bass and melody dip on every kick and swell back between kicks.

If it doesn’t: no pump? Hold A and press pad 1 again, then hold SHIFT and press B, then C, and lengthen X. Drums pump too? A was added as a group to dip.

Part 9 · Record your performance

Goal: capture your live song as an audio file on a phone or computer.

  1. Connect the K.O. II over USB-C

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Connect the K.O. II to your phone or computer with a USB-C cable, plus any adapter your phone needs.

    Check: The USB MIDI icon lights, and a new USB audio device appears.

    Why

    It works as a USB audio device with any phone or computer that supports standard USB audio.

  2. Choose it as your app’s input

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    In your recording app or DAW, choose the K.O. II as the input.

    Check: The app shows the K.O. II as its input.

    Not working?

    Look for a new USB audio device named “ko2”. Owners’ tools find it by “EP-133” in its name on a Mac (ep-series-sysex) and “EP133” on Linux (sound-arranger). Restarts or shows BAT? Fit fresh AAA batteries: USB power must give 5 V and at least 1 A.

  3. Set the recording level

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Start the song: hold MAIN, press ENTER, then SHIFT + PLAY. Watch your app’s level meter.

    Check: The app’s meter peaks below the top.

    Not working?

    Too loud? Lower the app’s input level, or select the loudest group in MAIN and lower its fader.

  4. Record your performance

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Start recording in the app, then, in the song list, press SHIFT + PLAY on the K.O. II and perform.

    Check: The song plays from the start while the app records.

    Add punch-in FX, a latched repeat or, if you play scenes by hand, scene changes.

  5. Stop the recording and listen

    After the song stops, wait for the last sound to fade, then stop the app. Listen back on the phone or computer.

    Check: Your song plays back, with your live effects.

  6. No USB audio? Use the line output

    Connect a stereo 3.5 mm cable to the line input of an audio interface or recorder, and start with low levels.

    Check: The recorder’s level meter moves when the K.O. II plays.

    Either way your recording is audio only: back up the project itself with the official EP sample tool on a computer.

    Why

    The 3.5 mm socket is a stereo headphone and line output.

  7. Your performance moves

    Read the eight moves below, then try one from memory.

    • Record live: at 1.1.1 tap RECORD, press PLAY, play after the count-in, then press RECORD.
    • TIMING: X sets the grid, − quantize, + free time, Y swing.
    • KEYS: select a pad, press KEYS; hold it and turn X for the key, Y for the scale.
    • Note repeat: hold TIMING and a pad; in TIMING, SHIFT + the pad latches it.
    • Punch-in FX: while playing, hold FX and press a pad.
    • Copy a pad’s notes: hold it, SHIFT + C; hold another, SHIFT + D.
    • Next scene in time: hold MAIN, − / + before the change lands.
    • Sidechain: SHIFT + FX, +; hold a group button and press a pad for the source, SHIFT + a group to dip.

Checkpoint: a file on your phone or computer plays your song, including your live effects.

If it doesn’t: the app records silence? Choose the K.O. II as its input, not the built-in microphone. Still silent? Some USB-C cables only charge: try another. On a phone, the app must accept an external USB input.
