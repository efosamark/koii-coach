K.O. II / COACH

CHAPTER 4 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Play & perform

Play the K.O. II live: feel, scales, rolls, effects and scene changes.

Until now you placed most notes one step at a time. In this chapter you play the K.O. II like an instrument. You record with feel, play a bassline in a scale and roll hi-hats. You also throw in effects, nudge notes by hand, switch scenes live, make the mix pump and record what you play. Each section is one short mini-project that starts from a practice copy of a scene you already made. Do the sections in any order: your finished scenes stay safe.

You will make: a live performance of your own beat, and a recording of it.

Before you start

  • Project 6 from Chapters 1–2
  • Headphones or speakers
  • Paper and a pencil
  • Part 9: a USB-C or 3.5 mm cable
Written for OS 2.5.1. Every step here works on a K.O. II running OS 2.5.1. The official “what’s new” page describes features up to OS 2.5 and has no separate 2.5.1 notes. This chapter was checked against it and the official guide on 13 October 2026. Older OS: update first. This app cannot see your hardware, so trust your display and your ears.

What you need

You need your K.O. II, headphones or speakers, and paper and a pencil for scene numbers. You also need the project from Chapters 1 and 2: the scenes DRUMS, BEAT, INTRO, MELODY and BREAK, and the 10-position song list. No project yet? Do Chapter 1 · First beat and Chapter 2 · Make it a song first. For Part 9 you also need a USB-C cable to a phone or computer, or a 3.5 mm stereo cable to a recorder.

Your project
Project 6 from Chapters 1–2 (Chapter 3 used project 7)
Starting settings
90 BPM, TIMING 16, quantize
Time
15–25 minutes per section
Order
Any. Parts 6 and 7 use your Chapter 2 scenes and song
  • Clearing and deleting a scene (hold ERASE + MAIN, Part 6) can’t be undone.
  • Work only in practice copies.
Safety. Work only in practice copies: every section starts by making one, and the ERASE moves here only touch those copies. Never use ERASE + SOUND: it deletes a sample from the device. Open system settings with SHIFT + ERASE only while the K.O. II is already switched on. Holding SHIFT + ERASE while you switch it on formats the drive, and the official guide warns that factory sounds “can not be recovered”. If this K.O. II holds work you care about, back it up first with the official EP sample tool on a computer. SHIFT + B undoes the last change while the umbrella icon is lit.

How every section starts

This chapter uses project 6, your project from Chapters 1 and 2; Chapter 3 worked in project 7. To get back, press MAIN, stop playback, hold MAIN, hold pad 6 as well, then release both (use your own pad if you chose another project in Chapter 1). On OS 2.0+ a quick MAIN + number can select a scene instead, so press PLAY and check that you hear your own beat. Then stop.

Stop playback. Hold MAIN, use − / + until the display shows the number of the scene the section names (usually BEAT), and release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once: commit copies the scene and moves you into the copy. Hold MAIN, read the new number and write it down with the name the section gives it. The original scene stays as it was.

To change a system setting: press SHIFT + ERASE, type the three-digit code on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN to return. The table at the end lists every code this chapter uses and its default.

Your pad plan

Part 1 · Record live with feel

Goal: replace your placed snare with one you play yourself, including soft “ghost notes”. Along the way you set up count-in, metronome, velocity, quantize and undo.

  1. Copy BEAT as a practice scene

    In project 6, make a practice copy of BEAT: stopped, hold MAIN, select BEAT with − / +, release, then press SHIFT + MAIN.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not BEAT’s.

  2. Name it LIVE and listen

    Hold MAIN and write the new number as LIVE. Press PLAY.

    Check: You hear the same drums and bass as BEAT.

    Press PLAY to stop.

  3. Turn on pad velocity (301)

    Turn on pad velocity, so soft taps play quietly: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 3, 0, 1 on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out. Soft taps now play quietly.

    Why

    301 is velocity “high”, for playing “with a soft touch”. 300 switches velocity off again; that is the default.

    Not working?

    If you hit hard and everything still sounds loud, use 302 (“for vigorous play styles”).

  4. Play A2 softly, then firmly

    Tap A, then tap A2 very softly and then firmly.

    Check: You hear a quiet snare, then a loud one.

  5. Turn on the practice click (400)

    Hear a click while you practise: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 0, 0, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out. The click now plays with the beat.

    Why

    The default, 401, clicks only while recording; 402 clicks only during the count-in.

  6. Set the click volume

    Tapping TEMPO repeatedly sets a tap tempo, and X changes the BPM, so leave both alone.

    Press PLAY: a metronome click now plays with the beat. Press TEMPO once and turn Y until the click is clear but not harsh.

    Check: The click gets louder or softer with Y.

    Press MAIN, then PLAY to stop.

  7. Clear the snare in LIVE

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be LIVE.

    Clears every note of A2 in LIVE.

    Stopped, in MAIN with group A selected, hold ERASE + A2 until TRK blinks, then release.

    If anything else disappeared, press SHIFT + B straight away.

    Check: Press PLAY: kick, hats and bass, but no snare.

    Stop.

  8. Set quantize on the 16 grid

    Press TIMING. Check that the display shows 16 (turn X if not), press − to choose quantize, then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 16; the MAIN icon is lit again.

    Why

    Quantize snaps every hit you record to the nearest of the 16 steps in the bar.

  9. Practise the backbeat on A2

    Press PLAY and, without touching RECORD, tap A2 on beats 2 and 4 (count “one, TWO, three, FOUR”).

    Check: Your snare joins the beat on 2 and 4.

    Nothing is recorded yet.

  10. Add soft ghost notes

    Then add one or two very soft taps right before beat 4. Practise until it feels steady, then stop.

    Check: The ghost notes sound much quieter than the backbeat.

    Why

    These quiet in-between hits are called ghost notes.

  11. Get ready to record live

    Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY.

    Check: You hear a four-beat count-in, then recording starts.

  12. Record one bar of snare

    After the four count-in clicks, play your snare part for one bar. Then stop tapping and press RECORD: recording stops, the beat keeps playing.

    Check: Your backbeat and ghost notes repeat by themselves, on the grid.

    Press PLAY to stop.

  13. Not happy? Undo, or erase while it plays

    Only in LIVE, your practice copy: hold MAIN to check.

    Erases only the A2 hits held through.

    Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again. Or, while it plays, hold ERASE + A2 as the unwanted hits sound.

    Too much gone? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Only the hits you wanted are gone; the rest repeats.

    Why

    The guide calls erasing while the beat plays “playback erase”.

  14. Fix a ghost note that is too loud

    This changes the velocity of every note on that step, so use it only where no hat or kick shares the step.

    Is one ghost note too loud? Stopped, go to its position, hold SHIFT and turn X.

    Check: The ghost note on that step now plays quieter.

  15. Switch to free time

    Now add loose ghost notes in free time: press TIMING, press + (free time), then MAIN.

    Check: The MAIN icon is lit again.

  16. Overdub loose ghost notes

    Press PLAY, hold RECORD, tap two or three soft A2 notes during one bar, then release RECORD.

    Check: Listen: free-time notes keep your exact timing, slightly ahead of or behind the grid.

    Stop.

    Why

    Holding RECORD while the beat plays records on top of what is there (an overdub).

    Not working?

    Sounds sloppy? Press SHIFT + B.

  17. Optional: turn on swing

    Press TIMING, press − (quantize), check the display shows 16, turn Y a little and press MAIN.

    Check: The SWING icon lights (official icon map: “lit when swing has been enabled”).

    Why

    Optional: give the hats some swing. Swing only shapes notes you record after setting it (the guide: “note interval and swing apply before recording and not after”), and only with the 1/8 and 1/16 intervals.

  18. Optional: clear the hats in LIVE

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be LIVE.

    Clears every note of A3 in LIVE.

    Stopped, with group A selected, hold ERASE + A3 until TRK blinks to clear the hats in LIVE only.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: no hats, everything else plays. Stop.

  19. Optional: record swung hats

    Tap and release RECORD, press PLAY. After the count-in, tap A3 on every “and” and every beat (eight even taps) for one bar, then press RECORD.

    Check: The hats lean into a shuffle.

    Hold MAIN and select BEAT to compare: its hats stay straight.

    Not working?

    Too much or too little? Press SHIFT + B, change Y and record again.

  20. Set quantize back, without swing

    Set things back: press TIMING, press − (quantize), turn Y back to no swing (the SWING icon goes out), then MAIN.

    Check: The SWING icon goes out.

    Why

    Later chapters expect quantize and no swing.

  21. Set the metronome back (401)

    Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 0, 1, press ENTER, then MAIN to return the metronome to its default.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out. The click now plays only while recording.

    Keep velocity on (301) if you like it, or type 300 the same way to switch it off.

    Why

    With velocity on, notes you write with held RECORD + pad also come out quieter when you press softly.

Checkpoint: LIVE plays your own snare backbeat with a few soft ghost notes, and BEAT still plays the snare you placed.

If it doesn’t: no count-in means you pressed RECORD and PLAY together, which starts without one: tap RECORD, release it, then press PLAY. Every hit equally loud: the velocity code was not confirmed with ENTER. Hits land off the grid: you are still in free time, so press TIMING, then −. Nothing was kept: RECORD was not armed before you pressed PLAY.

Official guide: 9.1 live record · 10.4 timing · 12.1 velocity · 12.3 metronome · system setting codes

Part 2 · Play a bassline in a scale with KEYS

Goal: turn one bass sound into a 12-note keyboard, choose a key and scale so every pad fits, and record a two-bar bassline live.

  1. Copy DRUMS as BASSLINE

    In project 6, make a practice copy of DRUMS, your drums-only scene: stopped, hold MAIN, select DRUMS with − / +, release, then press SHIFT + MAIN.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, the copy’s.

    Not working?

    No DRUMS scene? Copy BEAT instead.

  2. Name it, and listen

    Write the new number as BASSLINE. Tap B and press PLAY: drums only, no bass.

    Check: You hear drums only; no bass plays.

    Stop.

  3. Copied BEAT? Clear its bass

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be BASSLINE, not BEAT.

    Clears every note of B1 in BASSLINE.

    Then, stopped with B selected, hold ERASE + B1 until TRK blinks to clear its bass notes.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: drums only, no bass. Stop.

  4. Make group B two bars long

    Stopped in MAIN with group B selected, hold RECORD, press + once and release both.

    Check: The display shows Ln.2: B’s pattern is two bars long.

    Why

    A stays one bar long and repeats underneath.

  5. B1: switch on KEYS

    Tap B1 to select it; you hear your Chapter 1 bass. Press KEYS, then tap all twelve pads.

    Check: Each pad now plays B1’s sound at a different pitch.

    Not working?

    If the pads still play different sounds, KEYS is not on: tap B1 and press KEYS again.

  6. Drop an octave

    For a deeper bass, hold KEYS and press − once: every pad drops an octave.

    Check: Each pad plays B1’s bass an octave lower.

    Not working?

    Too boomy on small speakers? Hold KEYS and press + to go back up.

  7. Choose a key

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Hold KEYS and slowly turn X: the display shows the key, the note the scale is built on.

    Check: The display shows the key, changing as X turns.

    Why

    Over drums alone any key works, so pick one.

  8. Choose the blues scale

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Still holding KEYS, turn Y to choose the scale. OS 2.5 added BLU (blues), H.MI (harmonic minor) and M.MI (melodic minor); choose BLU for this bassline.

    Check: The display shows BLU.

  9. Play the scale

    Release KEYS and tap the pads.

    Check: Only notes from that scale play now, so a “wrong” note is hard to find.

    Why

    OS 2.5 saves the key and scale with the project.

  10. Count along for two bars

    Press PLAY. Count “1, 2, 3, 4” for two bars.

    Check: The drums play under your count.

  11. Play a two-bar bassline

    Play the lowest-sounding pad on beat 1 of each bar, and two or three other pads anywhere else.

    Check: You play the same two-bar line twice in a row.

    Stop.

  12. Record your bassline

    Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap RECORD, press PLAY, and after the count-in play your two-bar line once. Then press RECORD.

    Check: Your bassline repeats every two bars over the drums.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Not working?

    Messy? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again.

  13. Switch KEYS off

    Press KEYS again to switch KEYS off. Tap A, then A1 and A2.

    Check: You hear kick and snare, not one sound at different pitches.

    Check the display: the piano icon, KEYS MODE (official icon map: “all 12 pads in ‘chromatic’ mode”), should now be off. Finally, hold MAIN and select BEAT: it still has its two original bass pulses.

    Why

    The guide shows how to switch KEYS on but not off, and no owner report we found says either, so this step tries KEYS again. The piano icon and the pads tell you whether it worked.

    Not working?

    Piano icon still lit, or the pads still play one sound at many pitches? Try MAIN, then tap A again: no source says this works either. Tell us what worked on your unit with the “This step doesn’t match my K.O. II” link, so this step can say it for sure.

Checkpoint: BASSLINE plays the drums plus your own two-bar bassline, and every note fits the scale.

If it doesn’t: the bass repeats after one bar because B is still one bar long: repeat the two-bars step with B selected. Turning X or Y changed the volume or pitch instead: you let go of KEYS; keep it held while you turn. Key and scale selection with X and Y needs OS 2.5; older OS: update first.

Official guide: 9.3 keys · 12.2 change the scale in keys mode · what’s new: keys mode (OS 2.5)

Part 3 · Note repeat, hat rolls and the arpeggio

Goal: let the K.O. II repeat a pad for you. You finger-drum the hi-hats and a roll, latch a repeat so it plays hands-free, and arpeggiate notes in KEYS.

  1. Copy BEAT as ROLL

    Hold MAIN, select BEAT, release and press SHIFT + MAIN: note the copy as ROLL.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not BEAT’s.

  2. ROLL: clear the hats

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be ROLL.

    Clears every note of A3 in ROLL.

    Stopped, tap A and hold ERASE + A3 until TRK blinks.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: kick, snare and bass only.

    Stop.

  3. Set the repeat to eighth notes

    Press TIMING, turn X until the display shows 8 (eight steps per bar, not 8T), press − for quantize, then press MAIN. Press PLAY.

    Check: Kick, snare and bass play, with no hats.

  4. Hold TIMING + A3

    Hold TIMING, then press and hold A3. Release A3 and it stops.

    Check: The hat repeats by itself in even eighth notes.

    Stop.

    Why

    With velocity on (Part 1), pressing harder makes the repeats louder.

  5. Get ready to record the hats

    Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap RECORD, press PLAY.

    Check: You hear a four-beat count-in, then recording starts.

  6. Record one bar of repeated hats

    After the count-in, hold TIMING + A3 for exactly one bar, let go, then press RECORD.

    Check: You hear steady eighth-note hats again, this time played by you.

    Stop.

    Not working?

    Uneven? Press SHIFT + B and try again.

  7. Set a fast roll (32)

    Now add a roll into each loop: press TIMING, turn X to 32, then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 32; the MAIN icon is lit again.

  8. Record a roll on beat 4

    Tap RECORD and press PLAY. After the count-in, count the bar, and on beat 4 hold TIMING + A3 for that one beat only.

    Check: The hat rolls fast through beat 4.

  9. Let go and stop recording

    Let go before the next “one”, then press RECORD.

    Check: You hear a fast “trrrr” leading into every new loop.

    Stop.

  10. Set the grid back to 16

    Press TIMING, turn X back to 16, then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 16, not 16T; the MAIN icon is lit again.

  11. Set the repeat to one per beat

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hands-free hats: press PLAY. Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 4.

    Check: The beat plays and the display shows 4.

    Why

    One hat per beat (1/1, 1/2 and 1/4 arrived in OS 2.0).

  12. Latch the hat

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    While still in TIMING, hold SHIFT and tap A3: the hat keeps repeating after you let go.

    Check: The hat repeats once per beat, hands-free.

    Play A1 and A2 on top for fun; RECORD is off, so nothing is written.

  13. Stop the latch and reset

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    To stop it, hold SHIFT and tap A3 again while in TIMING. Stop playback, press TIMING, turn X back to 16 and press MAIN.

    Check: The hat repeat stops, then playback stops.

  14. C2: select an unused pad

    Tap C, press SOUND and tap C2, an unused pad.

    Check: The SOUND icon lights; C2 plays its current sound, if any.

    Why

    The arpeggio (OS 2.5) only works when the sound’s play mode is oneshot or legato.

  15. Load a short melodic sound

    Hold SOUND, type 5, 1, 0 and release, or browse 500–599 with − / + for a short melodic sound.

    Check: The display shows a number in 500–599: melodic sounds.

  16. Find the play mode page

    Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit. Use − / + to find the page where X changes the play mode.

    Check: The display changes with each page.

    Why

    The guide calls it the sound page, with X for play mode and Y for pan.

  17. Set C2 to oneshot or legato

    Turn X until the display shows oneshot or legato, not key. Press MAIN.

    Check: C2 now plays one note at a time.

    Why

    The OS 2.5 notes write legato as LEG. This changes C2 only.

  18. Switch on KEYS at the 16 grid

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Tap C2 so it is selected, then press KEYS. Press PLAY. Press TIMING and turn X to 16; the TIMING interval sets the arpeggio’s speed.

    Check: The piano icon lights and the beat plays.

  19. Play an arpeggio

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Now hold TIMING and press and hold three pads, one after another.

    Check: The notes play one at a time, in the order you pressed them.

    Let go to stop. Try 8 for a slower arpeggio, then stop playback.

  20. Set quantize back

    Clean up: press TIMING, set X to 16, press − (quantize), then press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 16, not 16T; the MAIN icon is lit again.

  21. Switch KEYS off

    Press KEYS again to switch it off, then tap C1.

    Check: C1 plays your Chapter 2 melody sound at its normal pitch.

    Not working?

    Piano icon still lit? See Not working? in the first Switch KEYS off step.

Checkpoint: ROLL plays hats you finger-drummed, with a roll into every loop. You can latch a repeat and stop it, and held KEYS notes arpeggiate.

If it doesn’t: the pad plays once instead of repeating, so hold TIMING first, then the pad. The arpeggio plays all notes together or only one: C2’s play mode is still key, so set oneshot or legato. A latch that won’t stop needs the same move again: in TIMING, hold SHIFT and tap the same pad. The arpeggio needs OS 2.5; latching and the 1, 2 and 4 intervals need OS 2.0. Older OS: update first.

Official guide: 10.4.1 note repeat and arpeggio · 10.4 timing · 8.2.1 sound (play mode) · what’s new: arpeggio (OS 2.5), larger intervals and latch (OS 2.0)

Part 4 · Punch-in FX and fader moves

Goal: perform effects on the running beat with punch-in FX. Then record a fader move into the pattern, and erase only that move.

  1. Copy BEAT as FXJAM

    Hold MAIN, select BEAT, release and press SHIFT + MAIN: note the copy as FXJAM. Press PLAY and leave it running.

    Check: The copy plays the same beat as BEAT.

  2. Beat repeat: FX + pad 1

    Use only the 12 pads here. FX + a group button (A–D) solos that group instead.

    Near the end of a loop, hold FX and press pad 1 for one beat. Let go on the next “one”.

    Check: The beat stutters, then carries on normally when you let go.

    Why

    Pad 1 is the pad marked 1, where A1 sits. The OS 2.5 notes call FX + 1 beat repeat.

  3. Try the other punch-in FX

    Still playing, hold FX and try the other pads one at a time, for a beat or two each. Write down your two favourite pad numbers.

    Check: Each pad changes the beat differently while held.

    Stop.

    Why

    Punch-in FX respond to how hard you press and combine when you hold two pads; the guide doesn’t list every pad, and the update notes name FX + 1 beat repeat and a tape-stop effect. Owner lists (unofficial cheat sheet, Elektronauts) agree on 2 tape stop, 4 low-pass, 5 high-pass, 6 effect send and 9 bit crush, and differ on the rest, so trust your ears.

  4. Read the fader labels

    Stopped, tap A (drums). Hold FADER and read the labels printed above the pads: they name the fader functions.

    Check: The labels include level, pitch and pan.

    Why

    The OS 2.5 notes name level, pitch and pan among them.

  5. Assign the fader to pitch

    Still holding FADER, press the pad labelled for pitch, then release. Press PLAY and move the fader slowly.

    Check: The whole drum group bends up and down.

  6. Find normal pitch again

    Find the fader position where the drums sound normal again, then press PLAY to stop.

    Check: The drums sound normal again, then playback stops.

  7. Record a fader move

    Keep your fingers off the pads while RECORD is held, or they record notes too.

    Press PLAY. At the start of a loop, hold RECORD and slide the fader down and back up during one bar, then release RECORD.

    Check: You hear your move repeat by itself every loop.

    Stop.

  8. See which function has moves

    Now hold FADER.

    Check: The pitch pad blinks, because functions with recorded moves blink.

  9. Erase the pitch moves only

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be FXJAM.

    Erases the recorded pitch moves only; the notes stay.

    Stopped, hold ERASE + FADER and press the pitch pad.

    Umbrella lit? SHIFT + B undoes it; otherwise record the move again.

    Check: Press PLAY: the drums no longer bend.

    Stop. To erase every fader move in this group and pattern at once: stopped, hold ERASE + FADER for 2 seconds, until FDR blinks and then DEL shows.

    Why

    This OS 2.5 move erases the recorded moves of that one fader function only.

  10. Reset the fader functions

    Stopped, press SHIFT + FADER.

    Pressing it again undoes the reset (SET shows).

    Check: RES shows, and every fader function of this group returns to its default position.

    Why

    OS 2.5 does not allow this during playback.

  11. Set the fader back to level

    Then hold FADER and press the pad labelled for level, so the fader controls group volume again, as the rest of the course expects.

    Check: The fader controls drum volume again, not pitch.

Checkpoint: FX + pad 1 makes the beat stutter. Your recorded pitch move replayed by itself, and you erased it without touching any notes.

If it doesn’t: punch-in FX do nothing while stopped, so press PLAY first. The fader move was not kept: hold RECORD the whole time the fader moves, while the beat plays. The drums still sound detuned after erasing: press SHIFT + FADER while stopped. Per-function erase needs OS 2.5; older OS: update first.

Official guide: 11.7 punch-in FX · 9.4 fader · 9.6 erase / undo · what’s new: sequencer and performance (OS 2.5)

Part 5 · Edit by pad: copy, paste and shift

Goal: layer your kick with a second sound by copying one pad’s notes to another pad. Then shift that layer, and one snare, by a few ticks to change the feel.

  1. Copy BEAT as EDIT

    Hold MAIN, select BEAT, release and press SHIFT + MAIN: note the copy as EDIT. Tap A.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not BEAT’s.

  2. A4: choose a free pad

    Press SOUND and tap A4, which Chapters 1–2 leave unused.

    Check: The SOUND icon lights; A4 plays its current sound, if any.

    Not working?

    If A4 holds a sound you use, pick another free pad and use it instead of A4 below.

  3. Load a short click on A4

    Hold SOUND, type 3, 0, 0 and release, or browse 300–399 with − / + for a short click, rim or clap.

    Check: The display shows a number in 300–399: percussion.

  4. Make it quieter than the kick

    Turn X so it is quieter than the kick. Press MAIN.

    Check: A4 now sounds quieter than the kick.

  5. Copy A1’s notes onto A4

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Stopped in MAIN: hold A1, press SHIFT + C, and release everything. Now hold A4, press SHIFT + D, and release.

    Check: Wherever A1 lights, A4 now lights too.

    Why

    This copies A1’s notes only (OS 2.5). Without a pad held, SHIFT + C copies a whole bar or pattern instead.

  6. Check the copied layer

    Go to 1.2.3 with − / +. Press PLAY.

    Check: A4 lights there, like A1. Every kick now has the percussion stacked on top.

    Stop.

  7. Open timing correct

    With playback stopped, press SHIFT + TIMING to open timing correct.

    Check: The display changes to timing correct; no note moves yet.

  8. A4: move the layer six ticks later

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Careful: in timing correct, a plain tap on a pad while stopped quantizes all its notes back onto the grid.

    Hold A4 and, still holding it, press + six times, then release.

    Check: The display still shows timing correct; you hear the result in the next step.

    Holding a pad while the beat plays quantizes its notes as they pass, so keep playback stopped.

    Why

    Each press shifts the whole layer, every A4 note, one tick later. A step is 24 ticks, so six ticks are a quarter step, about 40 ms at 90 BPM.

  9. Hear the flam

    Press MAIN, then PLAY.

    Check: The layer lands slightly after the kick, a thicker double hit called a flam.

    Stop.

  10. Free time, at 1.4.1

    Make one snare lazy: press TIMING, press + (free time), then MAIN. Stopped, go to 1.4.1.

    Check: The display shows 1.4.1, and A2 lights there.

  11. Nudge the beat-4 snare

    Hold A2 before you press +, because SHIFT + + on its own jumps a whole bar.

    Hold SHIFT and A2, keep both held, press + six times, then release. Press PLAY.

    Check: The snare on beat 4 drags slightly behind, while the beat 2 snare stays put.

    Stop.

    Why

    In free time each press nudges the note by one tick. In quantize mode the same move jumps a whole step, which is a quick way to move a note to the next step.

  12. Set quantize back and compare

    Press TIMING, press − (quantize), then MAIN. Compare: hold MAIN and switch between EDIT and BEAT while playing.

    Check: EDIT sounds thicker and slightly laid-back.

    Stop.

Checkpoint: EDIT has a percussion layer that follows the kick rhythm with a slight flam, plus a late snare on beat 4. BEAT is unchanged.

If it doesn’t: if the paste did nothing or pasted a whole bar, keep the pad held while you press SHIFT + C or SHIFT + D. The snare jumped a whole step because you were in quantize: press SHIFT + B, switch to free time and nudge again. The layer snapped back onto the grid because you tapped A4 in timing correct: hold it and press + again. Per-pad copy and the one-tick shift need OS 2.5; older OS: update first.

Part 6 · Scenes as a live performance

Goal: perform your Chapter 2 sections live, with every scene change landing on time, and learn to clear a scene safely.

  1. Make scene changes wait (412)

    In project 6, stop playback. Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 1, 2, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out. Scene changes now wait for the pattern’s end.

    Why

    Now a scene you pick while playing waits for the end of the current pattern (412). 411 waits for the end of the bar instead; 410, the default, changes immediately.

  2. Write a running order

    On paper, write a running order using your Chapter 2 scene numbers: INTRO 4 passes, BEAT 4, MELODY 2, BREAK 2, MELODY 2, BEAT 2.

    Why

    INTRO and BEAT are one bar long; MELODY and BREAK are two, so one pass of them lasts two bars.

  3. Play INTRO and count the passes

    Hold MAIN, select INTRO, release and press PLAY. Count the passes.

    Check: INTRO plays, repeating once per bar.

  4. Pick the next scene early

    During the last pass, hold MAIN, press − / + until the display shows the next scene’s number, and release.

    Check: The change lands at the end of the pattern, though you chose it early.

    Work through your running order, then press PLAY to stop.

  5. Try a direct jump to a scene

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Try a direct jump while stopped: hold MAIN and type a scene number on the pads, then press PLAY to check.

    Check: The scene whose number you typed plays.

    Why

    The official guide says MAIN + numpad “can now always switch to any scene 1-99” (OS 2.0+), and that you hold MAIN and press pad 1–9 to change project. A quick tap switches scene; keeping the pad held until the display shows P and its number changes project, as the guide’s picture (P 1) and owners show.

    Not working?

    If the music is suddenly not yours, you changed project. Hold MAIN and press your project’s pad (6 if you followed Chapter 1) to go back, and switch scenes with MAIN + − / + when performing.

  6. Perform your running order

    Perform your running order from start to finish, without stopping. Stop at the end.

    Check: Each scene change lands at the end of a pattern.

    Add a punch-in FX (Part 4) in the last beat before a change, or a latched hat repeat (Part 3) during BREAK.

  7. Copy a throwaway scene: TRASH

    Stopped, press SHIFT + MAIN to copy the scene you are in, then hold MAIN and write the new number as TRASH.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows the copy’s new number.

  8. Clear TRASH

    Needs OS 2.0.1 or newer

    Only ever do this on a scene whose number you checked a moment ago.

    Clears everything in this scene.

    Hold ERASE + MAIN for 2 seconds: CLR shows, because a scene with notes is cleared, not deleted.

    No undo: treat the cleared scene as gone for good.

    Check: Press PLAY: TRASH is silent. Stop.

  9. Delete the empty TRASH scene

    Needs OS 2.0.1 or newer

    Hold MAIN first: the number must still be TRASH.

    Deletes the scene itself.

    Hold ERASE + MAIN for 2 seconds again.

    No undo: a deleted scene cannot be brought back.

    Check: DEL shows: the empty TRASH scene is deleted.

    Then select the scene you copied and press PLAY: it should be unchanged.

    Why

    The scene is now empty, so if it is not in the song list, DEL blinks and it is deleted.

  10. Optional: start in lock mode

    Hold only MAIN: SHIFT + ERASE at power-on formats the drive.

    Optional, for a gig: switch the K.O. II off, then hold MAIN while you switch it on.

    Check: LOK shows for about a second.

    Why

    In lock mode “any changes made on the device will reset when the device is restarted”, so you can perform without changing your project.

  11. Optional: leave lock mode

    Switch off and on again without holding anything before you build anything you want to keep, and check that LOK does not appear.

    Check: The K.O. II starts without showing LOK.

  12. Set instant scene changes (410)

    To go back to instant scene changes, press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 1, 0, press ENTER, then MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out. Scene changes happen immediately again.

    Or keep 412 if you like performing this way.

Checkpoint: every scene change landed on a pattern boundary, and TRASH showed CLR and then DEL.

If it doesn’t: changes still happen instantly when the code was not confirmed with ENTER; enter it again. Nothing changed: you released MAIN on the same number, so watch the display while you press − / +. DEL never appears: the scene is in the song list, which is fine; leave it.

Part 7 · Song mode, deeper

Goal: lengthen the breakdown in your Chapter 2 song by giving its scene a longer pattern. Then rehearse with song loop and move a position. A song can hold up to 99 positions.

  1. Copy BREAK as BREAK2

    In project 6, stop playback. Hold MAIN, select BREAK, release, then press SHIFT + MAIN. Write the new number as BREAK2.

    Check: Press PLAY: melody, snare and hats, two bars long.

    Stop.

  2. Double C’s pattern in BREAK2

    In BREAK2, tap C. Stopped, tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + once, then release; tap RECORD again if it is still armed.

    Check: Press PLAY: the melody plays twice before the scene repeats.

    Stop.

    Why

    This doubles C’s pattern to four bars and copies its notes into the new bars. The RECORD icon “blinks when armed to record”, says the official icon map.

  3. Open the song list

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold MAIN and press ENTER to open the song list.

    Check: The display shows the current song position.

  4. Put BREAK2 at position 7

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Use − / + to go to position 7. Hold SHIFT and press − / + until position 7 holds BREAK2’s number.

    Check: Position 7 now holds BREAK2’s number.

    Why

    The first BREAK in the Chapter 2 song map.

  5. Play the longer song

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SHIFT + PLAY to play from the start.

    Check: The breakdown at position 7 now lasts four bars.

    Why

    A song position lasts as long as the longest pattern in its scene, and position 10 still uses BREAK, so the ending stays short. The song is now 18 bars, about 48 seconds at 90 BPM, and it stops by itself at the end.

  6. Make the song loop (421)

    For rehearsing, make the song repeat: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 1, press ENTER, then MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out. Song loop is now on.

  7. Rehearse with the loop

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Open the song list again (hold MAIN, press ENTER) and press SHIFT + PLAY: after position 10 the song starts again.

    Check: After position 10 the song starts again.

    Practise punch-in FX at the section changes. Press PLAY to stop; the song goes back to the position you started from.

  8. Switch the loop off (420)

    Then press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 0, press ENTER and MAIN, so the song stops at the end again.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out. Song loop is now off.

  9. Optional: cut position 10

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    In the song list, go to position 10, hold SHIFT and press C: the position is cut to the clipboard and the list has 9 positions.

    Check: The song list now has 9 positions.

    Why

    Optional: move a position.

  10. Optional: insert it at position 1

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Go to position 1, hold SHIFT and press D to insert it. Press SHIFT + PLAY and listen for where the BREAK landed.

    Check: BREAK now plays at or near the start.

    Prefer the old order? Go to the moved BREAK and cut it again with SHIFT + C. Then go to the last position, add one with SHIFT + A and set it to BREAK with SHIFT + − / +.

    Why

    The guide only says it is inserted “into the selected song position”.

Checkpoint: the song plays INTRO, BEAT, MELODY, a four-bar BREAK2, MELODY and a short BREAK, then stops.

If it doesn’t: a two-bar breakdown means position 7 still holds BREAK, or C in BREAK2 is still two bars long. The song never stops: song loop is still on, so set 420. The song list doesn’t open: song mode needs OS 2.0; update first. No Chapter 2 song yet? Build it in Chapter 2 · Make it a song first.

Official guide: 8.3.1 song mode · 6.2 song mode (position length) · 9.1 duplicate pattern length · system codes 420–421 · what’s new: song mode (OS 2.0, 2.0.1)

Part 8 · Make the mix pump with sidechain

Goal: make the bass and melody dip each time the kick hits, so the kick punches through and the groove “breathes” (OS 2.0+). Sidechain sits in the output settings, not in a scene, so expect it to affect every scene in this project.

  1. Play MELODY

    In project 6, hold MAIN, select MELODY (drums, bass and melody), release and press PLAY. Leave it running for the whole section.

    Check: MELODY plays: drums, bass and melody.

  2. Open the sidechain settings

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SHIFT + FX: the output settings (the master compressor) open. Press + once to reach the sidechain settings.

    Check: The display shows OUT, then S.Ch after +.

    Do not turn X or Y yet.

  3. A1: make the kick the trigger

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Choose the kick as the trigger: hold A, press pad 1 (A1), then release.

    Check: MELODY plays on unchanged: nothing dips until you choose groups.

    Why

    Sidechain on the K.O. II reacts to notes, so every A1 note now starts a dip.

  4. Choose B and C to dip

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Do not add A, or the drums would dip under their own kick.

    Choose which groups dip: hold SHIFT and press B, then hold SHIFT and press C.

    Check: The drums play on undipped; X and Y set the dip next.

    Why

    The next step sets how much.

  5. Shape the dip with X and Y

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Turn X (the length of the dip) and Y (its shape) a little at a time.

    Check: The kick sits clearly on top, and the melody swells back between kicks.

    Why

    A short X gives a quick dip right under the kick; a long X gives a big, breathing pump. The guide’s pictures show LEN on the display for the length and ShP for the shape.

  6. Leave the settings and listen

    Press MAIN to leave the settings, then stop. Play BEAT and INTRO too, and compare them.

    Check: BEAT’s bass pumps too; INTRO, drums only, stays steady.

    Why

    Sidechain belongs to this project, so your other projects never pump. To soften it later, open sidechain again (SHIFT + FX, then +) and shorten X.

    Not working?

    Want B or C to stop dipping? The guide explains how to add sources and destinations but not how to remove them, and no owner report we found does; holding SHIFT and pressing B or C again is the likeliest way, so listen to check.

Checkpoint: the bass and melody dip on every kick and swell back between kicks.

If it doesn’t: for no pump at all, hold A and press pad 1 again to set the source, then hold SHIFT and press B, then C, for the destinations, and lengthen X. If the drums pump too, A was added as a destination. No sidechain page: it needs OS 2.0; update first.

Official guide: 11.9.1 sidechain · 11.9 output · what’s new: sidechain (OS 2.0)

Part 9 · Record your performance

Goal: capture your live song as an audio file on a phone or computer.

  1. Connect the K.O. II over USB-C

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Connect the K.O. II to your phone or computer with a USB-C cable, plus any adapter your phone needs.

    Check: The USB MIDI icon lights, and a new USB audio device appears.

    Why

    Since OS 2.5 it works as a USB audio device with “any class compliant USB audio host”.

  2. Choose it as your app’s input

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    In your recording app or DAW, choose the K.O. II as the input.

    Check: The app shows the K.O. II as its input.

    Not working?

    Look for a new USB audio device. The guide calls it “ko2”; owners’ tools find it by “EP-133” in its name on a Mac (ep-series-sysex) and “EP133” on Linux (sound-arranger). If the K.O. II restarts or shows BAT while connected, fit fresh AAA batteries; the guide asks for USB power of 5 V and at least 1 A.

  3. Set the recording level

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Start the song (hold MAIN, press ENTER, then SHIFT + PLAY) and watch your app’s level meter: the loudest parts should stay below the top.

    Check: The app’s meter peaks below the top.

    Not working?

    Too loud? Lower the input level in the app if it has one. Or, in MAIN, select the loudest group and lower it with the fader, which sets the selected group’s level.

  4. Record your performance

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Start recording in the app, then, in the song list, press SHIFT + PLAY on the K.O. II and perform.

    Check: The song plays from the start while the app records.

    Add punch-in FX, a latched repeat, scene changes if you play scenes by hand.

  5. Stop the recording and listen

    After the song stops, wait for the last sound to fade, then stop the app. Listen back on the phone or computer.

    Check: Your song plays back, with your live effects.

  6. No USB audio? Use the line output

    Connect a stereo 3.5 mm cable to the line input of an audio interface or recorder, and start with low levels.

    Check: The recorder’s level meter moves when the K.O. II plays.

    Either way your recording is audio only: back up the project itself with the official EP sample tool on a computer.

    Why

    No USB audio? Use the 3.5 mm output, a stereo headphone/line output.

  7. Your performance moves

    Read the eight moves below, then try one from memory without reading this app.

    • Record live: at 1.1.1 tap RECORD, press PLAY, play after the count-in, then press RECORD.
    • TIMING: X sets the grid, − quantize, + free time, Y swing.
    • KEYS: select a pad, press KEYS; hold it and turn X for the key, Y for the scale.
    • Note repeat: hold TIMING and a pad; in TIMING, SHIFT + the pad latches it.
    • Punch-in FX: while playing, hold FX and press a pad.
    • Copy a pad’s notes: hold it, SHIFT + C; hold another, SHIFT + D.
    • Next scene in time: hold MAIN, − / + before the change lands.
    • Sidechain: SHIFT + FX, +; hold a group button and press a pad for the source, SHIFT + a group to dip.

Checkpoint: a file on your phone or computer plays your song, including your live effects.

If it doesn’t: if the app records silence, choose the K.O. II as its input, not the built-in microphone. USB audio needs OS 2.5. Some USB-C cables only charge, so try another cable. On a phone, the recording app must accept an external USB input.
