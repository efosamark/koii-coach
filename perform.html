CHAPTER 08 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Play & perform

Until now you placed most notes one step at a time. In this chapter you play the K.O. II like an instrument. You record with feel, play a bassline in a scale and roll hi-hats. You also throw in effects, nudge notes by hand, switch scenes live, make the mix pump and record what you play. Each section is one short mini-project that starts from a practice copy of a scene you already made. Do the sections in any order: your finished scenes stay safe.

Written for OS 2.5.1. Every step here works on a K.O. II running OS 2.5.1. The official “what's new” page describes features up to OS 2.5 and has no separate 2.5.1 notes. This chapter was checked against it and the official guide on 13 October 2026. Older OS: update first. This app cannot see your hardware, so trust your display and your ears.

What you need

You need your K.O. II, headphones or speakers, and paper and a pencil for scene numbers. You also need the project from chapters 01 and 02: the scenes DRUMS, BEAT, INTRO, MELODY and BREAK, and the 10-position song list. No project yet? Do 01 / First beat and 02 / Make it a song first. For section 09 you also need a USB-C cable to a phone or computer, or a 3.5 mm stereo cable to a recorder.

Your project
Project 6 from chapters 01–02 (chapter 07 used project 7)
Starting settings
90 BPM, TIMING 16, quantize
Time
15–25 minutes per section
Order
Any. Sections 06 and 07 use your chapter 02 scenes and song
Safety. Work only in practice copies: every section starts by making one, and the ERASE moves here only touch those copies. Never use ERASE + SOUND: it deletes a sample from the device. Open system settings with SHIFT + ERASE only while the K.O. II is already switched on. Holding SHIFT + ERASE while you switch it on formats the drive, and the official guide warns that factory sounds “can not be recovered”. If this K.O. II holds work you care about, back it up first with the official EP sample tool on a computer. SHIFT + B undoes the last change while the umbrella icon is lit.

How every section starts

This chapter uses project 6, your chapter 01–02 project; chapter 07 worked in project 7. To get back, press MAIN, stop playback, hold MAIN, hold pad 6 as well, then release both (use your own pad if you chose another project in chapter 01). On OS 2.0+ a quick MAIN + number can select a scene instead, so press PLAY and check that you hear your own beat. Then stop.

Stop playback. Hold MAIN, use − / + until the display shows the number of the scene the section names (usually BEAT), and release. Press SHIFT + MAIN once: commit copies the scene and moves you into the copy. Hold MAIN, read the new number and write it down with the name the section gives it. The original scene stays as it was.

To change a system setting: press SHIFT + ERASE, type the three-digit code on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN to return. The table at the end lists every code this chapter uses and its default.

01 / Record live with feel

Goal: replace your placed snare with one you play yourself, including soft “ghost notes”. Along the way you set up count-in, metronome, velocity, quantize and undo.

  1. In project 6, make a practice copy of BEAT: stopped, hold MAIN, select BEAT with − / +, release, then press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number as LIVE. Press PLAY: you should hear the same drums and bass as BEAT. Press PLAY to stop.
  2. Turn on pad velocity, so soft taps play quietly: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 3, 0, 1 on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN. Tap A, then tap A2 very softly and then firmly. You should hear a quiet snare, then a loud one. 301 is velocity “high”, for playing “with a soft touch”. If you hit hard and everything still sounds loud, use 302 (“for vigorous play styles”). 300 switches velocity off again; that is the default.
  3. Hear a click while you practise: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 0, 0, press ENTER, then press MAIN. Press PLAY: a metronome click now plays with the beat. Press TEMPO once and turn Y until the click is clear but not harsh. Tapping TEMPO repeatedly sets a tap tempo, and X changes the BPM, so leave both alone. Press MAIN, then PLAY to stop. The default, 401, clicks only while recording; 402 clicks only during the count-in.
  4. Remove the placed snare in LIVE: stopped, in MAIN with group A selected, hold ERASE + A2 until TRK blinks on the display, then release. Press PLAY: kick, hats and bass, but no snare. Stop. If anything else disappeared, press SHIFT + B straight away.
  5. Press TIMING. Check that the display shows 16 (turn X if not), press − to choose quantize, then press MAIN. Quantize snaps every hit you record to the nearest of the 16 steps in the bar.
  6. Press PLAY and, without touching RECORD, tap A2 on beats 2 and 4 (count “one, TWO, three, FOUR”). Then add one or two very soft taps just before beat 4. These quiet in-between hits are called ghost notes. Practise until it feels steady, then stop. Nothing is recorded yet.
  7. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY. After the four count-in clicks, play your snare part for one bar. Then stop tapping and press RECORD: recording stops, the beat keeps playing. You should hear your backbeat and the soft ghost notes repeat by themselves, on the grid. Press PLAY to stop.
  8. Not happy? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again. To remove hits while the beat plays, hold ERASE and A2 while they sound; the guide calls this playback erase. Is one ghost note too loud? Stopped, go to its position, hold SHIFT and turn X. This changes the velocity of every note on that step, so use it only where no hat or kick shares the step.
  9. Now add loose ghost notes in free time: press TIMING, press + (free time), then MAIN. Press PLAY, hold RECORD, tap two or three soft A2 notes during one bar, then release RECORD. Holding RECORD while the beat plays records on top of what is there (an overdub). Listen: free-time notes keep your exact timing, slightly ahead of or behind the grid. Sounds sloppy? Press SHIFT + B. Stop.
  10. Optional: give the hats some swing. Swing only shapes notes you record after setting it (the guide: “note interval and swing apply before recording and not after”), and only with the 1/8 and 1/16 intervals. Press TIMING, press − (quantize), check the display shows 16, turn Y a little and press MAIN; the SWING icon lights (official icon map: “lit when swing has been enabled”). Stopped, with group A selected, hold ERASE + A3 until TRK blinks to clear the hats in LIVE only. Tap and release RECORD, press PLAY, and after the count-in tap A3 on every “and” and every beat (eight even taps) for one bar, then press RECORD. You should hear the hats lean into a shuffle. Hold MAIN and select BEAT to compare: its hats stay straight. Too much or too little? Press SHIFT + B, change Y and record again.
  11. Set things back: press TIMING, press − (quantize), turn Y back to no swing (the SWING icon goes out), then MAIN; later chapters expect quantize and no swing. Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 0, 1, press ENTER, then MAIN to return the metronome to its default. Keep velocity on (301) if you like it, or type 300 the same way to switch it off. With velocity on, notes you write with held RECORD + pad may also come out quieter if you press softly.

Checkpoint: LIVE plays your own snare backbeat with a few soft ghost notes, and BEAT still plays the snare you placed. If it doesn't: no count-in means you pressed RECORD and PLAY together, which starts without one: tap RECORD, release it, then press PLAY. Every hit equally loud: the velocity code was not confirmed with ENTER. Hits land off the grid: you are still in free time, so press TIMING, then −. Nothing was kept: RECORD was not armed before you pressed PLAY.

02 / Play a bassline in a scale with KEYS

Goal: turn one bass sound into a 12-note keyboard, choose a key and scale so every pad fits, and record a two-bar bassline live.

  1. In project 6, make a practice copy of DRUMS, your drums-only scene: stopped, hold MAIN, select DRUMS with − / +, release, then press SHIFT + MAIN. Write the new number as BASSLINE. Tap B and press PLAY: drums only, no bass. Stop. No DRUMS scene? Copy BEAT instead. Then, stopped with B selected, hold ERASE + B1 until TRK blinks to clear its bass notes.
  2. Stopped in MAIN with group B selected, hold RECORD, press + once and release both. The display should show B's pattern length change from 1 to 2. A stays one bar long and simply repeats underneath.
  3. Tap B1 to select it; you hear your chapter 01 bass. Press KEYS, then tap all twelve pads. Each pad now plays B1's sound at a different pitch. If the pads still play different sounds, KEYS is not on: tap B1 and press KEYS again.
  4. For a deeper bass, hold KEYS and press − once: every pad drops an octave. Too boomy on small speakers? Hold KEYS and press + to go back up.
  5. Hold KEYS and slowly turn X: the display shows the key, the note the scale is built on. Over drums alone any key works, so pick one. Still holding KEYS, turn Y to choose the scale. OS 2.5 added BLU (blues), H.MI (harmonic minor) and M.MI (melodic minor); choose BLU for this bassline. Release KEYS and tap the pads: they now offer only notes from that scale, so a “wrong” note is hard to find. OS 2.5 saves the key and scale with the project.
  6. Press PLAY. Count “1, 2, 3, 4” for two bars. Play the lowest-sounding pad on beat 1 of each bar, and two or three other pads anywhere else. Repeat the same two-bar line until you can play it twice in a row. Stop.
  7. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap RECORD, press PLAY, and after the count-in play your two-bar line once. Then press RECORD. You should hear your bassline repeat every two bars over the drums. Press PLAY to stop. Messy? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again.
  8. Press KEYS again to switch KEYS off. Tap A, then A1 and A2: you should hear kick and snare, not one sound at different pitches. The official guide shows how to switch KEYS on but not how to switch it off. Check the display: the KEYS MODE icon (official icon map: “all 12 pads in ‘chromatic’ mode”) should now be off. If it is still lit, or the pads still play one sound at many pitches, check the guide's KEYS section. Finally, hold MAIN and select BEAT: it still has its two original bass pulses.

Checkpoint: BASSLINE plays the drums plus your own two-bar bassline, and every note fits the scale. If it doesn't: the bass repeats after one bar because B is still one bar long: repeat the two-bars step with B selected. Turning X or Y changed the volume or pitch instead: you let go of KEYS; keep it held while you turn. Key and scale selection with X and Y needs OS 2.5; older OS: update first.

Official guide: 9.3 keys · 12.2 change the scale in keys mode · what's new: keys mode (OS 2.5)

03 / Note repeat, hat rolls and the arpeggio

Goal: let the K.O. II repeat a pad for you. You finger-drum the hi-hats and a roll, latch a repeat so it plays hands-free, and arpeggiate notes in KEYS.

  1. In project 6, make a practice copy of BEAT and write it as ROLL. Tap A. Stopped, hold ERASE + A3 until TRK blinks to clear the hats. Press PLAY: kick, snare and bass only. Stop.
  2. Press TIMING, turn X until the display shows 8 (eight steps per bar, not 8T), press − for quantize, then press MAIN. Press PLAY. Hold TIMING, then press and hold A3: the hat repeats by itself in even eighth notes. Release A3 and it stops. With velocity on (section 01), pressing harder makes the repeats louder. Stop.
  3. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1. Tap RECORD, press PLAY. After the count-in, hold TIMING + A3 for exactly one bar, let go, then press RECORD. You should hear steady eighth-note hats again, this time played by you. Stop. Uneven? Press SHIFT + B and try again.
  4. Now add a roll into each loop: press TIMING, turn X to 32, then press MAIN. Tap RECORD and press PLAY. After the count-in, count the bar, and on beat 4 hold TIMING + A3 for just that one beat. Let go before the next “one”, then press RECORD. You should hear a fast “trrrr” leading into every new loop. Stop. Press TIMING, turn X back to 16, then press MAIN.
  5. Hands-free hats: press PLAY. Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 4: one hat per beat (1/1, 1/2 and 1/4 arrived in OS 2.0). While still in TIMING, hold SHIFT and tap A3: the hat keeps repeating after you let go. Play A1 and A2 on top for fun; RECORD is off, so nothing is written. To stop it, hold SHIFT and tap A3 again while in TIMING. Stop playback, press TIMING, turn X back to 16 and press MAIN.
  6. The arpeggio (OS 2.5) only works when the sound's play mode is oneshot or legato. Tap C, press SOUND and tap C2, an unused pad. Hold SOUND, type 5, 1, 0 and release, or browse 500–599 with − / + for a short melodic sound. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit. Use − / + to find the page where X changes the play mode; the guide calls it the sound page, with X for play mode and Y for pan. Turn X until the display shows oneshot or legato, not key (the OS 2.5 notes write legato as LEG). Press MAIN. This changes C2 only.
  7. Tap C2 so it is selected, then press KEYS. Press PLAY. Press TIMING and turn X to 16; the TIMING interval sets the arpeggio's speed. Now hold TIMING and press and hold three pads, one after another. You should hear the notes play one at a time, in the order you pressed them. Let go to stop. Try 8 for a slower arpeggio, then stop playback.
  8. Clean up: press TIMING, set X to 16, press − (quantize), then press MAIN. Press KEYS again to switch it off, then tap C1: it should play your chapter 02 melody sound at its normal pitch.

Checkpoint: ROLL plays hats you finger-drummed, with a roll into every loop. You can latch a repeat and stop it, and held KEYS notes arpeggiate. If it doesn't: the pad plays once instead of repeating, so hold TIMING first, then the pad. The arpeggio plays all notes together or only one: C2's play mode is still key, so set oneshot or legato. A latch that won't stop needs the same move again: in TIMING, hold SHIFT and tap the same pad. The arpeggio needs OS 2.5; latching and the 1, 2 and 4 intervals need OS 2.0. Older OS: update first.

Official guide: 10.4.1 note repeat and arpeggio · 10.4 timing · 8.2.1 sound (play mode) · what's new: arpeggio (OS 2.5), larger intervals and latch (OS 2.0)

04 / Punch-in FX and fader moves

Goal: perform effects on the running beat with punch-in FX. Then record a fader move into the pattern, and erase only that move.

  1. In project 6, make a practice copy of BEAT and write it as FXJAM. Press PLAY and leave it running.
  2. Near the end of a loop, hold FX and press pad 1 (the pad marked 1, where A1 sits) for one beat. Let go on the next “one”. The OS 2.5 notes call FX + 1 beat repeat: you should hear the beat stutter, then carry on normally when you let go. Use only the 12 pads here. FX + a group button (A–D) solos that group instead.
  3. Still playing, hold FX and try the other pads one at a time, for a beat or two each. Punch-in FX respond to how hard you press, and you can combine them by holding two pads. The guide does not list what every pad does, so use your ears: write down your two favourite pad numbers. Stop.
  4. Stopped, tap A (drums). Hold FADER and read the labels printed above the pads: they name the fader functions. The OS 2.5 notes name level, pitch and pan among them. Still holding FADER, press the pad labelled for pitch, then release. Press PLAY and move the fader slowly: the whole drum group bends up and down. Find the fader position where the drums sound normal again, then press PLAY to stop.
  5. Press PLAY. At the start of a loop, hold RECORD and slide the fader down and back up during one bar, then release RECORD. Keep your fingers off the pads while RECORD is held, or they record notes too. You should hear your move repeat by itself every loop. Stop. Now hold FADER: the pitch pad blinks, because functions with recorded moves blink.
  6. Stopped, hold ERASE + FADER and press the pitch pad. This OS 2.5 move erases the recorded moves of that one fader function only. Press PLAY: the drums no longer bend. Stop. To erase every fader move in this group and pattern at once: stopped, hold ERASE + FADER for 2 seconds, until FDR blinks and then DEL shows.
  7. Stopped, press SHIFT + FADER. RES shows, and every fader function of this group returns to its default position; pressing it again undoes the reset (SET shows). OS 2.5 does not allow this during playback. Then hold FADER and press the pad labelled for level, so the fader controls group volume again, as the rest of the course expects.

Checkpoint: FX + 1 makes the beat stutter. Your recorded pitch move replayed by itself, and you erased it without touching any notes. If it doesn't: punch-in FX do nothing while stopped, so press PLAY first. The fader move was not kept: hold RECORD the whole time the fader moves, while the beat plays. The drums still sound detuned after erasing: press SHIFT + FADER while stopped. Per-function erase needs OS 2.5; older OS: update first.

Official guide: 11.7 punch-in FX · 9.4 fader · 9.6 erase / undo · what's new: sequencer and performance (OS 2.5)

05 / Edit by pad: copy, paste and shift

Goal: layer your kick with a second sound by copying one pad's notes to another pad. Then shift that layer, and one snare, by a few ticks to change the feel.

  1. In project 6, make a practice copy of BEAT and write it as EDIT. Tap A.
  2. Press SOUND and tap A4, which chapters 01–02 leave unused. If A4 holds a sound you use, pick another free pad and use it instead of A4 below. Hold SOUND, type 3, 0, 0 and release, or browse 300–399 with − / + for a short click, rim or clap. Turn X so it is quieter than the kick. Press MAIN.
  3. Stopped in MAIN: hold A1, press SHIFT + C, and release everything. This copies A1's notes only (OS 2.5). Now hold A4, press SHIFT + D, and release. Go to 1.2.3 with − / +: A4 lights there, just like A1. Press PLAY: every kick now has the percussion stacked on top. Stop. Without a pad held, SHIFT + C copies a whole bar or pattern instead.
  4. Shift the whole layer a little later. With playback stopped, press SHIFT + TIMING to open timing correct. Hold A4 and, still holding it, press + six times, then release. Each press moves every A4 note one tick later. A step is 24 ticks, so six ticks are a quarter step, about 40 ms at 90 BPM. Press MAIN, then PLAY: the layer now lands just after the kick, a thicker double hit called a flam. Stop. Careful: in timing correct, a plain tap on a pad while stopped quantizes all its notes back onto the grid. Holding a pad while the beat plays quantizes its notes as they pass, so keep playback stopped.
  5. Make one snare lazy: press TIMING, press + (free time), then MAIN. Stopped, go to 1.4.1. Hold SHIFT and A2, keep both held, press + six times, then release. Hold A2 before you press +, because SHIFT + + on its own jumps a whole bar. In free time each press nudges the note by one tick. Press PLAY: the snare on beat 4 drags slightly behind, while the beat 2 snare stays put. Stop. In quantize mode the same move jumps a whole step, which is a quick way to move a note to the next step.
  6. Press TIMING, press − (quantize), then MAIN. Compare: hold MAIN and switch between EDIT and BEAT while playing. EDIT should sound thicker and slightly laid-back. Stop.

Checkpoint: EDIT has a percussion layer that follows the kick rhythm with a slight flam, plus a late snare on beat 4. BEAT is unchanged. If it doesn't: if the paste did nothing or pasted a whole bar, keep the pad held while you press SHIFT + C or SHIFT + D. The snare jumped a whole step because you were in quantize: press SHIFT + B, switch to free time and nudge again. The layer snapped back onto the grid because you tapped A4 in timing correct: hold it and press + again. Per-pad copy and the one-tick shift need OS 2.5; older OS: update first.

Official guide: 9.4 per-pad pattern copy and paste · 9.7 offset notes · 10.4.2 timing correct · what's new: per-pad copy and time shift (OS 2.5)

06 / Scenes as a live performance

Goal: perform your chapter 02 sections live, with every scene change landing on time, and learn to clear a scene safely.

  1. In project 6, stop playback. Press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 1, 2, press ENTER, then press MAIN. Now a scene you pick while playing waits for the end of the current pattern (412). 411 waits for the end of the bar instead; 410, the default, changes immediately.
  2. On paper, write a running order using your chapter 02 scene numbers: INTRO 4 passes, BEAT 4, MELODY 2, BREAK 2, MELODY 2, BEAT 2. INTRO and BEAT are one bar long; MELODY and BREAK are two, so one pass of them lasts two bars.
  3. Hold MAIN, select INTRO, release and press PLAY. Count the passes. During the last pass, hold MAIN, press − / + until the display shows the next scene's number, and release. The change should happen exactly at the end of the pattern, even though you chose it early. Work through your running order, then press PLAY to stop.
  4. Try a direct jump while stopped: hold MAIN and type a scene number on the pads, then press PLAY to check. The official guide says MAIN + numpad “can now always switch to any scene 1-99” (OS 2.0+). It also says to hold MAIN and press pad 1–9 to change project, without explaining how the two are told apart. If the music is suddenly not yours, you changed project. Hold MAIN and press your project's pad (6 if you followed chapter 01) to go back, and switch scenes with MAIN + − / + when performing.
  5. Perform your running order from start to finish, without stopping. Add a punch-in FX (section 04) in the last beat before a change, or a latched hat repeat (section 03) during BREAK. Stop at the end.
  6. Clear a throwaway scene. Stopped, press SHIFT + MAIN to copy the scene you are in, then hold MAIN and write the new number as TRASH. Hold ERASE + MAIN for 2 seconds: CLR shows, because a scene with notes is cleared, not deleted. Hold ERASE + MAIN for 2 seconds again. The scene is now empty, so if it is not in the song list, DEL blinks and it is deleted. Hold MAIN to see which scene you are in now. Then select the scene you copied and press PLAY: it should be unchanged. Only ever do this on a scene whose number you have just checked.
  7. Optional, for a gig: switch the K.O. II off, then hold MAIN while you switch it on. LOK shows for about a second. In lock mode “any changes made on the device will reset when the device is restarted”, so you can perform without changing your project. Switch off and on again without holding anything before you build anything you want to keep, and check that LOK does not appear. Hold only MAIN: SHIFT + ERASE at power-on formats the drive.
  8. Keep 412 if you like performing this way, or press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 1, 0, press ENTER, then MAIN to go back to instant scene changes.

Checkpoint: every scene change landed on a pattern boundary, and TRASH showed CLR and then DEL. If it doesn't: changes still happen instantly when the code was not confirmed with ENTER; enter it again. Nothing changed: you released MAIN on the same number, so watch the display while you press − / +. DEL never appears: the scene is in the song list, which is fine; leave it.

07 / Song mode, deeper

Goal: lengthen the breakdown in your chapter 02 song by giving its scene a longer pattern. Then rehearse with song loop and move a position. A song can hold up to 99 positions.

  1. In project 6, stop playback. Hold MAIN, select BREAK, release, then press SHIFT + MAIN. Write the new number as BREAK2. Press PLAY: melody, snare and hats, two bars long. Stop.
  2. In BREAK2, tap C. Stopped, tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + once, then release; tap RECORD again if it is still armed (the RECORD icon “blinks when armed to record”, says the official icon map). This doubles C's pattern to four bars and copies its notes into the new bars. Press PLAY: the melody plays twice before the scene repeats. Stop.
  3. Hold MAIN and press ENTER to open the song list. Use − / + to go to position 7, the first BREAK in the chapter 02 song map. Hold SHIFT and press − / + until position 7 holds BREAK2's number.
  4. Press SHIFT + PLAY to play from the start. The breakdown at position 7 now lasts four bars, because a song position lasts as long as the longest pattern in its scene. Position 10 still uses BREAK, so the ending stays short. The song is now 18 bars, about 48 seconds at 90 BPM, and it stops by itself at the end.
  5. For rehearsing, make the song repeat: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 1, press ENTER, then MAIN. Open the song list again (hold MAIN, press ENTER) and press SHIFT + PLAY: after position 10 the song starts again. Practise punch-in FX at the section changes. Press PLAY to stop; the song goes back to the position you started from. Then press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 0, press ENTER and MAIN, so the song stops at the end again.
  6. Optional: move a position. In the song list, go to position 10, hold SHIFT and press C: the position is cut to the clipboard and the list has 9 positions. Go to position 1, hold SHIFT and press D to insert it. Press SHIFT + PLAY and listen for where the BREAK landed; the guide only says it is inserted “into the selected song position”. Prefer the old order? Go to the moved BREAK and cut it again with SHIFT + C. Then go to the last position, add one with SHIFT + A and set it to BREAK with SHIFT + − / +.

Checkpoint: the song plays INTRO, BEAT, MELODY, a four-bar BREAK2, MELODY and a short BREAK, then stops. If it doesn't: a two-bar breakdown means position 7 still holds BREAK, or C in BREAK2 is still two bars long. The song never stops: song loop is still on, so set 420. The song list doesn't open: song mode needs OS 2.0; update first. No chapter 02 song yet? Build it in 02 / Make it a song first.

Official guide: 8.3.1 song mode · 6.2 song mode (position length) · 9.1 duplicate pattern length · system codes 420–421 · what's new: song mode (OS 2.0, 2.0.1)

08 / Make the mix pump with sidechain

Goal: make the bass and melody dip each time the kick hits, so the kick punches through and the groove “breathes” (OS 2.0+). Sidechain sits in the output settings, not in a scene, so expect it to affect every scene in this project.

  1. In project 6, hold MAIN, select MELODY (drums, bass and melody), release and press PLAY. Leave it running for the whole section.
  2. Press SHIFT + FX: the output settings (the master compressor) open. Press + once to reach the sidechain settings. Do not turn X or Y yet.
  3. Choose the kick as the trigger: hold A, press pad 1 (A1), then release. Sidechain on the K.O. II reacts to notes, so every A1 note now starts a dip.
  4. Choose which groups dip: hold SHIFT and press B, then hold SHIFT and press C. Do not add A, or the drums would dip under their own kick. You may already hear the bass and melody dip on every kick; the next step sets how much.
  5. Turn X (the length of the dip) and Y (its shape) a little at a time. A short X gives a quick dip right under the kick; a long X gives a big, breathing pump. Aim for this: the kick sits clearly on top, and the melody swells back between kicks.
  6. Press MAIN to leave the settings, then stop. Play BEAT and INTRO too, and listen for the pump there. To soften it later, open sidechain again (SHIFT + FX, then +) and shorten X. The guide explains how to add sources and destinations but not how to remove them. Holding SHIFT and pressing B or C again may remove a destination, so listen to check.

Checkpoint: the bass and melody dip on every kick and swell back between kicks. If it doesn't: for no pump at all, hold A and press pad 1 again to set the source, then hold SHIFT and press B, then C, for the destinations, and lengthen X. If the drums pump too, A was added as a destination. No sidechain page: it needs OS 2.0; update first.

Official guide: 11.9.1 sidechain · 11.9 output · what's new: sidechain (OS 2.0)

09 / Record your performance

Goal: capture your live song as an audio file on a phone or computer.

  1. Connect the K.O. II to your phone or computer with a USB-C cable, plus any adapter your phone needs. Since OS 2.5 it works as a USB audio device with “any class compliant USB audio host”. In your recording app or DAW, choose the K.O. II as the input. The guide does not say what name it shows, so look for a new USB audio device. If the K.O. II restarts or shows BAT while connected, fit fresh AAA batteries; the guide asks for USB power of 5 V and at least 1 A.
  2. Start the song (hold MAIN, press ENTER, then SHIFT + PLAY) and watch your app's level meter: the loudest parts should stay below the top. Too loud? Lower the input level in the app if it has one. Or, in MAIN, select the loudest group and lower it with the fader, which sets the selected group's level.
  3. Start recording in the app, then, in the song list, press SHIFT + PLAY on the K.O. II and perform: punch-in FX, a latched repeat, scene changes if you play scenes by hand. After the song stops, wait for the last sound to fade, then stop the app. Listen back on the phone or computer.
  4. No USB audio? Use the 3.5 mm output, a stereo headphone/line output. Connect a stereo 3.5 mm cable to the line input of an audio interface or recorder, and start with low levels. Either way your recording is audio only: back up the project itself with the official EP sample tool on a computer.

Checkpoint: a file on your phone or computer plays your song, including your live effects. If it doesn't: if the app records silence, choose the K.O. II as its input, not the built-in microphone. USB audio needs OS 2.5. Some USB-C cables only charge, so try another cable. On a phone, the recording app must accept an external USB input.
