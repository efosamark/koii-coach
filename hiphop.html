K.O. II / COACH · CHAPTER 07

Vinyl to a full hip-hop beat

Your small beat becomes a 64-bar old-school hip-hop beat: a few words sung by the artist, steady drums, optional bass, a stripped-down break and a repeated-word twist in the final return. At 90 BPM in 4/4, the plan lasts about 2 minutes 51 seconds, before any audio tail.

Do one numbered action at a time. Stop playback before editing notes. This guide assumes your first-beat recipe: A1 kick, A2 snare, A3 closed hat; B1 bass at 1.1.1 and 1.3.1. Use a disposable working copy. Do not erase valuable work. The app cannot see your hardware.

What you need

Your K.O. II, your Audio-Technica LP60X-family turntable, a record, a 3.5 mm stereo AUX cable, and headphones or speakers at a comfortable level. For the automatic arrangement you need K.O. II OS 2.0 or newer. The manual-performance alternative is included.

If the rear connector on your exact turntable differs, match that connector with a suitable stereo cable or adapter rather than forcing a plug. Check the label/manual. No Bluetooth pairing is needed.

0 / 50 steps checked

01 / Protect the beat and prepare two-bar patterns
  1. Press MAIN. If playback is running, press PLAY to stop it. Hold MAIN and use − / + to select the scene containing your finished first beat. Listen briefly, then stop. Write its scene number on paper as ORIGINAL.
  2. Press SHIFT + MAIN once. Write the newly selected scene number as WORK. This duplicates the scene for development, not an external backup. Check that ORIGINAL still plays correctly before changing WORK.
  3. In WORK, select group A. Check its pattern length. If it is one bar, tap RECORD to arm, then hold SHIFT and press + once to duplicate it to two bars. Release the buttons and tap RECORD to disarm if still armed. If already two bars, leave it alone. Check the display and listen: the same drums should play in both bars.
  4. Repeat that length check and duplication for group B. It must be two bars, with the two bass pulses copied into bar 2. Stop again. If either A or B is longer than two bars, do not shorten valuable notes: use a disposable one-bar foundation from chapter 01 instead.
  5. Select C, press MAIN, then SHIFT + A to choose the next empty pattern for this group. While stopped, tap RECORD to arm and use + to extend this empty one-bar pattern to two bars; disarm RECORD. Repeat for D. Press TEMPO and turn X to 90. Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 16: not 1 and not 16T. Press minus for quantized entry, then MAIN. Check that all four groups are two bars. Keep swing neutral for the first pass.
02 / Connect the turntable and choose the artist vocal
  1. Stop the turntable. Set its rear PHONO/LINE switch to LINE. On the LP60X-family setup, this uses the built-in phono preamp. Start with your listening volume low.
  2. Connect the turntable's Analog Out to the K.O. II's audio INPUT using the stereo AUX cable. Do not use the K.O. II OUTPUT, MIDI or SYNC socket. Connect headphones or speakers to the K.O. II output as appropriate.
  3. Play your record and listen for the artist singing TWO TO FOUR WORDS you like. Choose a compact phrase about one to two seconds long, ideally where the backing music is quiet. This is your MAIN PHRASE, not a piano or horn chord. Recording from vinyl captures the backing music too; the sampler does not automatically isolate the singer.
  4. Replay those words and remember where they begin. Write the approximate location as MAIN PHRASE. Pick one short word or syllable from that phrase as your ANSWER, ideally half a second or less. You will record the same passage a second time, so you do not need to hunt for another song. Do not aim for a perfect sample yet.
  5. Select group C on the K.O. II and press SAMPLE. Use − / + to select line input, not the microphone. Choose mono line input for this first attempt. Turn X up gradually while the record plays; keep the signal clean, not distorted. Turn Y threshold low enough that the sound can start capture. If silence persists, check LINE, the cable and the selected input.
03 / Record the sung phrase and trim the words
  1. Select C and press SAMPLE. Check line input again. Replay the MAIN PHRASE on the turntable. Just BEFORE the artist begins the first word, hold unused C1; keep holding through the words, then release just AFTER the last word. Press MAIN and tap C1. You should hear the artist singing your phrase. A little extra audio before and after is fine: we trim it next. Use a disposable pad assignment, not a sound you need to keep.
  2. Replay the SAME passage. Select C, press SAMPLE, and check line input. Hold unused C2 around your chosen ANSWER word, then release after it. Press MAIN and tap C2. It is fine if you captured more than one word: the next steps cut playback down to the word you want. C1 is the phrase; C2 is the short answer.
  3. Press SOUND and tap C1 to select it. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, then use minus or plus to find trim. Turn X a little at a time and tap C1 between changes. Stop when playback starts just before the first consonant or syllable of the first word. Do not cut off the start of the word. This removes the unwanted lead-in without trying to remove the backing music.
  4. Stay on trim. Turn Y gradually until playback ends just after the LAST word. Keep all the words intelligible: do not shorten the phrase into a tiny stab. At 90 BPM one bar lasts about 2.67 seconds; choose a phrase shorter than that for this first recipe. Then select C2 and repeat X start/Y length trimming around just the answer word, ideally half a second or less. Do not hold SHIFT + SOUND on trim: it permanently crops the audio file.
  5. For C1: press SOUND, select C1, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND and use minus or plus to find the sound play-mode page. Turn X to oneshot. Repeat for C2. In oneshot, a tap plays the trimmed phrase without you holding the pad. Return to ordinary SOUND and use X amplitude so the words are clear but not louder than everything else. Leave Y pitch unchanged to keep the voice natural, then MAIN.
04 / Put the phrase and answer into your beat
  1. Stop playback, press MAIN and select C. If you already followed the old chord recipe in this working copy, first briefly erase C1 at 1.1.1 and 2.1.1, and C2 at 1.2.3 and 2.3.1. Navigate to each position before using ERASE plus its pad. Otherwise leave the empty pattern alone. Now navigate to 1.1.1, hold RECORD, tap C1 once and release. This writes the FULL sung phrase at the start of bar 1.
  2. Press PLAY and listen to one two-bar pass, then stop. The artist should sing the full phrase once, starting near the beginning of bar 1, with room afterwards. If the first word starts noticeably late, select C1 and shorten only its silent lead-in on trim X. Quantization places the trigger on the grid; it does not automatically retime every word inside the recording.
  3. Return to stopped MAIN and select C. Navigate to 2.3.1: bar 2, beat 3, first subdivision. Hold RECORD, tap C2 once and release. This places the short ANSWER word later in bar 2, after the full phrase has finished. Simply tapping without RECORD only auditions it.
  4. Leave the other C positions EMPTY. Your entire two-bar vocal pattern is C1 at 1.1.1, then C2 at 2.3.1. Count: full phrase in bar 1, space, short answer in bar 2. Do not add the old chord notes at 1.2.3 or 2.1.1. If the phrase still overlaps the answer, choose a shorter phrase rather than cutting a word in half.
  5. Press PLAY for two complete two-bar passes, then stop. You should hear your drums underneath the artist singing the phrase and the answer. If the sampled backing rhythm fights the drums, choose a more exposed or rhythmically simpler vocal passage; it will not automatically match 90 BPM. For a wrong trigger, in stopped MAIN navigate to it and briefly use ERASE plus its pad. Never ERASE + SOUND to fix note timing.
05 / Make the bass support the artist voice
  1. Select B, press MAIN and check the bass pulses at 1.1.1, 1.3.1, 2.1.1 and 2.3.1. If a position is genuinely empty, navigate there and write B1 with held RECORD + pad 1. Do not add a second hit over a note already present.
  2. Play the loop and listen to bass plus the sung words. If the bass clashes with the singing, stop, press SOUND, select B1 and change its Y pitch a little, then play again. Keep the voice C1 and C2 at its original pitch for now; tune the bass to support the vocal rather than changing the singer to fit the bass.
  3. If no bass pitch fits the voice, in SOUND with B1 selected try another short bass from 400-499 using minus or plus. Custom libraries may differ. If it still clashes, in this disposable working scene briefly erase B1 at 1.1.1, 1.3.1, 2.1.1 and 2.3.1. Drums plus a good vocal sample can work without added bass. Check every scene if you later change the shared pad sound.
  4. Balance the parts in SOUND: select A3 and reduce X if the hats mask the words; select C1 and C2 and set X so you can understand the phrase and answer; select B1 and keep it low enough that the kick stays distinct. Make one small adjustment, listen, then stop. Do not turn X in MAIN expecting pad volume.
  5. Write the current WORK scene number as HOOK. Play it and confirm drums, optional bass, C1 full vocal phrase at 1.1.1 and C2 answer at 2.3.1 repeat across two bars. Stop. Press SHIFT + MAIN to duplicate HOOK before making the next section. Write the new scene number as MAIN.
06 / Create main groove and intro scenes
  1. In the new MAIN scene, select C and stopped MAIN. Navigate to 1.1.1 and briefly erase C1 with ERASE plus pad 1. Navigate to 2.3.1 and briefly erase C2 with ERASE plus pad 2. These are the only TWO vocal triggers in the new recipe. Keep the audio sample files; do not use ERASE + SOUND.
  2. Press PLAY. MAIN should have drums and optional bass but no artist vocal. Stop. Hold MAIN with minus or plus to reselect HOOK and confirm its phrase and answer are still there. Return to MAIN. If the earlier section changed too, stop and undo if available before continuing; do not assume copies are independent without checking.
  3. With MAIN selected, press SHIFT + MAIN once to duplicate it. Write the newly selected scene number as INTRO. Select B and briefly erase B1 at 1.1.1, 1.3.1, 2.1.1 and 2.3.1. INTRO should now be drums only.
  4. Play INTRO for two repeats. Stop. Check MAIN still has bass and HOOK still has vocal hook. Write all three scene numbers clearly. The sound settings may be shared within the project, so make section changes with note events, not pad amplitude.
  5. With INTRO selected, press SHIFT + MAIN once. Write the new scene number as BREAK. This is your disposable copy for the stripped-down section.
07 / Make a breakdown with breathing room
  1. In BREAK select A and stopped MAIN. Briefly erase the snare A2 at 1.2.1, 1.4.1, 2.2.1 and 2.4.1. Navigate to each position first; do not hold ERASE long enough to clear a whole track accidentally.
  2. Keep the hat only on beats 1 and 3 in each bar. Briefly erase A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.3, 1.4.1 and 1.4.3. Repeat those six positions starting with 2 instead of 1. Remaining hats: 1.1.1, 1.3.1, 2.1.1, 2.3.1.
  3. Play BREAK. You should hear the original kick rhythm with sparse hats, no snare, bass or artist vocal. Stop. If this feels too empty, that is intentional: the full return will feel larger.
  4. Hold MAIN and use − / + to select HOOK. Check the full beat once more. Stop and press SHIFT + MAIN to duplicate HOOK. Write the newly selected scene number as RETURN.
  5. Check RETURN still has the original full drums, bass and C vocal, and D has no notes yet. This is the only scene that will get the surprise sound. All scenes must still have a longest pattern of exactly two bars.
08 / Add the twist: repeat a word from the artist
  1. Stop playback. Keep the turntable connected and select D, then SAMPLE. Use minus or plus to choose LINE input, not the microphone. Replay the same artist passage and find a very short word or syllable for a repeated-word effect in RETURN. You are sampling the artist again, not recording your own voice.
  2. In SAMPLE with D selected and line input checked, replay that word. Hold unused D1 just before it, then release after it. Press MAIN and tap D1. It should be the artist voice again. If you captured extra words, keep going: the next step trims playback to one short fragment.
  3. Press SOUND and select D1. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, find trim with minus or plus, then adjust X start and Y length to keep one short word or syllable, ideally about 0.2 seconds. Keep it recognizable and remove silent lead-in. On the sound play-mode page turn X to oneshot. Return to ordinary SOUND and lower X amplitude so this is a little detail.
  4. In RETURN select D and stopped MAIN. Navigate to 2.4.1, hold RECORD and tap D1 once, then release. Repeat at 2.4.3. Play two passes: the artist fragment should go word-word near the end of bar 2. If the hits cut each other off, shorten the fragment gently or briefly erase the second trigger at 2.4.3. Stop before editing.
  5. Stop and verify HOOK has no D1 repeated words but RETURN does. Select INTRO and duplicate it with SHIFT + MAIN; write the new number as END. In END briefly erase A1 at 1.2.3, 1.3.1, 1.4.3 and those same positions in bar 2, leaving only the first kick in each bar plus the hats. Check END, then stop.
09 / Build the full 2–3 minute arrangement
  1. Read the song map below and keep your actual scene numbers beside you. Every scene is TWO bars. A song position plays once for the longest group pattern, so four positions using INTRO make eight bars. Scene numbers and song-position numbers are different.
  2. On OS 2.0 or newer, hold MAIN then press ENTER to open the song-list editor. For safety this recipe needs an unused song list in your disposable project. If a valuable arrangement is already there, leave it alone and use the manual-performance option. The new list starts with one position. Hold SHIFT and use − / + to assign INTRO to position 1.
  3. Hold SHIFT and press A to add a position. Hold SHIFT with − / + to assign its scene. Repeat until positions 1–4 are INTRO, 5–12 MAIN, 13–20 HOOK, 21–24 BREAK, 25–30 RETURN and 31–32 END. For positions that need the same scene, check the assigned scene rather than assuming it was copied. Release SHIFT and use − / + to move between song positions.
  4. Review all 32 positions, then press SHIFT + PLAY to start the song from the beginning. Listen through and stop. At 90 BPM this 64-bar plan lasts about 2:51. If it ends much sooner, you have too few repeated positions or a scene shorter than two bars. If it lasts longer, check for a group pattern longer than two bars.
  5. Manual alternative: leave the song editor and select INTRO in MAIN. Press PLAY. Count two-bar passes: INTRO 4, MAIN 8, HOOK 8, BREAK 4, RETURN 6, END 2. Select each next scene with held MAIN and − / + around the section boundary, watching its number. Stop after END. Rehearse switches first; manual timing may not be perfectly seamless. This replaces steps 42–44 on older firmware.
10 / Finish, listen and preserve your work
  1. Play from the beginning at a comfortable volume. Listen for kick and snare, then optional bass, then whether the artist words are understandable. Stop and change only one pad amplitude in SOUND if needed. Shared pad sound changes may affect more than one scene, so listen through again.
  2. Optional: add a little space using the official effects guide linked below. Keep bass dry and avoid recording fader automation accidentally. This is not required: a clear dry beat is better than a blurred beat with too much effect.
  3. Listen to BREAK followed by RETURN. The sparse drums should make the full sung phrase, answer and little repeated-word detail feel bigger. If the D1 repetition is distracting, in RETURN briefly erase D1 at 2.4.1 AND 2.4.3. Keep the main C1 phrase and C2 answer. Do not change every part trying to fix one sound.
  4. Record the full performance using a suitable external audio recorder/interface connected to the K.O. II output, or follow the official USB-audio instructions if your installed firmware supports them. COMMIT duplicates scenes; it is not a finished-song export. The browser coach cannot record hardware audio.
  5. Write the project number and ORIGINAL, HOOK, MAIN, INTRO, BREAK, RETURN and END scene numbers on paper. Use supported official backup tools for device data. Your checklist is saved only in this browser. For public release of music containing someone else's record, check and obtain the necessary permissions; do not assume sampling automatically grants rights.
