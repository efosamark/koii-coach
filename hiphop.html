K.O. II / COACH · CHAPTER 07

Vinyl to a full hip-hop beat

Your small beat becomes a 64-bar old-school instrumental: warm vinyl chords, steady drums, simple bass, a stripped-down break and a surprise vocal in the final return. At 90 BPM in 4/4, the plan lasts about 2 minutes 51 seconds, before any audio tail.

Do one numbered action at a time. Stop playback before editing notes. This guide assumes your first-beat recipe: A1 kick, A2 snare, A3 closed hat; B1 bass at 1.1.1 and 1.3.1. Use a disposable working copy. Do not erase valuable work. The app cannot see your hardware.

What you need

Your K.O. II, your Audio-Technica LP60X-family turntable, a record, a 3.5 mm stereo AUX cable, and headphones or speakers at a comfortable level. For the automatic arrangement you need K.O. II OS 2.0 or newer. The manual-performance alternative is included.

If the rear connector on your exact turntable differs, match that connector with a suitable stereo cable or adapter rather than forcing a plug. Check the label/manual. No Bluetooth pairing is needed.

0 / 50 steps checked

01 / Protect the beat and prepare two-bar patterns
  1. Press MAIN. If playback is running, press PLAY to stop it. Hold MAIN and use − / + to select the scene containing your finished first beat. Listen briefly, then stop. Write its scene number on paper as ORIGINAL.
  2. Press SHIFT + MAIN once. Write the newly selected scene number as WORK. This duplicates the scene for development, not an external backup. Check that ORIGINAL still plays correctly before changing WORK.
  3. In WORK, select group A. Check its pattern length. If it is one bar, tap RECORD to arm, then hold SHIFT and press + once to duplicate it to two bars. Release the buttons and tap RECORD to disarm if still armed. If already two bars, leave it alone. Check the display and listen: the same drums should play in both bars.
  4. Repeat that length check and duplication for group B. It must be two bars, with the two bass pulses copied into bar 2. Stop again. If either A or B is longer than two bars, do not shorten valuable notes: use a disposable one-bar foundation from chapter 01 instead.
  5. Select C, press MAIN, then SHIFT + A to choose the next empty pattern for this group. While stopped, tap RECORD to arm and use + to extend this empty one-bar pattern to two bars; disarm RECORD. Repeat for D. Set TEMPO X to 90. Set TIMING X to 1/16 and press − for quantized entry, then return to MAIN. Check that all four groups are two bars. Keep swing neutral for the first pass.
02 / Connect the turntable and choose two sounds
  1. Stop the turntable. Set its rear PHONO/LINE switch to LINE. On the LP60X-family setup, this uses the built-in phono preamp. Start with your listening volume low.
  2. Connect the turntable's Analog Out to the K.O. II's audio INPUT using the stereo AUX cable. Do not use the K.O. II OUTPUT, MIDI or SYNC socket. Connect headphones or speakers to the K.O. II output as appropriate.
  3. Play your record. Find a short piano, guitar or horn chord without vocals or busy drums. Aim for a single sound about half a second long, not a whole rhythmic loop. You do not need to know the record's tempo for this method.
  4. Find a second short chord or note that sounds pleasant after the first. Write their approximate locations on paper as FIRST and ANSWER. Repeated notes are fine; do not get stuck searching for perfection.
  5. Select group C on the K.O. II and press SAMPLE. Use − / + to select line input, not the microphone. Choose mono line input for this first attempt. Turn X up gradually while the record plays; keep the signal clean, not distorted. Turn Y threshold low enough that the sound can start capture. If silence persists, check LINE, the cable and the selected input.
03 / Capture and trim your vinyl chords
  1. Replay FIRST. In SAMPLE with group C selected, hold unused pad 1 just before the chord starts. Release after the short chord to stop capture. C1 is your FIRST sound. Press MAIN, then tap C1 to audition it.
  2. Replay ANSWER. Select C, press SAMPLE, check line input again, then hold unused pad 2 around the second chord and release to stop. Press MAIN and tap C2. Avoid recording onto a pad you want to keep.
  3. Press SOUND, select C1, then briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit. Use − / + to find trim. Adjust X until the beginning lands cleanly on the chord. Tap C1 between small changes. A little vinyl noise is fine; a long silent lead-in is not.
  4. On that trim page, gently shorten the playback length with Y. Aim for a short stab with no harsh cut. At 90 BPM, one beat is about 0.67 seconds. Repeat the start and length edits for C2. Do not hold SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page: that permanently crops the stored audio file.
  5. For C1 and C2, use the sound-edit sound page to set X play mode to oneshot; this makes each tap play its short sound without needing to hold the pad. Return to ordinary SOUND and lower X amplitude until each chord sits below the snare. Leave pitch Y unchanged initially. Return to MAIN.
04 / Write the two-bar sampled melody
  1. Stop playback. Press MAIN and select group C. Use − / + until the position reads 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap pad 1 once and release RECORD. This writes C1 at the first beat of bar 1.
  2. Navigate to 1.2.3. Hold RECORD, tap C2 once and release. This puts the answer between beats 2 and 3. Simply tapping without RECORD does not write a note.
  3. Navigate to 2.1.1. Hold RECORD and tap C1 once, then release. Check that the first number is 2: this is the second bar, not another note in bar 1.
  4. Navigate to 2.3.1. Hold RECORD and tap C2 once, then release. The complete melody is C1 at 1.1.1 and 2.1.1; C2 at 1.2.3 and 2.3.1. Leave the other positions empty.
  5. Press PLAY and listen to two complete two-bar passes, then stop. If a note is wrong, in stopped MAIN navigate to that position and briefly hold ERASE while tapping only its pad. Never use ERASE + SOUND to fix a sequenced note: that deletes audio.
05 / Make bass and melody work together
  1. Select B, press MAIN and check the bass pulses at 1.1.1, 1.3.1, 2.1.1 and 2.3.1. If a position is genuinely empty, navigate there and write B1 with held RECORD + pad 1. Do not add a second hit over a note already present.
  2. Play the loop. Listen only to bass plus chords. If they clash, stop, press SOUND, select B1 and change Y pitch a little. Play again. Keep the setting that sounds comfortable with BOTH vinyl sounds, not just one.
  3. If no bass pitch fits, try another short bass sample from 400–499 in SOUND with − / +. Your custom library may differ. If it still sounds wrong, use a drums-and-chords version without bass rather than forcing it. Check every saved scene if you change the shared pad sound later.
  4. Balance the parts in SOUND: select A3 and reduce X if the hats are sharp; select C1 and C2 and keep them below the snare; select B1 and keep it low enough that the kick is distinct. Make one small adjustment, listen, then stop. Do not turn X in MAIN expecting pad volume.
  5. Write the current WORK scene number as HOOK. Play it and confirm drums, bass and vinyl melody repeat correctly across two bars. Stop. Press SHIFT + MAIN to duplicate HOOK before making the next section. Write the new scene number as MAIN.
06 / Create main groove and intro scenes
  1. In the new MAIN scene, select C and stopped MAIN. Briefly erase C1 at 1.1.1 and 2.1.1, using ERASE + pad 1 at each position. Briefly erase C2 at 1.2.3 and 2.3.1 with ERASE + pad 2. Keep the sample files.
  2. Press PLAY. MAIN should have drums and bass but no vinyl melody. Stop. Hold MAIN with − / + to reselect HOOK and confirm its melody is still there. Return to MAIN. If the original section changed too, stop and undo if available before continuing; do not assume the copies are independent without checking.
  3. With MAIN selected, press SHIFT + MAIN once to duplicate it. Write the newly selected scene number as INTRO. Select B and briefly erase B1 at 1.1.1, 1.3.1, 2.1.1 and 2.3.1. INTRO should now be drums only.
  4. Play INTRO for two repeats. Stop. Check MAIN still has bass and HOOK still has melody. Write all three scene numbers clearly. The sound settings may be shared within the project, so make section changes with note events, not pad amplitude.
  5. With INTRO selected, press SHIFT + MAIN once. Write the new scene number as BREAK. This is your disposable copy for the stripped-down section.
07 / Make a breakdown with breathing room
  1. In BREAK select A and stopped MAIN. Briefly erase the snare A2 at 1.2.1, 1.4.1, 2.2.1 and 2.4.1. Navigate to each position first; do not hold ERASE long enough to clear a whole track accidentally.
  2. Keep the hat only on beats 1 and 3 in each bar. Briefly erase A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.3, 1.4.1 and 1.4.3. Repeat those six positions starting with 2 instead of 1. Remaining hats: 1.1.1, 1.3.1, 2.1.1, 2.3.1.
  3. Play BREAK. You should hear the original kick rhythm with sparse hats, no snare, bass or chord melody. Stop. If this feels too empty, that is intentional: the full return will feel larger.
  4. Hold MAIN and use − / + to select HOOK. Check the full beat once more. Stop and press SHIFT + MAIN to duplicate HOOK. Write the newly selected scene number as RETURN.
  5. Check RETURN still has the original full drums, bass and C melody, and D has no notes yet. This is the only scene that will get the surprise sound. All scenes must still have a longest pattern of exactly two bars.
08 / Add the twist: your own vocal answer
  1. Stop playback and pause the turntable. Select D, press SAMPLE, then use − / + to choose the built-in microphone, not line input. Keep your listening volume low to avoid feedback.
  2. Hold unused D1, say a short quiet “hey” or “yeah”, then release. Press MAIN and audition D1. Your own voice is enough; no special microphone or musical skill is needed.
  3. In SOUND select D1, briefly open SHIFT + SOUND and navigate to trim. Use X to remove silence and Y to shorten the tail. On the sound page select oneshot. Return to ordinary SOUND and lower X amplitude so this is a small detail, not the loudest part.
  4. In RETURN, select D and stopped MAIN. Navigate to 2.4.3. Hold RECORD and tap D1 once, then release. Play two passes: the voice should answer near the end of every second bar. If you dislike it, briefly erase that one event; keep the sample.
  5. Stop and verify HOOK has no D1 voice but RETURN does. Select INTRO and duplicate it with SHIFT + MAIN; write the new number as END. In END briefly erase A1 at 1.2.3, 1.3.1, 1.4.3 and those same positions in bar 2, leaving only the first kick in each bar plus the hats. Check END, then stop.
09 / Build the full 2–3 minute arrangement
  1. Read the song map below and keep your actual scene numbers beside you. Every scene is TWO bars. A song position plays once for the longest group pattern, so four positions using INTRO make eight bars. Scene numbers and song-position numbers are different.
  2. On OS 2.0 or newer, hold MAIN then press ENTER to open the song-list editor. For safety this recipe needs an unused song list in your disposable project. If a valuable arrangement is already there, leave it alone and use the manual-performance option. The new list starts with one position. Hold SHIFT and use − / + to assign INTRO to position 1.
  3. Hold SHIFT and press A to add a position. Hold SHIFT with − / + to assign its scene. Repeat until positions 1–4 are INTRO, 5–12 MAIN, 13–20 HOOK, 21–24 BREAK, 25–30 RETURN and 31–32 END. For positions that need the same scene, check the assigned scene rather than assuming it was copied. Release SHIFT and use − / + to move between song positions.
  4. Review all 32 positions, then press SHIFT + PLAY to start the song from the beginning. Listen through and stop. At 90 BPM this 64-bar plan lasts about 2:51. If it ends much sooner, you have too few repeated positions or a scene shorter than two bars. If it lasts longer, check for a group pattern longer than two bars.
  5. Manual alternative: leave the song editor and select INTRO in MAIN. Press PLAY. Count two-bar passes: INTRO 4, MAIN 8, HOOK 8, BREAK 4, RETURN 6, END 2. Select each next scene with held MAIN and − / + around the section boundary, watching its number. Stop after END. Rehearse switches first; manual timing may not be perfectly seamless. This replaces steps 42–44 on older firmware.
10 / Finish, listen and preserve your work
  1. Play from the beginning at a comfortable volume. Listen for kick and snare first, then bass, then chords. Stop and change only one pad amplitude in SOUND if needed. Global pad sound changes can affect more than one scene, so listen through again.
  2. Optional: add a little space using the official effects guide linked below. Keep bass dry and avoid recording fader automation accidentally. This is not required: a clear dry beat is better than a blurred beat with too much effect.
  3. Listen to BREAK followed by RETURN. The sparse drums should make the full melody and little voice answer feel bigger. If the voice is distracting, in RETURN remove only D1 at 2.4.3. Do not change every part trying to fix one sound.
  4. Record the full performance using a suitable external audio recorder/interface connected to the K.O. II output, or follow the official USB-audio instructions if your installed firmware supports them. COMMIT duplicates scenes; it is not a finished-song export. The browser coach cannot record hardware audio.
  5. Write the project number and ORIGINAL, HOOK, MAIN, INTRO, BREAK, RETURN and END scene numbers on paper. Use supported official backup tools for device data. Your checklist is saved only in this browser. For public release of music containing someone else's record, check and obtain the necessary permissions; do not assume sampling automatically grants rights.
