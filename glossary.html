Tools

Glossary

115 words you’ll meet with the K.O. II, in plain English.

#ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVDisplay

0–9

4/4
Four beats per bar, the time signature every chapter uses.

A

Amplitude (AMP)
A pad’s volume, set with X in SOUND mode.
Arpeggio
A chord played one note at a time: in KEYS, hold TIMING and press several pads. It needs a oneshot or legato sound.
Attack chop
An auto chop that cuts a sample at its loudest hits, good for drums.
Audition
Tapping a pad to hear it, without recording anything.
Automation
Fader moves recorded into a pattern by holding RECORD as you move it. They replay every loop. Hold ERASE + FADER until FDR blinks to erase them.

B

Backbeat
Snare on beats 2 and 4, as in Chapter 1.
Backup / restore
Copying your projects and sounds to a computer with the EP sample tool, and putting them back later. Commit is not a backup.
Bar
A group of beats: in 4/4, four beats.
Beat
The steady pulse you tap your foot to, counted “one, two, three, four”.
BPM
Beats per minute: the speed. At 90 BPM one bar lasts about 2.7 seconds.

C

Chop
Cutting one sample into slices, each on its own pad, with SHIFT + SAMPLE. An auto chop resets the pads of the group you choose.
Clash
Two notes that sound wrong together, such as a bass a semitone away from a voice. An octave apart, they fit. Hear both in Chapter 5: Retune the bass if it clashes.
Class compliant
Works without a special driver: computers and phones see the K.O. II as a standard USB audio device.
Commit
SHIFT + MAIN: saves the current scene and moves you into a copy of it. Notes you change in the copy stay in the copy. Sounds and their settings (sample, volume, pitch, trim, play mode) belong to the project, so every scene shares them. Not a backup.
Count-in
Four metronome beats before live recording starts: tap RECORD, then press PLAY.
Crop
Cutting the sample file to its trim: hold SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page. Cannot be undone.

D

DEL / CLR
Shown after holding ERASE + MAIN for two seconds. DEL: an empty scene not in the song was deleted. CLR: the scene was cleared.
Disk low
A warning when you press SAMPLE and there is no room left to record 20 seconds.
Drop / build
In dance music, the drop is the full-energy part and the build is the rise right before it.
Ducking
Turning a sound down briefly so another one stands out: what sidechain does.

E

Eighth notes
Two even notes per beat, counted “1 and 2 and…”. With 16 steps per bar they fall on every .1 and .3.
Envelope (attack, release)
How a sound fades in (attack) and fades out after you let go (release).
EP sample tool
teenage engineering’s browser tool for moving samples between the K.O. II and a computer over USB-C, and for backing up your work.
EQU (equal-length chop)
An auto chop that cuts a sample into equal slices, good for loops. In chop, hold the group pad, pick EQU with − / +, release, then pick the number of slices with − / +.

F

Factory sounds
The samples a new K.O. II comes with, numbered by type: kicks 1–99, snares 100–199, hi-hats 200–299, percussion 300–399, bass 400–499, melodic 500–599. Formatting the drive erases them for good.
Fader
The slider. It sets the selected group’s level, or in FX its send.
Firmware (OS)
The K.O. II’s own software. This course is written for OS 2.5.1, so update an older unit first.
Flam
A thicker double hit: a second sound lands slightly after the first. Chapter 4 shifts a kick layer six ticks late to make one: Hear the flam.
Format (FMT)
Erasing the whole drive by holding SHIFT + ERASE while switching on, with FMT on the display for about 10 seconds. All your work and all factory sounds are gone and cannot be recovered.
Four-on-the-floor
A kick on every beat (1.1.1, 1.2.1, 1.3.1, 1.4.1), typical of house music.
FX / master effect
The one effect every group shares: delay, reverb, distortion, chorus, filter or compressor.
FX send
How much of a group goes into the shared effect, set with the fader in FX mode.

G

Ghost note
A very soft hit between the main beats, much quieter than the backbeat. Chapter 4 adds one or two right before beat 4: Add soft ghost notes.
Groove
The main repeating feel of a track, usually drums plus bass.
Group
One of four banks of 12 pads, A to D, each with its own patterns.

H

Hands-free sampling
In SAMPLE, SHIFT + a pad records without holding the pad. Press SAMPLE to stop.
Hook
The short, catchy part listeners remember, often a vocal phrase.

I

Input level / gain
How loud incoming audio is when sampling: X in SAMPLE mode.
Input source
What SAMPLE records from, chosen with − / +: mic, line in (in, or L.IN / R.IN for one side), resample (rsp) or usb.
Intro / breakdown / outro
Song sections: the opening, a stripped-back part that makes the next one hit harder, and the ending.

K

Key mode (play mode)
A play mode in which the same sample can sound several times at once. Not the same as KEYS mode.
KEYS
A mode that plays one sample at 12 different pitches across the pads.

L

Latch
Keeping something going after you let go. For note repeat, press TIMING, then hold SHIFT and press a pad. Do it again to stop.
Layer
One part of a beat, such as the kick, hats or bass, added on top of the others.
Legato
A play mode that plays a sample one at a time. Changing notes while one is held carries on from the same point.
Line input
The 3.5 mm input for sampling a phone or synth instead of the built-in mic.
Live chop
Chopping by hand: in chop mode, press pads while the sample plays to place the cuts. The display shows Cho.
Live monitoring
Hearing incoming audio through the K.O. II as it arrives. For USB audio, system setting 511 turns it on, and 510, the default, turns it off.
Live recording
Playing pads while the loop runs and RECORD is on.
Lock mode (LOK)
Holding MAIN while switching on: LOK shows, and no change to projects, patterns, scenes, pads or settings survives a restart.
Loop
A pattern repeating over and over.
Loop function
SHIFT + TEMPO repeats a short part of the pattern live. X slides the loop, Y sets its length. TEMPO stops it at once, MAIN at the next bar.

M

Metronome
The click that keeps time while you record. TEMPO Y sets its volume.
Mixing
Setting how loud each part is compared with the others.
Mono / stereo
Mono is one audio channel, stereo two: left and right. Mono samples can be up to 40 seconds long.
Mute group
A sound-edit setting that stops certain samples playing together, such as an open and a closed hi-hat.

N

Note interval (16, 8, 16T)
TIMING X: how many steps a bar has. 16 means 16 steps. 16T means triplets: 24 steps per bar.
Note repeat
Hold TIMING and press a pad: the pad repeats in time with the grid.

O

Off-beat
A note between the main beats, such as the “and” after a beat (1.1.3).
Oneshot
A play mode that plays the whole sample, one at a time.
Output / master compressor
SHIFT + FX: the compressor on the whole mix. X sets drive, Y speed.
Overdub
Recording new notes on top of a pattern that already has notes.

P

Pad
One of the 12 buttons that play sounds. In many modes they also type numbers.
Pan
Where a sound sits between left and right: Y on the sound page of sound edit.
Pattern
The notes one group plays, and when.
Pattern length
How many bars a pattern lasts before it repeats. New patterns are one bar.
Phrase
A short musical idea, like a sentence: a few notes or words that belong together.
Pickup
A note right before a main beat that leads into it, like the kicks at 1.2.3 and 1.4.3.
Pitch (PTC)
How high or low a sound plays: Y in SOUND mode.
Play mode (oneshot / key / legato)
How a pad plays its sample: oneshot, key or legato, shown as one, key or leg. X on the sound page of sound edit.
Position (1.2.3)
What the display shows in MAIN: bar . beat . step. 1.2.3 is bar 1, beat 2, third step (grid step 7).
Project
One whole song. The K.O. II holds nine: on a new unit 1–5 hold factory demos and 6–9 are empty.
Punch-in FX
Temporary effects played by holding FX and pressing pads while the beat runs.

Q

Quantize
Snapping what you record live to the nearest step. Free time records exactly as you play.

R

Record arm
RECORD pressed and waiting: the next PLAY starts recording.
Resample (RSP)
Recording the K.O. II’s own sound, such as a whole pattern with effects, into one new sample. In SAMPLE, press + until rsp shows.
Resample bars
With hands-free sampling on, − / + sets how many bars to record and PLAY starts it. With no bar count, it records the selected pattern’s length.
Reverb
An effect that adds the sound of a room. X sets its length, Y its colour.
Reverse (rev)
Playing a sample backwards: SHIFT + SOUND, press + until tim, then turn X to rev.

S

Sample
A short recording used as an instrument. The K.O. II holds up to 999.
Sample rate
How finely audio is recorded. The default, 46,875 Hz (system setting 502), sounds best. 500 and 501 record smaller, more lo-fi samples.
Save sound edits
Holding SHIFT + SOUND for two seconds saves your edits to the current sound. On the trim page the same hold crops the file instead.
Scale / key / octave
In KEYS, the scale is which notes the pads offer, the key is the note they start from, and the octave is how high they play. Hold KEYS and turn Y for scale, X for key, or press − / + for octave. BLU is the blues scale. Each project keeps its key and scale.
Scene
A saved combination of the four groups’ patterns: one section of a song, such as INTRO or BREAK. Sounds and their settings (sample, volume, pitch, trim, play mode) belong to the project, so every scene shares them.
Sidechain
Briefly turning chosen groups down each time a source sound, such as the kick, plays. SHIFT + FX, then +, opens it. X sets how long the dip lasts, Y its shape.
Slice
One piece of a chopped sample, played from its own pad.
Solo
Hearing one group alone: while playing, hold FX and press its group button.
Song list / song position
In song mode, the numbered positions that play your scenes in order, each once.
Song mode
Automatic playback of the song list. Hold MAIN and press ENTER to open its editor.
Sound edit
SHIFT + SOUND: pages for play mode, trim, envelope, time and more for the selected pad.
Stab
A short, punchy chord or sound hit.
Step
One slot in the sequencer grid. With TIMING at 16, a bar has 16 steps, four per beat.
Step entry
Placing notes one at a time while stopped: go to a position, hold RECORD and tap a pad.
Subdivision
A smaller slice of a beat. In 1.2.3 the last number is the subdivision.
Swing
Delays every second step slightly for a looser, bouncier feel, on the 8 and 16 grids.
System settings
Device-wide options: with the unit on, press SHIFT + ERASE, type a code such as 411 and press ENTER. MAIN returns to your beat.

T

Tail
The part of a sound that keeps ringing after the hit. Long tails can blur a beat.
Tempo match
Hold SAMPLE + TEMPO and play audio into the mic or line in: the K.O. II sets the project tempo to match it.
Threshold
How loud a sound must be before sampling starts: Y in SAMPLE mode.
Time shift / nudge
Moving a recorded note earlier or later: stopped, hold SHIFT and its pad, then press − / +. In free time each press moves it one tick. In quantize it jumps a whole step. Also called offset notes.
Time stretch (BPM / BAR)
The time page (tim) of sound edit. BPM stretches a sample to the project tempo once you set its own BPM with Y. BAR fits it to a number of bars.
Timing correct
SHIFT + TIMING: snaps notes you already recorded on a pad to the grid.
Trim
Choosing where a sample starts and how long it plays, on the trim page of sound edit. The file stays unchanged until you crop.
TRK / PTN / SND / FDR
What the display shows as you erase. TRK: all of a pad’s notes, with ERASE + pad while stopped. PTN: a group’s pattern, with ERASE + group. SND: a sample, with ERASE + SOUND. FDR: fader moves, with ERASE + FADER. SND is also the first page of sound edit.

U

Undo (umbrella)
SHIFT + B undoes the last change. The umbrella icon lights when undo is possible.
Update utility
teenage engineering’s browser tool that installs the latest OS on the K.O. II (teenage.engineering/apps/update).
USB audio
Sound to and from a computer or phone over the USB-C cable. The K.O. II can sample it (source usb), and the other device can record or play through it.

V

Velocity
How hard a pad is hit. It is off by default: system setting 301 or 302 turns it on, 300 turns it off.

On the display

The short words the display shows, grouped by where you meet them.

Sound-edit pages
SHIFT + SOUND opens sound edit, and − / + turns its six pages: SND sound (play mode and pan), TRI trim, ENV envelope, tim time, MID midi and GRP mute group. Chapter 3 steps through them: Step through the six pages.
Output pages
SHIFT + FX shows OUT, the output settings (master compressor). Press + for S.Ch, the sidechain settings. There LEN is the length of the dip (X) and ShP its shape (Y). Chapter 4 opens them: Open the sidechain settings.
Ln.2 / Ln.4 / Ln.8
The selected group’s pattern length in bars: Ln.2 is two bars. It shows when you add a bar or double a pattern. Chapter 2 shows it first: Give the melody two bars.
Settings menu words
In system settings, − / + steps through the groups: mid MIDI, syn sync, pad pads and knobs, seq metronome, scene changes and song loop, and smp recording rate and USB input. ENTER opens one and SHIFT + ENTER goes back. In seq, met is the metronome: on clicks while playing and recording, rec only while recording (the default), cnt only for the count-in. Chapter 0 opens the menu: Open the system settings.
Fader reset (RES, SET)
Stopped, SHIFT + FADER sets every fader function of the group back to its default position, and RES shows. Pressing it again undoes the reset, and SET shows. Chapter 4: Reset the fader functions.
Error codes (E.01–E.12)
E.01, E.02 and E.03 mean the unit needs repair: contact teenage engineering support, and don’t format. E.05, E.10, E.11 and E.12 are file-system errors, fixed by a format (see Format). Chapter 0: Error code? Look it up.

Also on the display: AMP, PTC, one / key / leg, BPM / BAR, rev, mic, in, usb, rsp, EQU, Cho, BLU, 16 / 8 / 16T, 1.2.3, TRK / PTN / SND / FDR, DEL / CLR, LOK, FMT and disk low.
