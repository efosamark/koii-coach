CHAPTER 3 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Sample, chop & resample

Record your own sounds, chop them and resample a beat.

So far you used factory sounds. Now you record your own, then shape them, chop them into slices and bounce a whole beat onto one pad.

You will make: a drum kit from your voice, slices and a bounced beat.

Before you start

  • Empty project 7, OS 2.5 or newer
  • Headphones or a speaker
  • Optional: a phone and a cable
  • Cropping a sample (hold SHIFT + SOUND, Part 5) can’t be undone.
  • Never hold SHIFT + ERASE as you switch on.
Safety, read this first. Press SHIFT + ERASE only while the K.O. II is already on: it opens system settings, used in Parts 3 and 4. Held while you switch the unit on, SHIFT + ERASE formats the drive, erasing all your work and every factory sound. Factory sounds cannot be recovered. Back up first with the official EP sample tool (see Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II). Two more actions are permanent: holding ERASE + SOUND deletes the selected sample from the device, and holding SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page crops a sample file (one optional step). Work in an empty project so nothing you care about is overwritten.

What you need

OS 2.5 or newer. Older? Update first with the official update utility, as in Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II.

Headphones or a speaker. For Part 3, optionally a phone and a cable: 3.5 mm stereo (or a headphone adapter), or USB-C.

Chapters 1 and 2 done. Their moves come back here without explanation. New to them? Start with Chapter 1 · First beat and Chapter 2 · Make it a song.

Sounds you may use. Sample your voice, sounds you make, or audio you have permission to use. Sampling grants no rights: get permission before you publish someone else’s recording.

Your pad plan

Each group has one job, so you always know where a sound lives.

Every recording goes into the K.O. II’s shared sound library, which holds up to 999 samples or 128 MB. A pad only points to a sample.

Part 1 · Record your first sample

Goal: record a sound with the built-in mic onto D1, then put it in a beat.

  1. Open empty project 7

    Press MAIN. If the K.O. II is playing, press PLAY to stop it. Hold MAIN, hold pad 7 as well, then release both.

    Check: While you hold both, the display shows P 7.

    Why

    Project 7 is empty on a new unit, and your Chapters 1 and 2 work in project 6 stays untouched.

  2. Check that project 7 is empty

    Tap a few pads in groups A, B, C and D.

    Check: None of them makes a sound.

  3. Set the tempo to 90 BPM

    Press TEMPO and turn X until the display shows 90 (or hold TEMPO and type 9, 0).

    Check: The display shows 90, the new tempo in BPM.

  4. Set the grid to 16 steps

    Press TIMING, turn X until the display shows 16 (not 16T) and press − for quantize. Press MAIN.

    Check: The display showed 16, not 16T; the MAIN icon is lit again.

    Why

    Quantize snaps notes to this grid, so every position means what it meant in Chapters 1 and 2.

  5. Choose the microphone

    Tap D to select group D, then press SAMPLE. Press − or + until the display shows mic.

    Check: The pads light up and blink, which means you are in sample mode.

    Why

    The MIC icon lights while the microphone is in use. The other sources are line in (in, L.IN, R.IN), resample (rsp) and USB (usb).

  6. Set the level and threshold

    Check that the pads are still blinking before you turn.

    Turn Y (threshold) all the way down, so recording starts as soon as you hold a pad. Set X (input level) to about halfway.

    Check: The pads keep blinking, still in sample mode.

    Why

    In sample mode, X sets the input level and Y the threshold: how loud a sound must be to start recording.

  7. D1: record your first sample

    Get close to the K.O. II. Hold D1, say “hey!” or clap once, then release D1.

    Check: D1 stops blinking and stays lit, which means it now holds a recording.

    Why

    Recording stops as soon as you let go.

  8. Listen, and set the level

    Tap D1 to hear it. Too quiet or distorted? Turn X (input level) up or down, press SAMPLE and record D1 again.

    Check: D1 plays back your sound.

    Not working?

    To retake it, press SAMPLE, hold D1 and record again.

  9. Write down the sample number

    Press SOUND and tap D1. Write the number down as FIRST. Press MAIN.

    Check: Your paper shows FIRST and its sample number.

    Why

    It is the library slot that holds your recording, saved automatically. Hold SOUND and type that number to load it on any pad, in any project.

  10. Go to 1.1.1 with group D

    In MAIN, stopped, with group D selected: hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1.

    Check: The display shows 1.1.1.

  11. D1: place it and play

    Hold RECORD, tap D1, release RECORD. Press PLAY.

    Check: Your sound plays at the start of every loop, about every 2.7 seconds.

    Press PLAY to stop.

Mono, stereo and length. Mono records one channel, stereo left and right. A mono sample can last up to 40 seconds, a stereo one less. An unofficial cheat sheet says mic records mono. The MONO icon lights when a sample is mono. A “disk low” warning when you press SAMPLE means there isn’t room for 20 seconds of audio: back up, then delete recordings you no longer need. Holding ERASE + SOUND until SND blinks deletes the selected sample permanently, so check its number first.

Checkpoint: your own sound plays on D1 at the start of every bar.

If it doesn’t: D1 still blinks? Nothing was recorded: turn Y down, check the source is mic and keep holding the pad while you make the sound. D1 lit but silent? Press SOUND, tap D1 and check X (amplitude) isn’t at zero. In MAIN, check the fader isn’t at the bottom. Pads made sounds in step 1? You are still in your old project: hold MAIN and pad 7 together until the display shows P 7. See help “My sample will not start recording”.

Part 2 · Build a drum kit from your voice

Goal: record a kick, a snare and a hat with your voice onto A1, A2 and A3, then program the Chapter 1 rhythm with them. No factory sounds on your unit? This kit works for the whole course.

  1. Erase the test note

    Press briefly: a long hold clears every note of the pad.

    Removes only that D1 note; the recording stays.

    In MAIN, stopped, with group D selected, go to 1.1.1 and briefly press ERASE + D1.

    Wrong note? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: D1 no longer lights at 1.1.1 and PLAY stays silent.

    Why

    Erasing a note changes the pattern, not the sound. The recording stays on the pad and in the library.

  2. Set up group A for sampling

    Tap A, then press SAMPLE. Turn Y (threshold) up a little from the bottom.

    Check: The display still shows mic as the source.

    Why

    Recording now waits until your sound is loud enough, so it starts right on the hit, with no silence before it.

  3. A1: record a kick

    Hold A1, make one short, low sound close to the K.O. II, then release A1. Tap A1.

    Check: A short, low thud.

    Why

    For example a deep “dm” or “boom”, or a knock on the table next to it.

    Not working?

    Not punchy enough? Press SAMPLE and retake it.

  4. A2: record a snare

    Hold A2, make one sharp sound, such as “psh”, “k” or a single hand clap, then release A2 and tap it.

    Check: Brighter and snappier than the kick.

  5. A3: record a hat

    Hold A3, make a short “ts” or “t”, release and tap it.

    Check: A short, bright tick.

    Not working?

    Recording never starts? Lower Y a little. Background noise starts it too early? Raise Y a little.

  6. Make the kick deeper

    Press SOUND and tap A1. Turn Y (pitch) down a little: your mouth kick gets deeper.

    Check: A1 plays your kick at a lower pitch.

  7. Make the hat quieter

    Tap A3 and turn X (amplitude) down until the hat is quieter than the kick and snare. Press MAIN.

    Check: Kick low and solid, hat in the background.

  8. Go to 1.1.1 with group A

    In MAIN, stopped, with group A selected, hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1.

    Check: The display shows 1.1.1.

  9. A1: place the kicks

    Hold RECORD + A1 at 1.1.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.1 and 1.4.3, moving between positions with − / +.

    Check: A1 lights at each of the four positions.

    Why

    The same kick rhythm as Chapter 1.

  10. A2: place the snares

    Hold RECORD + A2 at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1.

    Check: A2 lights at both positions.

  11. A3: place the hats

    Hold RECORD + A3 at 1.1.1, 1.1.3, 1.2.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.1, 1.3.3, 1.4.1 and 1.4.3.

    Check: A3 lights at all eight positions.

    Why

    Some share a step with a kick or snare, which is correct.

  12. Listen to your voice kit

    Press PLAY, listen to a few loops and press PLAY to stop.

    Check: Your voice kick, snare and hats play the Chapter 1 rhythm.

  13. Commit it as VOICE KIT

    Hold MAIN, read the scene number and write it down as VOICE KIT. Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once to commit.

    Check: Holding MAIN now shows a different number.

    Why

    You are working in a copy, and VOICE KIT stays as it is.

Checkpoint: your voice kick, snare and hats play the Chapter 1 rhythm.

If it doesn’t: a sound starts late? Its recording begins with silence: retake it with Y slightly higher, or trim it in Part 5. Extra or missing hits? Press SHIFT + B (undo) while the umbrella icon is lit, or go to the wrong position and briefly press ERASE + that pad.

Part 3 · Hands-free sampling and sampling your phone

Goal: record without holding a pad, then sample your phone. Follow the line-input steps or the USB steps: both end with a phone sample on D3.

  1. Set up hands-free sampling

    Tap D and press SAMPLE; check the mic source. Leave Y slightly up so the recording waits for your voice.

    Check: Empty pads blink and the display shows mic.

  2. D2: start hands-free sampling

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold SHIFT and press D2, an empty, blinking pad.

    Check: Nothing records yet: the K.O. II waits for your voice.

    Why

    The K.O. II holds the pad for you.

  3. Count to four, then stop

    Count “one, two, three, four” evenly and briskly, about two words per second, then press SAMPLE straight after “four” to stop.

    Check: D2 stays lit; tap it to hear your count.

    Why

    You will chop this in Part 6, so the spacing matters.

    Not working?

    Uneven? Record it again on an empty pad such as D6 and use that pad instead of D2.

  4. Connect your phone to the line input

    Line input: connect your phone’s headphone output (or its headphone adapter) to the K.O. II’s audio INPUT with a 3.5 mm stereo cable.

    Check: The cable runs from the phone into INPUT.

    The input sits with the other sockets on the top side; do not use OUTPUT, MIDI or SYNC.

  5. Set the phone’s volume to half

    Set the phone’s volume to about half.

  6. Choose what to sample

    Choose 2–8 seconds of something you made or may use, such as a voice memo or your own loop.

  7. Select the line input

    Tap D, press SAMPLE and press − / + until the display shows in. Leave Y slightly up.

    Check: The display shows in; empty pads blink.

    Why

    That is the line input. Its mono and stereo versions share this label, and either works here.

  8. D3: record the phone, hands-free

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold SHIFT and press D3, start the phone, and press SAMPLE when your part has finished. Tap D3.

    Check: You hear the phone’s sound, clean and undistorted.

    Not working?

    Distorted: turn X down and record again on an empty pad. Too quiet: raise X or the phone’s volume.

  9. Connect over USB audio (OS 2.5)

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Connect the K.O. II to the phone or a computer with a USB-C cable.

    Check: The K.O. II appears as an audio device.

    Why

    USB audio works even when your phone has no headphone output.

  10. Choose the K.O. II as audio output

    On the phone or computer, choose the K.O. II as the audio output device.

    Check: The phone or computer shows the K.O. II as its output.

    Why

    Any phone or computer that supports standard USB audio works.

  11. Optional: hear the phone while sampling

    With the K.O. II already on.

    Press SHIFT + ERASE to open system settings. Type 5, 1, 1 on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Type 510 the same way to switch back.

    Why

    You hear the phone through the K.O. II while you sample. 511 is sampler + live monitoring, and 510, the default, is sampler only.

  12. Select USB as the source

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Tap D, press SAMPLE and press − / + until the display shows usb (mono and stereo share this label). Leave Y slightly up.

    Check: The display shows usb; empty pads blink.

  13. D3: record over USB, hands-free

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Hold SHIFT and press D3, start the audio on the phone and press SAMPLE when it has finished. Tap the pad.

    Check: You hear the phone’s sound from the K.O. II.

    Not working?

    If D3 already holds a line-in sample, use the next empty pad.

Checkpoint: D2 plays your evenly spaced count, and D3 a few clean seconds from your phone.

If it doesn’t: hands-free didn’t start? Use an empty pad, one that is still blinking. No sound from the line input? Check the cable is in INPUT, the phone’s volume is up, the source is in, X is up and Y not too high. No sound over USB? The phone or computer must use the K.O. II as its output, and the source must be usb. See help “Where did USB or reverse go?”.

Part 4 · Recording quality: lo versus hi

Goal: record the same bright sound at hi and lo quality, compare them, then set hi back.

  1. Choose the mic again

    Tap D, press SAMPLE and choose the mic source.

    Check: Empty pads blink and the display shows mic.

    Why

    The K.O. II records at hi quality unless you change it.

  2. D4: record keys at hi quality

    Hold D4, jingle a bunch of keys close to the K.O. II for about two seconds, then release.

    Check: D4 is lit, and tapping it plays bright jingling.

  3. Set the lo recording rate (500)

    With the K.O. II already on.

    Press SHIFT + ERASE to open system settings. Type 5, 0, 0 on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Why

    500 is the lo rate. The menu route gets there too: SHIFT + ERASE, press + until the display shows smp, press ENTER, rec appears, press ENTER again, use − / + to select lo, then press ENTER.

  4. D5: record the keys again at lo

    Tap D, press SAMPLE, choose the mic, hold D5 and jingle the same keys the same way. Release.

    Check: D5 is lit.

  5. Compare hi and lo

    Tap D4, then D5, several times.

    Check: D5 (lo) sounds duller and grittier, and the sparkle of the keys is softer.

    Curious about mid? Type 501 the same way and record a third take on an empty pad.

    Why

    Lower rates add character and use less storage.

  6. Set the rate back to hi (502)

    Set it back: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 5, 0, 2, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: The SYSTEM icon goes out.

    Why

    Otherwise every sample you record from now on is lo-fi. D5 keeps its sound: the setting only affects new recordings.

Checkpoint: D4 and D5 sound clearly different: D5 is darker and rougher.

If it doesn’t: no difference? Use a bright sound such as keys or a long “sss”: dull sounds hide it. Not sure which rate is set? Take the menu route (smp, then rec) and read the display. Typed a wrong code? Type the right one and press ENTER.

Official guide: what’s new, OS 2.5 (sample rate) · 14 system settings 500–502 · 10.2 sample tool

Part 5 · Shape and reverse a sound in SOUND EDIT

Goal: tighten your snare in sound edit, then make a reversed copy that swells into the next bar.

  1. A2: open sound edit

    Always press SHIFT + SOUND briefly: held, it saves your edits, and on the trim page it crops the file.

    Press SOUND, tap A, then tap A2 (your snare). Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND: sound edit opens.

    Check: A2 plays your snare as you select it.

  2. Step through the six pages

    Press − / + to step through its six pages: sound (play mode and pan), trim, envelope, time, midi and mute group.

    Check: The display changes with each page.

    Why

    The trim page is TRI, the time page tim.

  3. Trim the start of A2

    Go to the trim page. Turn X (start point) a little at a time, tapping A2 between turns, until the snare starts the instant you tap.

    Check: A2 sounds the instant you tap it.

    Hold SHIFT while turning for finer steps.

  4. Shorten the tail

    Turn Y (length) down until the tail ends cleanly.

    Check: A2 is quick and tight.

    Why

    Trim sets where the sound starts and stops, in this project only. The stored recording stays whole.

  5. Try the attack, then undo it

    Press + to the envelope page. Turn X (attack) up and tap A2: the snare fades in and loses its crack. Turn X back down.

    Check: A2 has its crack back.

    Why

    Y is release: whether the sound keeps playing after you let go of the pad or is cut off at once.

  6. Set A2 to oneshot

    Press − until you reach the sound page. Turn X to oneshot. Tap A2 very briefly.

    Check: The whole snare plays even though you let go at once.

    Why

    Key lets copies of the sound overlap (good for KEYS), and legato carries on from the same point when you change notes while holding. Y on this page is pan.

  7. Copy A2’s sound

    Press SHIFT + C only once; pressing it twice copies every sound in the group.

    Press MAIN, then press SOUND and tap A2. Hold SHIFT and press C once: this copies A2’s sound.

    Check: A2 plays your snare as you select it.

  8. Paste it onto A4

    Tap A4, an empty pad, then hold SHIFT and press D: this pastes it. Tap A4.

    Check: A4 plays your snare too.

  9. A4: open the time page

    With A4 selected in SOUND, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, then press − / + until the display shows tim.

    Check: The display shows tim.

  10. Reverse A4

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Turn X until it shows rev. Tap A4.

    Check: The snare plays backwards, a soft swell that ends in a snap.

    Tap A2: it still plays forwards.

  11. A4: place the reversed snare

    Press MAIN. Stopped, with group A selected, go to 1.4.3, hold RECORD + A4 and release. Press PLAY.

    Check: The reversed snare swells and its loud end lands as the loop starts again.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Not working?

    Too early or too late? Briefly press ERASE + A4 at 1.4.3 and try 1.4.2 or 1.4.4 instead.

  12. Optional: set the kick’s crop points

    Optional and permanent: skip this if you are unsure.

    Press SOUND, tap A1, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, go to the trim page and set X and Y so only the kick remains.

    Check: A1 plays only the kick.

  13. Optional: crop the kick

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Only crop a recording you made today, never a factory sound.

    Overwrites the kick’s file: only the part between your trim points is kept.

    Then hold SHIFT + SOUND.

    Cropping rewrites the sample file to its trim points; the guide says it “will overwrite the file and cannot be undone”.

    Check: A1 sounds as before; the file now ends at your trim points.

    Every pad, in every project, that uses this sample now plays the cropped version.

Checkpoint: A2 is tight, and A4 swells backwards into the start of each loop.

If it doesn’t: X or Y changed volume or pitch? You were in SOUND, not sound edit: press SHIFT + SOUND briefly. A2 plays backwards too? Tap A4 in SOUND before you open sound edit. Don’t like an edit? Turn the knob back: trim, envelope and play mode are not permanent.

Part 6 · Chop a sample into slices

Goal: cut your count on D2 into four slices on group C, then make a pattern from them.

  1. Optional: try live chop

    In MAIN tap D, tap D2, tap C, press SHIFT + SAMPLE and tap C pads in time with the words.

    Check: In CHOP the display shows Cho.

    Whatever happens, press MAIN when done; the next steps overwrite group C anyway.

    Why

    In live chop, each pad you press while the sample plays starts a new slice at that moment. What starts playback isn’t documented, so treat this as an experiment.

  2. D2: select the count to chop

    Needs OS 2.0.1 or newer

    In MAIN, tap D, then tap D2. Tap C: group C becomes the destination.

    Check: You hear the count, and D2 is now the selected sample.

    Why

    This chop resets every pad in the destination group, so use the empty group C.

  3. Choose equal-length slices

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Press SHIFT + SAMPLE to enter CHOP. Hold the C button and press − / + until the display shows EQU. Release C.

    Check: The display shows EQU: equal-length slices.

    Why

    The other auto mode, attack, cuts at the loudest moments instead.

  4. Chop into four slices

    Press − / + until the slice count on the display is 4, then press MAIN.

    Check: The MAIN icon is lit again.

    Why

    Going back to MAIN finishes the chop.

  5. Find your four slices

    In MAIN, tap C if it isn’t selected. Tap its pads from the bottom-left one, going right along the bottom row and then up.

    Check: Four pads say “one”, “two”, “three” and “four”.

    Write down which pad says which.

    Why

    Slices fill the pads from bottom left to top right.

  6. Go to 1.1.1 with group C

    Stopped, with group C selected, go to 1.1.1 (hold SHIFT and press −).

    Check: The display shows 1.1.1.

  7. Place the four slices

    Hold RECORD and tap “one” at 1.1.1, “two” at 1.2.1, “three” at 1.3.1 and “four” at 1.4.1. Press PLAY.

    Check: Your voice counts the four beats over your drum kit and the reversed snare.

    Press PLAY to stop.

  8. Remix the count

    Stopped, press briefly: a long hold clears every note of the pad.

    Removes only the “three” note at 1.3.1.

    Go to 1.3.1, briefly press ERASE + the “three” pad, then hold RECORD and tap the “one” pad there. Press PLAY.

    Wrong note? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: The count now goes one, two, one, four.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    Rearranging slices like this is the heart of chopping.

Checkpoint: four pads in group C each hold one word, and your pattern plays them on the beats.

If it doesn’t: slices cut words in half? Record a more evenly spaced count on an empty pad and chop again, or choose attack instead of EQU to cut at the start of each word. Slices landed in the wrong group? Select C before pressing SHIFT + SAMPLE. One slice slightly off? Nudge it on the Part 5 trim page: X start, Y length.

Part 7 · Resample: bounce your beat onto one pad

Goal: record the K.O. II’s own output (resampling), so your whole beat plays from B1, then add a filter sweep on B2.

  1. Stop, and select group B

    Stop playback. Tap B: the empty group B will receive the recording.

    Check: Nothing plays, and group B is selected.

  2. Choose the resample source

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SAMPLE, then press − / + until the display shows rsp (resample; mono and stereo share this label).

    Check: rsp is on the display.

  3. B1: resample one bar

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Do not press − / + now, because that changes how many bars it records.

    Hold SHIFT and press B1: hands-free sampling is engaged and waits. Press PLAY.

    Check: The beat plays and the K.O. II records it.

    If the beat keeps playing afterwards, press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    It records the length of the selected pattern, one bar, about 2.7 seconds, from the pattern start.

  4. Listen to B1

    Press MAIN and tap B1.

    Check: The whole bar plays from a single pad.

    Why

    That is kick, snare, hats, the reversed snare and your counting voice, together.

  5. Commit a new scene: BOUNCE

    Press SHIFT + MAIN to commit, then hold MAIN and write the new scene number down as BOUNCE.

    Check: The BOUNCE number is different from VOICE KIT.

  6. Empty groups A and C here

    In BOUNCE, tap A and press SHIFT + A. Tap C and press SHIFT + A too. Press PLAY.

    Check: Silence, because A and C now play empty patterns here.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    Each group now plays an empty pattern in this scene. Your notes stay safe in the previous scene.

  7. B1: place the bounced beat

    Tap B. In MAIN, stopped, go to 1.1.1, hold RECORD + B1 and release. Press PLAY.

    Check: Your beat is back, but now it is one sample triggered once per bar.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    The K.O. II plays up to 16 mono or 12 stereo sounds at once. A bounced loop uses one instead of several, leaving room for new parts in A and C.

  8. Choose the filter for group B

    Press PLAY, tap B, press FX and press − / + until you reach the filter. Raise the fader.

    Check: Your bounced beat plays from B1.

    Why

    In FX, the fader sets group B’s send: how much of it goes through the effect. The effects are delay, reverb, distortion, chorus, filter and compressor.

  9. Shape the sound with X

    Do not hold FX while pressing a group button: that solos the group.

    Turn X and listen: “turn left to cut the highs and right to cut the lows.”

    Check: Left, the beat sounds muffled; right, it loses its low end.

    Press PLAY to stop.

  10. B2: get ready to resample

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Tap B, press SAMPLE and check that rsp is still the source. Hold SHIFT and press B2.

    Check: rsp shows; hands-free sampling waits for PLAY.

    For more time, press + once or twice now to record more bars, and watch the display.

  11. Record a filter sweep

    Turn only the knobs while holding FX: pads trigger punch-in effects and group buttons solo.

    Press PLAY. While it records, hold FX and turn X slowly from fully left towards the middle, then release FX.

    Check: The beat opens up from muffled to bright.

  12. Listen to B2

    Press MAIN and tap B2.

    Check: The beat opens up from muffled to bright.

  13. Set group B back to dry

    Then press PLAY, tap B, press FX and pull the fader down, so group B plays dry again in the next section.

    Check: The beat plays dry, with no filter.

    Press PLAY to stop.

Checkpoint: B1 plays your whole beat from one pad, and B2 the same beat with a filter sweep.

If it doesn’t: B1 silent or empty? Select the scene with your beat (hold MAIN, − / +), check the source is rsp and try again on an empty pad. Recording too long or too short? You pressed − / + after SHIFT + B1, which sets the number of bars. X changed the input level, not the filter? Hold FX while turning.

Resampling can also turn a chord played in KEYS mode into one sample on one pad.

Part 8 · Fit a loop to any tempo, then keep your samples

Goal: stretch B1 so it follows any tempo, try tempo match and LOOP, then keep your samples safe.

  1. Hear B1 at 110 BPM

    In BOUNCE, press PLAY. Press TEMPO and turn X up until the display shows 110.

    Check: B1 still plays at the speed you recorded it.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    The bar is now shorter than the loop, so the loop’s end is cut off or runs into the next one.

    Not working?

    B1 already follows the tempo? Its time-stretch is on: with B1 selected, the stopwatch TIME icon is lit. The next step shows where to change it.

  2. B1: open the time page

    Press SOUND, tap B, then tap B1. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND and press − / + until the display shows tim.

    Check: The display shows tim.

  3. Stretch B1 to one bar

    Turn X (time stretch mode: BPM, BAR or reverse) until BAR shows. Turn Y (sample length) until the display shows 1 bar.

    Check: The stopwatch TIME icon is now lit.

    Why

    With 1 bar, the sample stretches to fill exactly one bar at any tempo. The stopwatch TIME icon lights while time-stretch is on.

  4. Play it at 110 BPM

    Press MAIN, then PLAY.

    Check: At 110 BPM B1 now fits the bar, faster but in time.

  5. Go back to 90 BPM

    Press TEMPO and turn X back to 90 while it plays: the loop follows. Press PLAY to stop.

    Check: B1 slows down and stays in time.

    Why

    Heavy stretching changes the sound a little. BPM mode works too: choose BPM and set Y to 90, the tempo you recorded at.

  6. Optional: match a song’s tempo

    Optional, tempo match: hold SAMPLE + TEMPO and play a song from your phone into the line input, or close to the mic.

    Check: The display shows the song’s tempo.

    Why

    The K.O. II listens, finds the tempo, shows it and sets the project tempo to match.

  7. Optional: play your stretched loop

    Press PLAY: your stretched B1 follows the new tempo. Set TEMPO back to 90 afterwards.

    Check: B1 plays in time at the matched tempo.

  8. Optional: start a LOOP

    Optional, LOOP, a performance trick: while the beat plays, press SHIFT + TEMPO.

    Check: The K.O. II repeats a short piece of the music.

  9. Optional: move the loop

    Turn X to slide the loop position (it stays locked to the tempo) and Y to change its length.

    Check: The repeated piece moves, still in time.

  10. Optional: leave the loop

    Press MAIN to leave at the next bar, or TEMPO to leave at once.

    Check: The loop ends and the full beat plays on.

    Why

    Chapter 4 · Play & perform has more live tricks.

  11. Write down where your sounds live

    Keep your work: write down project 7, the scene numbers VOICE KIT and BOUNCE, and the sample number FIRST.

    To copy samples to a computer, load audio files or make a backup, use the official EP sample tool over USB-C. Press MAIN to leave SAMPLE mode first: on older OS versions, uploads made in SAMPLE could be corrupted.

    Why

    Your recordings are already stored on the K.O. II.

  12. Your sampling moves

    Read the seven moves below, then try one from memory without reading this app.

    • Sample: tap the group, press SAMPLE, pick the source with − / +; X is the level, Y the threshold. Hold a pad to record.
    • Hands-free: hold SHIFT and press an empty pad; press SAMPLE to stop.
    • Sound edit: SOUND, tap the pad, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND; − / + steps through its pages.
    • Copy a sound: in SOUND, SHIFT + C; tap another pad, SHIFT + D.
    • Time page (tim): X chooses BPM, BAR or rev.
    • Chop: select the sample, tap the destination group, SHIFT + SAMPLE; hold the group button and − / + for EQU.
    • Resample: source rsp, hold SHIFT and press an empty pad, then PLAY.

Checkpoint: B1 stays in time at both 90 and 110 BPM.

If it doesn’t: B1 out of time? The tim page must show BAR, and Y 1 bar. Using BPM mode? Set Y to 90, the tempo you recorded at. Still out of time? Check that you edited B1: press SOUND, tap B1 and open sound edit again.
