CHAPTER 3 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Sample, chop & resample

In Chapters 1 and 2 you built a beat from factory sounds. Now you make the sounds yourself. You will record your voice with the built-in mic, turn mouth noises into a drum kit, sample your phone, shape and reverse a sound, chop a recording into slices and bounce a whole beat onto one pad. Each section is one small project with a check at the end. Take a break between sections whenever you like: your recordings are saved automatically.

You will make: a drum kit from your own voice, a reversed snare, a recording chopped into slices and your whole beat bounced onto one pad, in project 7.

Safety, read this first. Only press SHIFT + ERASE while the K.O. II is already switched on: that opens system settings, which this chapter uses in Parts 3 and 4. Holding SHIFT + ERASE while you switch the unit on does something completely different: it formats the drive and erases all your work and every factory sound, and the official guide warns that factory sounds cannot be recovered. Before you change settings, back up with the official EP sample tool (see Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II). Two more actions are permanent: holding ERASE + SOUND deletes the selected sample from the device, and holding SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page crops a sample file (one optional step below). Work in an empty project so nothing you care about is overwritten.

What you need

Your K.O. II on OS 2.5 or newer; this chapter was checked against the official guide for the current OS 2.5 series (latest release OS 2.5.1). Sample reverse, USB audio, 40-second mono samples, the lo/mid/hi recording rates, equal-length chop, cropping and FX tweaks while resampling arrived in OS 2.5; hands-free sampling and resampling in OS 2.0. Older OS: update first with the official update utility, as described in Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II.

Headphones or a speaker at a comfortable level. For Part 3, optionally a phone with a 3.5 mm stereo cable (or a headphone adapter) for the line input, or a USB-C cable for USB audio.

Finish Chapter 1 · First beat and Chapter 2 · Make it a song first. This chapter reuses their moves without explaining them again: choosing a project, SOUND, placing notes with RECORD + pad, commit with SHIFT + MAIN and undo with SHIFT + B.

Sample your own voice, sounds you make, or audio you are allowed to use. If you publish music containing someone else's recording, check and obtain the necessary permissions first; sampling does not automatically grant rights.

Your pad plan

You work in empty project 7. Each group gets one job, so you always know where a sound lives:

Group A · your drum kit
A1 kick, A2 snare, A3 hat (Part 2), A4 reversed snare (Part 5)
Group B · resampled bounces
B1 your beat on one pad, B2 the same with a filter sweep (Part 7)
Group C · chopped slices
Your spoken count, cut into four slices (Part 6)
Group D · raw recordings
D1 first sample (Part 1), D2 count and D3 phone (Part 3), D4 and D5 the quality test (Part 4)

The official get-started guide suggests keeping group D for loops and samples. Every recording goes into the K.O. II's shared sound library (up to 999 samples or 128 MB); a pad only points to a sample.

Part 1 · Record your first sample

Goal: record a sound with the built-in microphone onto pad D1, hear it back and put it in a beat.

  1. Open empty project 7

    Press MAIN. If the K.O. II is playing, press PLAY to stop it. Hold MAIN, hold pad 7 as well, then release both.

    Why

    The official guide says “hold main and one of the pads (1-9) to select an empty project”, and project 7 is empty on a new unit. Your work from Chapters 1 and 2 in project 6 stays untouched.

  2. Check that project 7 is empty

    Tap a few pads in groups A, B, C and D.

    Check: None of them makes a sound.

  3. Set the tempo to 90 BPM

    Press TEMPO and turn X until the display shows 90 (or hold TEMPO and type 9, 0).

  4. Set the grid to 16 steps

    Press TIMING, turn X until the display shows 16 (not 16T) and press − for quantize. Press MAIN.

    Check: This is the same 90 BPM, 16-step setup as Chapter 1.

    Why

    So every position in this chapter means what you are used to.

  5. Choose the microphone

    Tap D to select group D, then press SAMPLE. Press − or + until the display shows mic.

    Check: The pads light up and blink, which means you are in sample mode.

    Why

    The official icon map’s MIC icon is “lit when the microphone is in use” (LINE-IN lights for a line input). The guide lists the sources as mic, line in mono and stereo (in), resample mono and stereo (rsp), line in left and right (L.IN, R.IN) and USB mono and stereo (usb).

  6. Set the level and threshold

    Check that the pads are still blinking before you turn.

    Turn Y (threshold) all the way down, so recording starts as soon as you hold a pad. Set X (input level) to about halfway.

    Why

    In sample mode X is input level and Y is threshold, which are different jobs from SOUND mode.

  7. D1: record your first sample

    Get close to the K.O. II. Hold D1, say “hey!” or clap once, then release D1.

    Check: D1 stops blinking and stays lit, which means it now holds a recording.

    Why

    The guide: “as soon as you let go it will stop sampling.”

  8. Listen, and set the level

    Tap D1. Too quiet: turn X up. Crunchy or distorted: turn X down.

    Check: You should hear your sound.

    Not working?

    To try again, the guide says you “get a second chance by pressing sample again and retaking it”: press SAMPLE, hold D1 and record again.

  9. Write down the sample number

    Press SOUND and tap D1. Write the number down as FIRST. Press MAIN.

    Check: The display shows a number.

    Why

    That is the library slot where your recording was stored (“samples are stored in the next available slot”). It is saved automatically, and you could load it on any pad in any project by holding SOUND and typing that number.

  10. Go to 1.1.1 with group D

    In MAIN, stopped, with group D selected: hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1.

  11. D1: place it and play

    Hold RECORD, tap D1, release RECORD. Press PLAY.

    Check: Your sound plays at the start of every loop, about every 2.7 seconds.

    Press PLAY to stop.

Mono, stereo and length. A stereo source records left and right; a mono source records one channel. On OS 2.5 a mono sample can be up to 40 seconds long; stereo recordings stop sooner (the update notes offer 40-second mono as the answer to “20 seconds not enough?”). To record a long sample, choose a source the guide marks as mono. The guide gives mic no mono or stereo label; an unofficial cheat sheet for OS 2.5 says the mic records mono, up to 40 seconds. The display’s MONO icon is “lit when a sample is mono”, so it shows which you got. If pressing SAMPLE shows a “disk low” warning, there isn't room for 20 seconds of audio: back up, then remove recordings you no longer need. Holding ERASE + SOUND until SND blinks deletes the selected sample permanently, so check its number first.

Checkpoint: your own sound plays on D1 at the start of every bar.

If it doesn't: D1 still blinks after recording? Nothing was captured: turn Y down, check the mic source and keep holding the pad while you make the sound. D1 lit but silent? Press SOUND, tap D1 and check X (amplitude) isn't at zero; in MAIN, check the fader (group level) isn't at the bottom. Pads already made sounds in step 1? On OS 2.0 and newer, MAIN + a number can also select a scene, so you may still be in your old project: hold MAIN and pad 7 together again until the display shows P 7. See help “My sample will not start recording” in Troubleshooting.

Official guide: 10.1 sample · 7.1 get started, steps 1 and 11–13 · 8.1 sound

Part 2 · Build a drum kit from your voice

Goal: record a kick, a snare and a hi-hat with the mic onto A1, A2 and A3, then program the Chapter 1 rhythm with them. If your unit has no factory sounds, for example after a drive format, this kit is your fallback for the whole course.

  1. Erase the test note

    In MAIN, stopped, with group D selected, go to 1.1.1 and briefly press ERASE + D1.

    Check: D1 no longer lights at 1.1.1 and PLAY stays silent.

    Why

    Erasing a note only removes it from the pattern; your recording stays on the pad and in the library.

  2. Set up group A for sampling

    Tap A, then press SAMPLE. Turn Y (threshold) up a little from the bottom.

    Check: Check that the microphone source is still selected (press − / + if not).

    Why

    Now recording waits until your sound is loud enough, so it starts right on the hit with no silent gap: the guide says threshold lets you “trigger the recording to start only when you make just the right amount of noise”.

  3. A1: record a kick

    Hold A1, make one short, low sound close to the K.O. II, then release A1. Tap A1.

    Check: A short, low thud.

    Why

    For example a deep “dm” or “boom”, or a knock on the table next to it.

    Not working?

    Not punchy enough? Press SAMPLE and retake it.

  4. A2: record a snare

    Hold A2, make one sharp sound, such as “psh”, “k” or a single hand clap, then release A2 and tap it.

    Check: Brighter and snappier than the kick.

  5. A3: record a hat

    Hold A3, make a short “ts” or “t”, release and tap it.

    Check: A short, bright tick.

    Not working?

    If a recording never starts, lower Y a little; if background noise starts it too early, raise Y a little.

  6. Make the kick deeper

    Press SOUND and tap A1. Turn Y (pitch) down a little: your mouth kick gets deeper.

  7. Make the hat quieter

    Tap A3 and turn X (amplitude) down until the hat is quieter than the kick and snare. Press MAIN.

    Check: Kick low and solid, hat in the background.

  8. Go to 1.1.1 with group A

    In MAIN, stopped, with group A selected, hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1.

  9. A1: place the kicks

    Hold RECORD + A1 at 1.1.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.1 and 1.4.3, moving between positions with − / +.

    Check: A1 lights at each of the four positions.

    Why

    The same kick rhythm as Chapter 1.

  10. A2: place the snares

    Hold RECORD + A2 at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1.

    Check: A2 lights at both positions.

  11. A3: place the hats

    Hold RECORD + A3 at 1.1.1, 1.1.3, 1.2.1, 1.2.3, 1.3.1, 1.3.3, 1.4.1 and 1.4.3.

    Check: A3 lights at all eight positions.

    Why

    Some share a step with a kick or snare, which is correct.

  12. Listen to your voice kit

    Press PLAY, listen to a few loops and press PLAY to stop.

  13. Commit it as VOICE KIT

    Hold MAIN, read the scene number and write it down as VOICE KIT. Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once to commit.

    Check: Holding MAIN now shows a different number.

    Why

    You are working in a copy, and VOICE KIT stays as it is.

Checkpoint: kick, snare and hats made from your own voice, in the Chapter 1 rhythm: kick on 1, the “and” of 2, 3 and the “and” of 4, snare on 2 and 4, hats on every eighth.

If it doesn't: a sound starts late? There is silence at the start of the recording: Part 5 trims it, or retake with Y slightly higher. Extra or missing hits? Press SHIFT + B (undo) while the umbrella icon is lit, or go to the wrong position and briefly press ERASE + that pad, as in Chapter 1.

Official guide: 10.1 sample (threshold) · 8.1 sound (amplitude, pitch) · 9.2 step sequence

Part 3 · Hands-free sampling and sampling your phone

Goal: record without holding a pad, then capture a few seconds from your phone, either through the line input or over USB. Do the line-input steps or the USB steps, whichever suits your phone; both end with a phone sample on D3.

  1. Set up hands-free sampling

    Tap D and press SAMPLE; check the mic source. Leave Y slightly up so the recording waits for your voice.

  2. D2: start hands-free sampling

    Hold SHIFT and press D2, an empty, blinking pad.

    Why

    The K.O. II now “holds the pad for you”.

  3. Count to four, then stop

    Count “one, two, three, four” evenly and briskly, about two words per second, then press SAMPLE straight after “four” to stop.

    Check: D2 stays lit; tap it to hear your count.

    Why

    You will chop this in Part 6, so the spacing matters.

    Not working?

    Uneven? Record it again on an empty pad such as D6 and use that pad instead of D2.

  4. Connect your phone to the line input

    Line input: connect your phone's headphone output (or its headphone adapter) to the K.O. II's audio INPUT with a 3.5 mm stereo cable.

    The input sits with the other sockets on the top side; do not use OUTPUT, MIDI or SYNC.

  5. Set the phone’s volume to half

    Set the phone's volume to about half.

  6. Choose what to sample

    Choose 2–8 seconds of something you made or may use, such as a voice memo or your own loop.

  7. Select the line input

    Tap D, press SAMPLE and press − / + until the display shows in. Leave Y slightly up.

    Why

    That is the line input: the mono and stereo versions share this label, and either is fine here.

  8. D3: record the phone, hands-free

    Hold SHIFT and press D3, start the phone, and press SAMPLE when your part has finished. Tap D3.

    Check: You hear the phone's sound, clean and undistorted.

    Not working?

    Distorted: turn X down and record again on an empty pad. Too quiet: raise X or the phone's volume.

  9. Connect over USB audio (OS 2.5)

    Connect the K.O. II to the phone or a computer with a USB-C cable.

    Why

    This is USB audio (OS 2.5), for example if your phone has no headphone output.

  10. Choose the K.O. II as audio output

    On the phone or computer, choose the K.O. II as the audio output device.

    Why

    The guide says any “class compliant usb audio host” works.

  11. Optional: hear the phone while sampling

    With the K.O. II already on.

    Press SHIFT + ERASE to open system settings. Type 5, 1, 1 on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Type 510 the same way to switch back.

    Why

    Optional, to hear the phone through the K.O. II while you sample. Setting 511 is “sampler + live monitoring”; the default, 510, is “sampler only”.

  12. Select USB as the source

    Tap D, press SAMPLE and press − / + until the display shows usb (mono and stereo share this label). Leave Y slightly up.

  13. D3: record over USB, hands-free

    Hold SHIFT and press D3, start the audio on the phone and press SAMPLE when it has finished. Tap the pad.

    Check: You hear the phone's sound from the K.O. II.

    Not working?

    If D3 already holds a line-in sample, use the next empty pad.

Checkpoint: D2 plays your evenly spaced count; D3 plays a few clean seconds from your phone.

If it doesn't: hands-free didn't start? Use a pad that is still blinking; hands-free needs an empty pad and OS 2.0 or newer. If you forget to stop, it stops by itself at the maximum length. Line input: the cable must be in INPUT, the phone's volume up, the source in, X up and Y not too high. USB: the phone or computer must use the K.O. II as its output and the source must be usb; USB audio needs OS 2.5. See help “Where did USB or reverse go?” in Troubleshooting.

Part 4 · Recording quality: lo versus hi

Goal: record the same bright sound at the default hi rate and at lo, hear the difference, then set it back.

  1. Choose the mic again

    Tap D, press SAMPLE and choose the mic source.

    Why

    The K.O. II records at hi quality by default (46,875 Hz, setting 502).

  2. D4: record keys at hi quality

    Hold D4, jingle a bunch of keys close to the K.O. II for about two seconds, then release.

    Check: D4 is lit, and tapping it plays bright jingling.

  3. Set the lo recording rate (500)

    With the K.O. II already on.

    Press SHIFT + ERASE to open system settings. Type 5, 0, 0 on the pads, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Why

    Setting 500 is the lo rate, 26,250 Hz. Menu route instead of the code: SHIFT + ERASE, press + until the display shows smp, press ENTER, rec appears, press ENTER again, use − / + to select lo, then press ENTER.

  4. D5: record the keys again at lo

    Tap D, press SAMPLE, choose the mic, hold D5 and jingle the same keys the same way. Release.

    Check: D5 is lit.

  5. Compare hi and lo

    Tap D4, then D5, several times.

    Check: D5 (lo) sounds duller and grittier, and the sparkle of the keys is softer.

    Curious about the middle? Type 501 the same way for mid (32,000 Hz) and record a third take on an empty pad.

    Why

    The guide's reason to use it: “add character and use less storage”.

  6. Set the rate back to hi (502)

    Set it back: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 5, 0, 2, press ENTER, then press MAIN.

    Check: You are back on 502.

    Why

    The setting only affects new recordings, so D5 keeps its lo-fi sound. Otherwise every sample you record from now on is lo-fi.

Checkpoint: D4 and D5 sound clearly different: D5 is darker and rougher.

If it doesn't: use a bright sound such as keys or a long “sss”, because dull sounds hide the difference. To see the current rate, use the menu route (smp, then rec) and read the display. Typed a wrong code? Type the right one and press ENTER; every code is listed on the official system page. Lower rates need OS 2.5. Audio files you import with the EP sample tool keep their own rate if it is lower than 46,875 Hz.

Official guide: what's new, OS 2.5 (sample rate) · 14 system settings 500–502 · 10.2 sample tool

Part 5 · Shape and reverse a sound in SOUND EDIT

Goal: tidy your snare with trim, envelope and play mode, then make a reversed copy that swells into the next bar.

  1. A2: open sound edit

    Always press SHIFT + SOUND briefly: held, it saves your edits, and on the trim page it crops the file.

    Press SOUND, tap A, then tap A2 (your snare). Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND: sound edit opens.

  2. Step through the six pages

    Press − / + to step through its six pages: sound (play mode and pan), trim, envelope, time, midi and mute group.

    Check: The display changes with each page.

    Why

    The guide calls the trim page TRI and the time page tim.

  3. Trim the start of A2

    Go to the trim page. Turn X (start point) a little at a time, tapping A2 between turns, until the snare starts the instant you tap.

    Hold SHIFT while turning for finer steps.

  4. Shorten the tail

    Turn Y (length) down until the tail ends cleanly.

    Check: A2 is quick and tight.

    Why

    Trim changes how the sound plays in this project only; it does not write back to the stored file.

  5. Try the attack, then undo it

    Press + to the envelope page. Turn X (attack) up and tap A2: the snare fades in and loses its crack. Turn X back down.

    Check: A2 has its crack back.

    Why

    Y is release: whether the sound keeps playing after you let go of the pad or is cut off at once.

  6. Set A2 to oneshot

    Press − until you reach the sound page. Turn X to oneshot. Tap A2 very briefly.

    Check: The whole snare plays even though you let go at once.

    Why

    The guide names the three play modes oneshot, key and legato, so check the display as you turn. Oneshot plays the whole sample, one at a time; key lets copies of the sample overlap (good for KEYS); legato continues from the same point when you change notes while holding. Y on this page is pan.

  7. Copy A2’s sound

    Press SHIFT + C only once; pressing it twice copies every sound in the group.

    Press MAIN, then press SOUND and tap A2. Hold SHIFT and press C once: this copies A2's sound.

  8. Paste it onto A4

    Tap A4, an empty pad, then hold SHIFT and press D: this pastes it. Tap A4.

    Check: A4 plays your snare too.

  9. A4: open the time page

    With A4 selected in SOUND, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, then press − / + until the display shows tim.

  10. Reverse A4

    Turn X until it shows rev. Tap A4.

    Check: The snare plays backwards, a soft swell that ends in a snap.

    Tap A2: it still plays forwards.

  11. A4: place the reversed snare

    Press MAIN. Stopped, with group A selected, go to 1.4.3, hold RECORD + A4 and release. Press PLAY.

    Check: The reversed snare swells and its loud end lands as the loop starts again.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Not working?

    Too early or too late? Briefly press ERASE + A4 at 1.4.3 and try 1.4.2 or 1.4.4 instead.

  12. Optional: set the kick’s crop points

    Optional and permanent: skip this if you are unsure.

    Press SOUND, tap A1, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, go to the trim page and set X and Y so only the kick remains.

  13. Optional: crop the kick

    Only crop a recording you made today, never a factory sound.

    Then hold SHIFT + SOUND.

    Cropping rewrites the sample file to its trim points; the guide says it “will overwrite the file and cannot be undone”.

    Because the file itself changes, every pad in every project that uses this sample plays the cropped version from now on.

Checkpoint: A2 is tight, and A4 swells backwards into the start of each loop.

If it doesn't: turning X or Y changed volume or pitch? You were in SOUND, not sound edit: press SHIFT + SOUND briefly. No rev on the tim page? Reverse needs OS 2.5. Don't like an edit? Turn the knob back: trim, envelope and play mode are not permanent. Did A2 also play backwards? Check that A4 was selected (tap it in SOUND) before you opened sound edit.

Part 6 · Chop a sample into slices

Goal: cut your count on D2 into four equal slices on group C, then make a new pattern from the slices.

  1. Optional: try live chop

    In MAIN tap D, tap D2, tap C, press SHIFT + SAMPLE and tap C pads in time with the words.

    Whatever happens, press MAIN when done; the next steps overwrite group C anyway.

    Why

    Optional first look at manual chopping, which the guide calls live chop: in CHOP mode you “press a pad to CHOP… keep pressing pads as the sample plays”, and the time between presses decides where each cut falls. The guide’s picture shows Cho on the display and the first pad pressed at the very start of the sample. It doesn’t say what starts playback, so treat this as an experiment.

  2. D2: select the count to chop

    In MAIN, tap D, then tap D2. Tap C: group C becomes the destination.

    Check: You hear the count, and D2 is now the selected sample.

    Why

    Chopping from one group into another needs OS 2.0.1 or newer. Auto chop “will reset any pad assignments” in the destination group, which is why you use the empty group C.

  3. Choose equal-length slices

    Press SHIFT + SAMPLE to enter CHOP. Hold the C button and press − / + until the display shows EQU. Release C.

    Why

    EQU means equal-length slices; the other auto mode, attack, cuts at the loudest moments.

  4. Chop into four slices

    Press − / + until the slice count on the display is 4, then press MAIN.

    Why

    The guide: “go back to (main) and your sample has been chopped.”

  5. Find your four slices

    In MAIN, tap C if it isn’t selected. Tap its pads from the bottom-left one, going right along the bottom row and then up.

    Check: Four pads say “one”, “two”, “three” and “four”.

    Write down which pad says which.

    Why

    Slices fill the pads “from bottom left to top right”.

  6. Go to 1.1.1 with group C

    Stopped, with group C selected, go to 1.1.1 (hold SHIFT and press −).

  7. Place the four slices

    Hold RECORD and tap “one” at 1.1.1, “two” at 1.2.1, “three” at 1.3.1 and “four” at 1.4.1. Press PLAY.

    Check: Your voice counts the four beats over your drum kit and the reversed snare.

    Press PLAY to stop.

  8. Remix the count

    Remix it: go to 1.3.1, briefly press ERASE + the “three” pad, then hold RECORD and tap the “one” pad there. Press PLAY.

    Check: The count now goes one, two, one, four.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    Rearranging slices like this is the heart of chopping.

Checkpoint: four pads in group C each hold one word, and your pattern plays them on the beats.

If it doesn't: slices cut words in half? Your count wasn't evenly spaced. Record a new, more even count on an empty pad and chop again, or choose attack mode instead of EQU, which cuts at the loudest moments, the start of each word. To nudge a single slice, try the trim page from Part 5 on that pad (X start, Y length). Slices landed in the wrong group? Select C before pressing SHIFT + SAMPLE. EQU missing? Equal-length chop needs OS 2.5.

Official guide: 10.3 chop · what's new, OS 2.5 (equal length auto chop) and OS 2.0.1 (chop from one group to another)

Part 7 · Resample: bounce your beat onto one pad

Goal: record the K.O. II's own output onto B1 so the whole beat plays from one pad, then capture a filter sweep while resampling.

  1. Stop, and select group B

    Stop playback. Tap B: the empty group B will receive the recording.

  2. Choose the resample source

    Press SAMPLE, then press − / + until the display shows rsp (resample; mono and stereo share this label).

    Check: rsp is on the display.

  3. B1: resample one bar

    Do not press − / + now, because that changes how many bars it records.

    Hold SHIFT and press B1: hands-free sampling is engaged and waits. Press PLAY.

    Check: The beat plays and the K.O. II records it.

    If the beat keeps playing afterwards, press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    It records the length of the selected pattern, one bar, about 2.7 seconds, from the pattern start.

  4. Listen to B1

    Press MAIN and tap B1.

    Check: The whole bar plays from a single pad.

    Why

    That is kick, snare, hats, the reversed snare and your counting voice, together.

  5. Commit a new scene: BOUNCE

    Press SHIFT + MAIN to commit, then hold MAIN and write the new scene number down as BOUNCE.

  6. Empty groups A and C here

    In BOUNCE, tap A and press SHIFT + A. Tap C and press SHIFT + A too. Press PLAY.

    Check: Silence, because A and C now play empty patterns here.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    Group A switches to its next empty pattern in this scene. Your notes are safe in the previous scene.

  7. B1: place the bounced beat

    Tap B. In MAIN, stopped, go to 1.1.1, hold RECORD + B1 and release. Press PLAY.

    Check: Your beat is back, but now it is one sample triggered once per bar.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    The K.O. II plays up to 16 mono or 12 stereo sounds at once; a bounced loop needs one of them instead of several, which leaves room for new parts in groups A and C.

  8. Choose the filter for group B

    Press PLAY, tap B, press FX and press − / + until you reach the filter. Raise the fader.

    Why

    This sets up an effect for group B. The guide lists delay, reverb, distortion, chorus, filter and compressor. In FX it sets group B's send.

  9. Shape the sound with X

    Do not hold FX while pressing a group button: that solos the group.

    Turn X and listen: “turn left to cut the highs and right to cut the lows.”

    Press PLAY to stop.

  10. B2: get ready to resample

    Tap B, press SAMPLE and check that rsp is still the source. Hold SHIFT and press B2.

    For more time, press + once or twice now to record more bars, and watch the display.

  11. Record a filter sweep

    Turn only the knobs while holding FX: pads trigger punch-in effects and group buttons solo.

    Press PLAY. While it records, hold FX and turn X slowly from fully left towards the middle, then release FX.

  12. Listen to B2

    Press MAIN and tap B2.

    Check: The beat opens up from muffled to bright.

  13. Set group B back to dry

    Then press PLAY, tap B, press FX and pull the fader down, so group B plays dry again in the next section.

    Press PLAY to stop.

Checkpoint: B1 plays your whole beat from one pad, and B2 the same beat with a filter sweep.

If it doesn't: B1 silent or empty? rsp wasn't the source, or nothing was playing: select the scene with your beat (hold MAIN, − / +) and try again on an empty pad. Recording too long or too short? You pressed − / + after SHIFT + B1, which sets the number of bars. X changed the input level instead of the filter? Hold FX while turning. Resampling and hands-free sampling need OS 2.0; turning FX X/Y during latched resampling needs OS 2.5.

Resampling can also combine sounds: the official how-to resamples a chord played in KEYS mode onto one pad, and the guide notes resample can “stack and time stretch whole sections of your beat”.

Part 8 · Fit a loop to any tempo, then keep your samples

Goal: make B1 follow the project tempo with time-stretch, try tempo match and LOOP, and learn how to keep your samples safe.

  1. Hear B1 at 110 BPM

    In BOUNCE, press PLAY. Press TEMPO and turn X up until the display shows 110.

    Check: B1 still plays at the speed you recorded it.

    Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    So it no longer fits the shorter bar; the end of the loop gets cut off or runs into the next one.

    Not working?

    (If B1 already follows the tempo, its time-stretch is already on: with B1 selected, the stopwatch TIME icon, which “indicates if active sound has time stretch enabled”, is lit. The next step shows where to change it.)

  2. B1: open the time page

    Press SOUND, tap B, then tap B1. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND and press − / + until the display shows tim.

  3. Stretch B1 to one bar

    Turn X (time stretch mode: BPM, BAR or reverse) until BAR shows. Turn Y (sample length) until the display shows 1 bar.

    Why

    The guide: “if you choose 1 bar it stretches your sample to be 1 bar long.” The mode can also be off: teenage engineering’s factory sounds are stored with "time.mode":"off". The stopwatch TIME icon “indicates if active sound has time stretch enabled”, so watch it as you turn X.

  4. Play it at 110 BPM

    Press MAIN, then PLAY.

    Check: At 110 BPM B1 now fits the bar, faster but in time.

  5. Go back to 90 BPM

    Press TEMPO and turn X back to 90 while it plays: the loop follows. Press PLAY to stop.

    Why

    Heavy stretching changes the sound a little (OS 2.5 made it cleaner), and stretched samples can use more of the K.O. II's voices. The BPM mode works too: choose BPM and set Y to 90, the tempo you recorded the loop at.

  6. Optional: match a song’s tempo

    Optional, tempo match: hold SAMPLE + TEMPO and play a song from your phone into the line input, or close to the mic.

    Why

    The guide: the K.O. II “will listen, find the tempo, display it on screen and then set the project tempo to match”.

  7. Optional: play your stretched loop

    Press PLAY: your stretched B1 follows the new tempo. Set TEMPO back to 90 afterwards.

  8. Optional: start a LOOP

    Optional, LOOP, a performance trick: while the beat plays, press SHIFT + TEMPO.

    Check: The K.O. II repeats a short piece of the music.

  9. Optional: move the loop

    Turn X to slide the loop position (it stays locked to the tempo) and Y to change its length.

  10. Optional: leave the loop

    Press MAIN to leave at the next bar, or TEMPO to leave at once.

    Why

    Chapter 4 · Play & perform has more live tricks.

  11. Write down where your sounds live

    Keep your work: write down project 7, the scene numbers VOICE KIT and BOUNCE, and the sample number FIRST.

    Check: You know where each of your sounds lives.

    To copy them to a computer, load audio files onto the K.O. II or make a backup, use the official EP sample tool over USB-C. Press MAIN to leave SAMPLE mode before transferring: older OS versions could corrupt uploads made while in SAMPLE.

    Why

    Your recordings are already stored on the K.O. II.

Checkpoint: B1 stays in time at both 90 and 110 BPM.

If it doesn't: the tim page must show BAR (not BPM or rev) and Y must be 1 bar. Using BPM mode instead? Y must match the tempo you recorded at, 90. Still out of time? Check that you edited B1: press SOUND and tap B1, then open sound edit again. The tempo knob turns from 60 to 180 BPM; hold TEMPO and type a number for anything outside that.
