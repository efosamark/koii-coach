CHAPTER 7 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Vocal house

House with your own vocal samples

Build a 4-minute house track at 128 BPM from factory drums and bass, plus five short vocal samples you record or upload yourself. This page calls them V1 to V5. The voice gives the track its identity: a hook, an answer, tiny chops, a long texture and a second phrase. The drums and bass keep people moving. You build the biggest section first, then copy it and take things away to make the quieter sections. In one live-played moment you bring the kick back in yourself. The finished song list has 32 positions of four bars: 128 bars, exactly 4:00.

You will make: a 4-minute house track at 128 BPM built around five vocal samples of your own: 32 song positions, 128 bars.

Before you start. Written for OS 2.5.1. Older OS: update first (Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II). Song mode needs OS 2.0 or newer; USB audio, reverse and crop need OS 2.5. This chapter builds on Chapter 1 · First beat, Chapter 2 · Make it a song (scenes, commit, song list) and Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample, and doesn’t teach them again. The app can’t see your K.O. II, so trust your display and your ears.
Moves you’ll use all chapter. Stop playback before you edit positions. In stopped MAIN, − / + moves one step and SHIFT + − / + jumps a whole bar, but only while RECORD is not armed: when it is armed, SHIFT + + doubles the pattern instead. Don’t hold a pad while you do this, because SHIFT + pad + − / + nudges that pad’s note. Write a note: hold RECORD, tap the pad, release. Remove one note: go to it and briefly press ERASE + that pad. Remove every note of one pad in this pattern: with playback stopped, hold ERASE + that pad until TRK blinks. Undo: SHIFT + B while the umbrella icon is lit.
Never use ERASE + SOUND: held until SND blinks, it deletes the sample from the device.

What you need

Your K.O. II on OS 2.5.1, headphones or speakers, and paper and a pencil for project, scene and sample numbers.

Five short vocal sounds from one of these sources: your own voice through the built-in mic; a phone or laptop headphone output into the K.O. II’s 3.5 mm input; a phone or computer connected by USB-C as a USB audio source (OS 2.5); or audio files on a computer, sent with the official EP sample tool.

Use your own voice, or recordings you have permission to use.

Sound roles

The same roles as in Learn: A drums, B bass, D your own samples. Group C stays empty.

A1
Kick (factory 1–99)
A2
Clap or snare (100–199)
A3
Closed hat (200–299)
A4
Open hat (200–299) or percussion (300–399)
B1
Bass (400–499)
D1 · V1
Main hook: two to four words, under about 1.9 s (one bar at 128 BPM)
D2 · V2
Short answer: one word, about half a second
D3 · V3
Tiny chop: one syllable or a breath, about 0.2 s
D4 · V4
Texture: a long hum, “ooh” or breath, 2–4 s
D5 · V5
Second phrase: a variation of the hook, under about 1.9 s

A dance track needs a clear low end and a clear voice. Let B1 carry the bass and keep the vocals above it. The voice is the feature, not the kick, clap or hat.

Part 1 · Set up an empty 128 BPM project

Goal: a project with no notes in it, at 128 BPM with a 16-step grid, ready for your own sounds.

  1. Choose a project for this chapter

    Is one of projects 6–9 still empty, because you skipped a chapter? Use that one. Otherwise reuse the project you need least.

    For most people that is 7, the Chapter 3 practice project.

    Why

    This chapter needs a project of its own. A new unit has four empty projects, 6–9, and if you followed the course they now hold your work: 6 = Chapters 1, 2 and 4, 7 = Chapter 3, 8 = Chapter 5 and 9 = Chapter 6.

  2. Reusing one? Back it up first

    Back it up first with the official EP sample tool (Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II, Part 4).

    Your recordings stay in the sound library either way.

    Why

    The official product page says you can “back up and restore individual projects”.

  3. Open the project

    Press MAIN. If music is playing, press PLAY to stop it. Then hold MAIN, hold the project’s pad as well, and release both.

  4. Set the tempo to 128 BPM

    Press TEMPO and turn X until the display shows 128. Press MAIN.

    Why

    At 128 BPM one bar lasts 1.875 seconds and a four-bar loop 7.5 seconds.

  5. Set the grid to 16

    Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 16, not 1, 4 or 16T.

  6. Quantize on, swing alone

    Press − to choose quantize. Leave Y (swing) alone. Press MAIN.

  7. Listen: is the project empty?

    Press PLAY. Silence means an empty project: press PLAY again to stop and go on.

    Music? Press PLAY to stop and don’t erase anything yet: first make sure you are in the project you chose. If you hear your Chapter 6 tech house track, your Chapter 5 hip-hop beat or your Chapter 1–2 beat, MAIN + the pad switched scene instead of project: go back to step 3.

  8. Old music? Clear the old patterns

    Only when you hear the reused project’s own old music, and only after you have backed it up.

    Hold ERASE and A until PTN blinks on the display, and do the same with B, C and D.

    Wrong project? Press SHIFT + B at once while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY again: now you should hear nothing.

    In project 7, that is your Chapter 3 voice kit or the bounced beat. Or restore your backup. Stop.

    Why

    The official guide says this erases “the current patterns”.

  9. Reused project 7? Empty D1 to D5

    Empty them first, with playback stopped: press SOUND, tap D, tap D1, hold SOUND, type 0, 0, 0 and release SOUND. Repeat for D2 to D5.

    A reused project keeps its pad sounds and pad settings; Parts 2 and 4 put new sounds on every pad this chapter uses. If one of them later plays backwards, stretched or cut short (Chapter 3 leaves A4 reversed and B1 stretched to one bar), check that pad in SOUND EDIT, as in Chapter 3, Parts 5 and 8. The Chapter 3 recordings stay in the sound library.

    Why

    Reused project 7? Its D1 to D5 still hold your Chapter 3 recordings, so they won’t blink in SAMPLE, and hands-free sampling is not allowed on a pad that already holds a recording. The official how-to says setting a pad to “000” “will create an empty pad”.

  10. Write the project and V1 to V5

    On your paper, write the project number (for example 7 = VOCAL HOUSE), then V1 to V5 with a blank beside each for a sample number.

  11. Write the scene names

    Below that, write the scene names DROP A, DROP B, PEAK, GROOVE, INTRO, BREAK and BUILD, each with a blank for its scene number.

Checkpoint: PLAY plays nothing, TEMPO shows 128 and TIMING shows 16.

If it doesn’t: PLAY still plays music after step 7? On OS 2.0 and newer, MAIN + a number can also switch scenes, so you may have changed scene instead of project. Hold MAIN first, then hold the project’s pad as well, check your display, then repeat step 3 before step 7. If you already erased patterns in the wrong project, press SHIFT + B at once while the umbrella is lit, or restore that project from your backup. Not sure which project to give up? See help “My project is not empty” in Troubleshooting. A reused project may also keep old song positions; Part 13 shows how to remove extra ones.

Part 2 · Get your five vocals onto D1–D5

Goal: V1 to V5 on pads D1 to D5 of this project, with each sample’s number on your paper. The official guide suggests group D “for loops and samples”. Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample teaches sampling in depth; this is the short version.

  1. Plan your five vocals

    Decide what V1 to V5 will be. Keep V1 and V5 under about 1.9 seconds, so each fits in one bar, and keep V3 tiny.

    See “Sound roles” above. Your own voice is perfect: sing, hum, speak or whisper. Using someone else’s recording? Read the rights note in Part 14 first.

  2. D: open SAMPLE

    Press MAIN and tap D. Press SAMPLE.

    Check: The lights blink while you are in SAMPLE.

  3. Choose the source

    Press − / + to choose the source.

    mic is the built-in microphone. in is the 3.5 mm input, for example a phone’s headphone output connected with a cable. usb (OS 2.5) is a phone or computer connected by USB-C, with the K.O. II chosen as its audio output.

    Why

    Mono is fine for a voice; on OS 2.5 a mono sample can be up to 40 seconds long.

  4. Set the input level

    While you sing or play the vocal, turn X (input level) up until it is loud but clean, never distorted.

  5. Set the threshold low

    Turn Y (threshold) low, so recording starts as soon as you make a sound.

  6. D1: record V1

    Get ready, then press and hold pad D1 (pad 1, with D selected). Perform V1, and release just after the last word.

    Check: The pad stops blinking and stays lit: it now has a sample.

    Need both hands? In SAMPLE, SHIFT + an empty, blinking pad starts hands-free recording, and pressing SAMPLE stops it.

  7. Listen to D1

    Press MAIN and tap D1 to hear it.

    A little silence before and after is fine; you trim it in Part 3.

  8. D2 to D5: record V2 to V5

    Press SAMPLE again, with D still selected. Record V2 on D2, V3 on D3, V4 on D4 and V5 on D5 the same way.

  9. Listen to D2 to D5

    Check each one in MAIN.

    Not working?

    A bad take? Record that pad again. The pad gets the new take, and the old one stays in the sound library.

  10. Audio files? Transfer them

    Only if your vocals are audio files: connect the K.O. II to a computer by USB-C, open teenage.engineering/apps/ep-sample-tool and transfer the five files.

    Recorded on the K.O. II? Tick this step and move on.

  11. Load each file onto its pad

    Then, for each vocal: press SOUND, tap D, tap its pad, hold SOUND, type the vocal’s sample number on the pads and release SOUND.

    D1 for V1 and so on.

    Not working?

    Not sure of a number? Select the pad in SOUND and press − / + until you hear the vocal (SHIFT + − / + jumps ten numbers).

  12. Vocals already on pads?

    Only if your vocals are already on pads, for example from Chapter 3: first write down each vocal’s number.

  13. Read each number, then come back

    In that project, press SOUND, select the pad and read the display. Then come back to this project (hold MAIN, then hold its pad as well).

  14. Load D1 to D5 by number

    Load the vocals onto D1 to D5 by number, as for audio files above.

    Why

    Loading or pasting a sound only changes which sample a pad plays; the sample stays in the library.

  15. Or copy a sound inside one project

    Always paste onto an empty pad, so no vocal is replaced before it has been copied.

    In SOUND, select the source pad and press SHIFT + C, then select an empty pad and press SHIFT + D.

    Why

    Inside one project you can also copy a sound.

  16. Write down the sample numbers

    Press SOUND, tap D, then tap D1. Write the number beside V1. Do the same for D2 to D5.

    Check: The display shows a number.

    Why

    The official guide says “this is the number of the sample for the corresponding pad”. With these numbers you can always put a vocal back: select the pad, hold SOUND, type the number and release.

Checkpoint: in MAIN with D selected, pads D1 to D5 play V1 to V5, and five sample numbers are on your paper.

If it doesn’t: recording never starts? Lower Y (threshold), raise X and check the source on the display. Recording from a phone or computer over USB? Choose the K.O. II as its audio output first. A pad is silent? Record it again, or load the sample by number. No vocals yet? To learn the recipe first, load five factory melodic sounds (500–599) onto D1 to D5 as stand-ins, and swap your vocals in later by number.

Part 3 · Trim the vocals and make them play in full

Goal: each vocal starts right on its first sound, ends cleanly and always plays to the end.

Briefly pressing SHIFT + SOUND opens sound edit, which is safe. Never hold SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page: on OS 2.5 that crops the file for good (the guide: it will “permanently trim the file on disk”). Ordinary trimming can always be changed back.

  1. D1: open the trim page

    Press SOUND, tap D and tap D1. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit, then press − / + to step through its pages to trim.

    The guide lists six pages: sound, trim, envelope, time, midi and mute group, so check your display.

  2. Trim the start of V1

    Turn X (start) a little at a time, tapping D1 between turns, until playback begins just before the first sound. Don’t cut into the first word.

  3. Trim the end of V1

    Stay on trim. Turn Y (length) down until playback ends just after the last word. Press MAIN when it sounds right.

    For smaller steps, hold SHIFT while you turn, and keep your fingers off SOUND while SHIFT is down.

  4. Trim D2 to D5

    Trim D2 to D5 the same way.

    V2: one word, about half a second. V3: one syllable or breath, about 0.2 seconds. V4: long and smooth. V5: just the phrase.

  5. D1: set oneshot

    Select D1 in SOUND, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND and press − / + to reach the sound page. Turn X until the display shows oneshot.

    Why

    The guide says oneshot “plays the whole sample, one at a time”, so a single trigger plays the whole vocal.

  6. D2 to D5: oneshot too

    Repeat for D2 to D5.

  7. Optional: reverse V4 (OS 2.5)

    Select D4, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, press + until the display shows tim, then turn X until it shows rev. Tap D4.

    Don’t like it? Turn X back to where it was.

    Check: It plays backwards.

    Check its trim again afterwards.

    Why

    Optional, OS 2.5: turn V4 into a reverse swell.

  8. Set the vocal levels

    Press SOUND for plain SOUND (not sound edit). Tap each D pad and set X (amplitude). Leave Y (pitch) alone so the voice stays natural.

    Check: D1 and D5 clear, D2 a little quieter, D3 and D4 low.

    Why

    You balance against the drums in Part 9.

Checkpoint: in MAIN, one short tap on each of D1 to D5 plays the whole vocal, starting on its first sound with no silence in front.

If it doesn’t: starts late? Turn trim X a little further. Stops early? Check oneshot on the sound page, then trim Y. A vocal sounds higher or lower than before? You turned Y in plain SOUND; turn it back. Cropped one by mistake? Record it again. A crop can’t be undone.

Part 4 · Choose the drum kit and the bass

Goal: four factory drum sounds on A1 to A4 and one factory bass on B1.

  1. A1: load sample 001

    Press SOUND, tap A, tap A1. Hold SOUND, type 1 on the pads and release SOUND to load sample 001. Tap A1.

    Want a different kick? Press − / + within 1–99, the factory kicks (SHIFT + − / + jumps ten numbers). Choose a short, punchy one.

  2. A2: a clap or snare

    Tap A2 and choose a clap or snare: the factory snares are 100–199 (hold SOUND, type 1, 0, 0).

    No clap you like there? Try percussion, 300–399.

  3. A3: a closed hat

    Tap A3 and choose a short closed hat from 200–299.

  4. A4: an open hat

    Tap A4 and choose an open hat (200–299) or a shaker-like percussion sound (300–399). Turn X down so it is quieter than the kick and clap.

  5. B1: choose a bass

    Tap B, then B1. Hold SOUND, type 4, 0, 0 and release, then press − / + within 400–499 for a short, round bass.

  6. Turn the bass down

    Turn X down so it sits under the kick.

  7. Play the kit

    Press MAIN. Tap A and play A1, A2, A3 and A4; tap B and play B1.

Checkpoint: you hear kick, clap, closed hat, open hat and bass, all factory sounds, while D1 to D5 still play your vocals.

If it doesn’t: a vocal plays on an A or B pad? You loaded the sound with D selected. Tap the right group, select the pad and load the factory number again. Your vocal is safe in the library: put it back on its D pad by number.

Part 5 · Write the four-on-the-floor bar

Goal: one bar of house drums in group A.

  1. Go to 1.1.1 with group A

    Press MAIN, tap A and make sure playback is stopped. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1.

  2. A1: the first kick

    Hold RECORD, tap A1, then release RECORD.

    Check: The kick is written on the first step.

  3. A1: a kick on every beat

    Press + until the display shows 1.2.1 and write A1 the same way. Repeat at 1.3.1 and 1.4.1.

    Why

    A kick on every beat is the “four on the floor”.

  4. A2: claps on 2 and 4

    Write A2 at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1, beats 2 and 4.

  5. A3: off-beat hats

    Write A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3, the “and” between the beats. Then press PLAY, listen to a few loops and press PLAY to stop.

Checkpoint: boom, tss, clap, tss on repeat. A kick on every beat, a clap on 2 and 4, and a closed hat between the kicks.

If it doesn’t: a sound plays only while you tap it? You didn’t hold RECORD while tapping. A hit is missing? Go to its position and write it again. An extra hit? Go to it and briefly press ERASE + that pad, or press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit. The display shows a sample number instead of a position? You are still in SOUND: press MAIN.

Part 6 · Make the drums four bars long

Goal: a four-bar drum loop with small changes at the end of bars 2 and 4.

  1. Double A to two bars

    With A selected and playback stopped, tap RECORD to arm it. Hold SHIFT and press + once.

    Check: The pattern doubles to two bars, and bar 2 is a copy of bar 1.

  2. Double again to four bars

    Still armed, hold SHIFT and press + once more: four bars. Tap RECORD to disarm.

    Check: Check your display: the pattern length should now be 4 bars.

  3. A3: two hat accents

    Disarmed and stopped, press SHIFT + + to jump to bar 2, then + until the display shows 2.2.4, and write A3. Do the same at 4.2.4.

  4. A4: open hats instead of closed

    Write A4 at 2.4.3 and 4.4.3. At both positions, also briefly press ERASE + A3, so the open hat replaces the closed hat.

  5. Play the drum loop

    Press PLAY and listen to two passes, about 15 seconds, then stop.

Checkpoint: the groove runs for four bars and repeats, and the open hat at the end of bars 2 and 4 pulls you back to bar 1.

If it doesn’t: the loop repeats after one bar? The doubling didn’t happen: arm RECORD first, then SHIFT + +. SHIFT + + jumped a bar instead of doubling? RECORD wasn’t armed. Closed and open hat sound together? Briefly erase A3 at that position.

Official guide: Play and record (duplicate a pattern, navigate bars, erase)

Part 7 · Add the off-beat bassline

Goal: a four-bar bassline in group B that bounces between the kicks.

  1. B1: off-beat bass in bar 1

    Press MAIN and tap B, with playback stopped. Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Write B1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3.

    Why

    This is the classic house off-beat bass, between the kicks.

  2. Double B to four bars

    Tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + twice (one bar, then two, then four), then tap RECORD to disarm.

    Check: All four bars now have the same bass notes.

  3. B1: a gap at the end of bar 4

    Go to 4.4.3 (SHIFT + + jumps to bar 4, then + moves step by step) and briefly press ERASE + B1.

    Why

    The gap at the end of bar 4 helps the loop turn around.

  4. Checkpoint: drums and bass

    Press PLAY and listen to the drums and bass together for two passes, then stop.

  5. Optional: open sidechain

    Press SHIFT + FX (output settings), then + to reach sidechain.

    The guide doesn’t show what the display says here, so check yours, and skip this step if it isn’t clear.

    Why

    Optional, OS 2.0+: make the bass duck each time the kick hits.

  6. Optional: kick in, bass out

    Hold A and tap pad 1 to add the kick as the source. Hold SHIFT and tap B to add the bass group as the destination.

    Why

    The guide: “hold a group pad and select a sound”.

  7. Optional: shape the duck

    X sets how long the duck lasts, Y its shape. Press MAIN.

Checkpoint: the bass bounces between the kicks for four bars, with a short gap just before the loop starts again.

If it doesn’t: the bass blurs into the kick? Select B1 in SOUND, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, go to trim and turn Y (length) down a little. You can also turn Y (release) down on the envelope page, or lower X in plain SOUND. Bass only in bar 1? Check that B was selected, then double it again with RECORD armed.

Part 8 · Place the vocals in the drop

Goal: a four-bar vocal pattern in group D with the hook, answer, chops and texture.

  1. Make D four bars long

    Press MAIN and tap D, with playback stopped. Tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + twice, then tap RECORD to disarm.

    Check: D’s empty pattern is now four bars long, like A and B.

  2. D1: the hook at 1.1.1

    Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap D1, then release.

    Why

    This is the main hook, once every four bars.

  3. D2: the answer at 3.2.3

    Go to 3.2.3 (SHIFT + + to bar 3, then + step by step) and write D2, the answer.

  4. D3: two tiny chops

    Write D3 at 2.4.4 and 4.2.4: two tiny chops just before a beat.

  5. D4: the texture under the hook

    Write D4 at 1.1.1, under the hook. Then press PLAY for two passes and stop.

Checkpoint: bar 1 has the hook with the texture under it, the end of bar 2 a chop, bar 3 the answer and bar 4 another chop. The drums and bass run underneath.

If it doesn’t: a vocal plays only when you tap it? Write it again while holding RECORD. A vocal stops early? Set it to oneshot (Part 3). Hook and texture fight? Go to 1.1.1, briefly press ERASE + D4, then write D4 at 3.1.1 instead. A vocal in the wrong bar? Check the first number on the display before you write.

Official guide: Play and record (step sequence) · Get started (group D for samples)

Part 9 · Mix, add space and keep DROP A

Goal: a balanced main drop, saved as the scene DROP A.

  1. Balance the mix

    Press PLAY, then SOUND. While the loop plays, select one pad at a time and adjust its X. Press MAIN when done.

    The kick and bass are strongest, the clap a little under the kick and the hats low. D1 should be easy to understand but under the kick, D2 a bit lower, D3 and D4 low.

    Why

    Always set a pad’s volume in SOUND.

  2. D: choose reverb

    Don’t hold RECORD while you move the fader (that records the movement), and don’t hold FX while you press a group button (that solos the group).

    With the loop still playing, tap D, press FX and press − / + until you reach reverb. Raise the fader a little.

    Why

    In FX it sets how much of group D goes to the effect.

  3. Set the reverb

    X sets the reverb length, so start short. Tap A and keep its fader low; leave B at none. Press MAIN.

  4. Checkpoint: listen to the drop

    Listen to four passes at a comfortable volume, then press PLAY to stop.

  5. Write down DROP A

    Hold MAIN and read the scene number on the display. Write it beside DROP A.

  6. Commit, and name the copy DROP B

    Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN again. Write the new one beside DROP B.

    Check: The number has changed.

    Why

    DROP A is kept, and you are now in a copy of it.

Checkpoint: you want to nod along. The kick is steady, the bass bounces, the hook is easy to understand and there is a little space around the voice. The DROP A and DROP B numbers are on your paper.

If it doesn’t: words unclear? Lower the hats and the texture before you raise the hook. Muddy? Less reverb on D, none on B. A level now moves by itself every loop? You recorded fader movement: press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit (OS 2.5 can also erase fader automation with ERASE + FADER + pad). Same number after SHIFT + MAIN? Hold MAIN and select the copy, which is the new number, before you edit anything.

Official guide: Effects (fx level, reverb) · Modes (sound volume, scenes, commit) · Play and record (fader)

Part 10 · Grow it: DROP B and PEAK

Goal: two bigger versions of the drop. From now on, always copy the newest scene with SHIFT + MAIN and change a few things. Hold MAIN to check the number before every edit.

  1. D5: the second phrase in DROP B

    You are in DROP B. Press MAIN and tap D, with playback stopped. Go to 2.1.1 and write D5: the second phrase answers the hook.

  2. Commit, and name the copy PEAK

    Press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside PEAK.

  3. D3 and D5: more for PEAK

    In PEAK, with D selected, write D3 at 1.4.4 and 3.4.4 (two more chops) and D5 at 4.1.1 (the second phrase again).

  4. Listen to DROP A, DROP B and PEAK

    Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP A. Press PLAY for one pass, then stop. Do the same for DROP B, then PEAK.

Checkpoint: each scene sounds a little fuller than the one before, and DROP A has no V5.

If it doesn’t: DROP A has V5 too? You wrote it before committing. In DROP A, go to 2.1.1 and briefly press ERASE + D5. A number plays the wrong section? Step through the scenes with MAIN + − / +, listen and correct your paper.

Part 11 · Strip it down: GROOVE and INTRO

Goal: two lighter scenes, made with the official one-move clear: hold ERASE + a pad until TRK blinks.

With playback stopped, hold ERASE + a pad until TRK blinks on the display. Every note of that pad in this scene’s pattern is gone, and the sample stays. Hold MAIN first and check that you are in the right scene. SHIFT + B undoes while the umbrella is lit.

  1. Commit, and name the copy GROOVE

    In PEAK, with playback stopped, press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside GROOVE.

  2. GROOVE: clear D3, D4 and D5

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be GROOVE.

    In GROOVE, tap D. Hold ERASE + D3 until TRK blinks, then release. Do the same with D4 and D5.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: GROOVE keeps the drums, bass, hook and answer.

  3. Commit, and name the copy INTRO

    Press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside INTRO.

  4. INTRO: clear the bass and vocals

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be INTRO.

    In INTRO, tap B and hold ERASE + B1 until TRK blinks. Tap D and do the same with D1 and D2.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: INTRO is now drums only, easy for a DJ to mix in.

  5. Listen to INTRO, GROOVE and PEAK

    Hold MAIN with − / + to select each scene, and play one pass each of INTRO, GROOVE and PEAK.

Checkpoint: INTRO plays drums only. GROOVE plays drums, bass, hook and answer. PEAK still plays everything.

If it doesn’t: PEAK or GROOVE lost notes? You cleared in the wrong scene: press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit, or write the notes again from the scenes table below. TRK never appeared? Playback was running, or you let go too soon. Only one note vanished? That was a brief press: hold longer.

Part 12 · Breakdown and a live-played build

Goal: a sparse BREAK, then a BUILD where you play the kick back in live after a count-in.

  1. Commit, and name the copy BREAK

    In INTRO, with playback stopped, press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside BREAK.

  2. BREAK: clear the kick and clap

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be BREAK.

    In BREAK, tap A. Hold ERASE + A1 until TRK blinks, then do the same with A2.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: The closed and open hats stay.

  3. D1 and D4: voice in BREAK

    Tap D. At 1.1.1, hold RECORD, tap D1 and release; then hold RECORD, tap D4 and release. Go to 3.1.1 and write D4 again.

  4. Listen to BREAK

    Press PLAY for one pass and stop.

    Check: Hats, the hook and the texture, with lots of space around them.

  5. Commit, and name the copy BUILD

    Press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside BUILD.

  6. Set the grid to 4

    Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 4: four steps per bar, one per beat (the guide calls it 1/4). Press MAIN.

    Why

    Quantize (−) stays on, so every tap you record lands exactly on a beat.

  7. Practise the kick on every beat

    Practise first. Tap A and press PLAY. Without touching RECORD, tap A1 on every beat along with the hats, counting 1, 2, 3, 4.

    Press PLAY to stop when it feels steady.

  8. Arm recording with a count-in

    Now record it. With playback stopped, tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY.

    Check: After a four-beat count-in, recording starts and a metronome clicks.

    TEMPO Y sets its volume.

  9. A1: 16 kicks, one per beat

    Tap A1 on every beat through all four bars, 16 taps. Then press RECORD.

    Check: Recording stops and the loop keeps playing.

    Listen, then press PLAY to stop.

  10. Set the grid back to 16

    Press TIMING and turn X back to 16. Press MAIN. − / + now moves four steps per beat again.

  11. A2: a clap roll in bar 4

    With A selected and playback stopped, write A2 at 4.3.1, 4.3.3, 4.4.1 and 4.4.3. Press PLAY for one pass and stop.

    Why

    A clap roll that leads into the drop.

Checkpoint: BREAK has hats, hook and texture but no kick. BUILD has the same plus an even kick on every beat, and claps that speed up at the very end.

If it doesn’t: a kick missing or doubled? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again, or fix single beats by step entry at x.1.1, x.2.1, x.3.1 and x.4.1. Nothing recorded? Tap RECORD before PLAY. BREAK has a kick too? You recorded before committing: in BREAK, hold ERASE + A1 until TRK blinks. Claps on the beats instead of between them? TIMING was still 4: set 16, erase them and write them again. More live playing: Chapter 4 · Play & perform.

Part 13 · Arrange 32 positions: 128 bars, 4:00

Goal: a song list that plays the whole track in order by itself (song mode, OS 2.0+).

  1. Read the song map

    Read the song map below, with your paper beside you.

    Why

    The guide says “the length of the song position is the same as the longest pattern in the scene”. Every scene’s longest pattern is four bars, so each position plays four bars: 32 positions × 4 = 128 bars.

  2. Check every scene is four bars

    Quick length check: hold MAIN and select INTRO. With playback stopped, tap A, B and D in turn and press SHIFT + + from 1.1.1.

    Check: Each display reaches bar 4.

    Group C stays one empty bar, which is fine.

    Why

    Every scene is a copy of DROP A, so the lengths are the same everywhere.

  3. Open the song list

    Hold MAIN and press ENTER to open the song list editor.

    Why

    The display shows the current song position.

  4. Set position 1 to INTRO

    Hold SHIFT and press − / + until position 1 holds INTRO’s scene number.

  5. How to add a position

    Hold SHIFT and press A to add a position. Still holding SHIFT, press − / + to give it its scene.

    Set every position yourself: don’t assume a new one copies the last.

  6. Positions 2 to 12

    Set positions 2–4 to INTRO, 5–8 to GROOVE and 9–12 to DROP A.

  7. Positions 13 to 24

    Carry on: positions 13–16 DROP B, 17–19 BREAK, 20 BUILD, 21–24 PEAK.

  8. Positions 25 to 32

    Finish with 25–28 GROOVE and 29–32 INTRO again, now a drums-only outro for DJs.

    That makes 31 presses of SHIFT + A in total.

  9. Check all 32 positions

    Release SHIFT and step from position 1 to 32 with − / +, checking each scene number against the song map.

  10. Checkpoint: play the song

    Press SHIFT + PLAY to play the song from the start, and listen to the whole track.

Checkpoint: INTRO, GROOVE, DROP A, DROP B, BREAK, BUILD, PEAK, GROOVE, INTRO, then it stops after about 4:00. DROP A starts at 1:00, BREAK at 2:00 and PEAK at 2:30.

If it doesn’t: much shorter? A position is missing, or a scene is shorter than four bars. Longer? There are extra positions, or a pattern is longer than four bars. To remove an extra position, select it and press SHIFT + C (cut). A wrong scene on a position? Select it and hold SHIFT + − / +. Positions missing? Add them at the end with SHIFT + A, then re-check every position from the gap onwards. Keeps looping? Song loop is on: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 0, press ENTER, then MAIN. No song list on your K.O. II? Update the OS first (Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II).

Part 14 · Quality gate before you share it

Goal: check the track like a DJ would, record it, back it up and know the rules before you share it.

  1. Dance test

    Dance test: play the whole track with SHIFT + PLAY and stand up.

    Check: You should want to move in DROP A, DROP B and PEAK.

  2. Low-end test

    Low-end test: play it at low volume.

    Check: The kick and bass should still be clear.

    Not working?

    If they aren’t, shorten B1 on the trim page (Y) or lower the vocals’ X in SOUND.

  3. Voice test

    Voice test: the D1 hook should be easy to remember and understand.

    Not working?

    If the words disappear, lower D3 and D4 first.

  4. Contrast test

    Contrast test: BREAK should feel clearly emptier than the drops, and PEAK should feel like the biggest moment.

    Not working?

    If not, take more out of BREAK or add one more V3 chop to PEAK.

  5. Record one full pass

    On OS 2.5, connect the K.O. II by USB-C to a computer or phone and record its USB audio output.

    You can also record the headphone output with an external recorder.

    Why

    Record one full pass before you change anything else. Commit only copies scenes inside the K.O. II; it doesn’t export audio. The guide: “usb audio out, to record sound directly into your DAW”.

  6. Back up, and check the rights

    Back up the project with the official EP sample tool on a computer over USB-C, and keep your paper (project, scene and V1–V5 numbers) with it.

    Using anyone else’s voice or recording? For public release of music containing someone else's record, check and obtain the necessary permissions; do not assume sampling automatically grants rights.

Checkpoint: you have a recording of the full 4-minute track, a backup and your paper list.

If it doesn’t: the computer gets no audio? In your recording app, choose the K.O. II as the input, and check that the USB cable carries data (some only charge). This coach can’t record your K.O. II itself.

Going further

Resample and chop (Chapter 3): resample (RSP, OS 2.0+) records what the K.O. II itself is playing, for example your BREAK, as one new sample. Equal-length auto chop (EQU, OS 2.5) slices a vocal into even pieces, but it resets the pad assignments of the group you chop into, so use an empty group such as C, never D. Both are in Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample.

Play it live (Chapter 4): scene switching with settings 411 and 412, punch-in FX (hold FX + pads), and KEYS with key, scale and arpeggio. All in Chapter 4 · Play & perform.

Per-pad copy and paste (OS 2.5): hold a pad and press SHIFT + C to copy that pad’s notes, then hold another pad and press SHIFT + D to paste them.
