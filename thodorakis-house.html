CHAPTER 09 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Thodorakis voice house

Build a club-ready 160-bar house track at 128 BPM—about five minutes—using a normal K.O. II drum kit plus your five Thodorakis voice samples: C4, C5, C3, C7 and C2. The voice is the musical identity: main hook, answers, chops, bass-like movement and atmosphere. It is not the kick, snare or hat.

Important: Use a disposable project. Stop playback before editing positions. Use 16 timing. To remove one wrong sequenced note, navigate to it and briefly press ERASE + that pad. Never use ERASE + SOUND to remove a sequenced note: that deletes the audio sample.

Sound roles

A1
Normal punchy kick
A2
Normal clap or snare
A3
Normal closed hat
A4
Normal open hat, ride or shaker
B1
C4 main Thodorakis vocal hook
B2
C5 short vocal answer
C1
C3 tiny vocal chop
C2
C7 atmospheric vocal texture
C3
C2 second vocal phrase / variation
D1
Normal bass or low tonal sample

A dance track needs a clear low end and a clear voice. If C4 is your only low/musical element, use it sparingly and let D1 carry the bass. If C4 already works as bass, keep D1 for a sub-bass or omit it.

0 / 56 steps checked

01 / Set up a safe 128 BPM project
  1. Choose a disposable project. Press MAIN. If PLAY is lit or music runs, press PLAY once to stop it.
  2. Press TEMPO. Turn X until the display shows 128. Press MAIN.
  3. Press TIMING. Turn X until the display shows 16, not 1 or 16T. Leave swing neutral for now. Press MAIN.
  4. Check that groups A, B, C and D contain empty one-bar patterns. If they contain music you want to keep, choose another empty project.
02 / Build the normal drum kit
  1. Press SOUND and select A1. Choose a normal short punchy kick. Do not assign a Thodorakis voice sample here.
  2. Select A2 and choose a normal clap or snare. Select A3 and choose a normal closed hat.
  3. Select A4 and choose a normal open hat, ride or shaker. Keep it quieter than kick and clap.
  4. Select D1 and choose a normal bass sample, or a low tonal sound that can support the kick. If you have no suitable bass, leave D1 empty for now.
  5. Play A1, A2, A3, A4 and D1. They should sound like a drum kit and bass—not like five voice samples.
03 / Write the four-on-the-floor foundation
  1. Press MAIN, select A, and stop playback. Go to 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap A1, release.
  2. Write A1 at 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1.
  3. Write A2 at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1.
  4. Write A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3. Play one loop, then stop.
04 / Make the drums four bars long
  1. With A selected and stopped, tap RECORD to arm. Hold SHIFT and press plus once to double the pattern to two bars.
  2. Repeat SHIFT + plus to make the drum pattern four bars. Disarm RECORD.
  3. Add A3 accents at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4.
  4. Write A4 at 2.4.3 and 4.4.3. At both positions, briefly erase A3 so the open hat replaces the closed hat.
  5. Play the drum loop. Bar 4 should pull you back to bar 1.
05 / Add a simple bassline
  1. Select D. Stop playback. Arm RECORD and press plus until D is four bars.
  2. Write D1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.2, 1.3.3 and 1.4.2.
  3. Repeat that rhythm in bars 2 and 3.
  4. In bar 4, write D1 at 4.1.3, 4.2.3 and 4.3.2. Leave 4.4.3 empty.
  5. Play A and D. If they blur together, shorten D1’s tail with Y or lower its X level.
06 / Prepare the Thodorakis voice samples
  1. Press SOUND and select B1. Assign C4, your strongest vocal phrase. Trim silence from the start with X and shorten the tail with Y.
  2. Select B2 and assign C5. Choose a short word, syllable or vocal answer.
  3. Select C1 and assign C3. Choose a very short vocal chop, breath or syllable.
  4. Select C2 and assign C7 as a longer atmospheric vocal texture. Select C3 and assign C2 as a second phrase for variation.
07 / Balance the voice
  1. Set B1 (C4) clearly audible but below the kick and clap.
  2. Set B2 (C5) quieter than B1.
  3. Set C1 (C3) low; it is a rhythmic detail.
  4. Set C2 (C7) low enough to create air without hiding the words.
  5. Keep C3 (C2) for a later section. Do not use all five voice samples constantly.
08 / Place the main vocal hook
  1. Select B. Stop playback. Arm RECORD and press plus until B is four bars.
  2. Write B1 (C4) at 1.1.1. This is the main hook.
  3. Write B2 (C5) at 3.2.3 as an answer. Move it to 3.3.3 or remove it if it clashes.
  4. Play A, B and D. The kick and bass should groove while the voice feels like the feature.
09 / Add chops and atmosphere
  1. Select C. Stop playback. Arm RECORD and press plus until C is four bars.
  2. Write C1 (C3) at 2.4.4 and 4.2.4.
  3. Write C2 (C7) at 1.1.1 only.
  4. Play everything. If it feels crowded, remove C1 first—not the kick, bass or main hook.
10 / Save the main drop
  1. Listen to A, B, C and D for several loops.
  2. If C4 and C7 overlap badly, move C7 to 3.1.1 or shorten its tail.
  3. If the voice feels dry, add a small reverb send to group C. Keep group A’s FX send low.
  4. Press SHIFT + MAIN to commit this version as DROP A. Write down its scene number.
11 / Create DJ intro and groove
  1. Select DROP A and press SHIFT + MAIN to copy it as DJ INTRO. Erase B1, B2, C1, C2 and D1. Keep only drums.
  2. Select DROP A again and commit a copy as GROOVE. Erase C1 and C2, leaving drums, bass and the C4 hook.
  3. Select DROP A once more and commit a copy as DROP B. Add C3 (C2) at 2.1.1 as a second vocal phrase.
  4. Play DJ INTRO → GROOVE → DROP A → DROP B. Each section should add one clear element.
12 / Build breakdown and final drop
  1. Select DROP B and commit a copy as BREAK. Erase A1, A2, B1 and D1. Keep A3, a little A4, and C2 (C7).
  2. Select BREAK and commit a copy as BUILD. Return A1 at 1.1.1, 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1. Add A2 at 4.3.1, 4.3.3 and 4.4.3.
  3. Select DROP B and commit a copy as PEAK. Erase A1 only at 4.4.1.
  4. For OUTRO, copy GROOVE. Remove C1/C2 and extra A4 hits. Keep drums, bass and C4 hook simple.
13 / Arrange 160 bars
  1. Open the Song list with MAIN + ENTER on OS 2.0 or later. Use an unused song list.
  2. Assign positions 1–4 DJ INTRO; 5–8 GROOVE; 9–16 DROP A; 17–24 DROP B; 25–28 BREAK; 29–32 BUILD; 33–40 DROP A; 41–48 DROP B; 49–52 PEAK; 53–56 GROOVE; 57–60 OUTRO.
  3. Check every scene has a longest pattern of exactly four bars.
  4. Select Song position 1 and press SHIFT + PLAY. At 128 BPM, 160 bars is about five minutes.
14 / Quality gate before you share it
  1. Dance test: play the full track and stand up. You should want to move during DROP A, DROP B and PEAK.
  2. Low-end test: at low volume, kick and bass should still be clear. If not, shorten bass tail or reduce voice low frequencies.
  3. Voice test: the main C4 phrase should be memorable and understandable. Reduce C3/C7 if the words disappear.
  4. Contrast test: BREAK should feel noticeably emptier than DROP A/B; PEAK should feel like the biggest moment.
  5. Record one full pass before changing more. COMMIT copies a scene; it does not export audio.

Voice-first rules

ProblemFix
Voice sounds like percussionUse longer phrases, lower level, and place them off the kick
Words are unclearReduce C3/C7, shorten tails, or add only a little reverb
Track feels emptyReturn C4 or add one C2 phrase—not more drums
Track feels crowdedRemove a C3 chop or C7 texture
No dancefloor energyCheck kick, bass and drop contrast before adding sounds
