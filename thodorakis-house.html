CHAPTER 7 / ABOUT 2–3 HOURS

Vocal house

House with your own vocal samples

Build a 4-minute house track around five vocal samples of your own.

Factory drums and bass keep people moving, and your voice gives the track its identity. You build the biggest section first, then strip it back for the quieter ones.

You will make: a 128 BPM house track that plays all 4 minutes by itself.

Before you start

  • Headphones or speakers
  • Five short vocal sounds
  • Paper and a pencil
Written for OS 2.5.1. On an older OS, update first with Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II. This chapter builds on Chapter 1 · First beat, Chapter 2 · Make it a song and Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample.
  • Never use ERASE + SOUND: it deletes a sample.
  • SHIFT + B undoes a cleared note while the umbrella icon is lit.
Moves you’ll use all chapter. Stop playback before you edit. Move: in MAIN, − / + moves one step and SHIFT + − / + one bar, with RECORD off. Write a note: hold RECORD, tap the pad, release. Remove one note: go to it and briefly press ERASE + that pad. Remove all of one pad’s notes in this pattern: hold ERASE + that pad until TRK blinks. Undo: SHIFT + B while the umbrella icon is lit.
Never use ERASE + SOUND: held until SND blinks, it deletes the sample from the device.

What you need

Paper and a pencil for project, scene and sample numbers.

Five short vocal sounds. Sing into the built-in mic, or play them from a phone or computer into the 3.5 mm input or over USB-C. Audio files go across with the EP sample tool.

Use your own voice, or recordings you have permission to use.

Sound roles

The same roles as in Learn: A drums, B bass, D your own samples. Group C stays empty.

A1
Kick (factory 1–99)
A2
Clap or snare (100–199)
A3
Closed hat (200–299)
A4
Open hat (200–299) or percussion (300–399)
B1
Bass (400–499)
D1 · V1
Main hook: two to four words, under about 1.9 s (one bar at 128 BPM)
D2 · V2
Short answer: one word, about half a second
D3 · V3
Tiny chop: one syllable or a breath, about 0.2 s
D4 · V4
Texture: a long hum, “ooh” or breath, 2–4 s
D5 · V5
Second phrase: a variation of the hook, under about 1.9 s

A dance track needs a clear low end and a clear voice. Let B1 carry the bass and the voice sit on top as the feature.

Part 1 · Set up an empty 128 BPM project

Goal: an empty project at 128 BPM on a 16-step grid.

  1. Choose a project for this chapter

    Is one of projects 6–9 still empty? Use it. Otherwise, reuse the project you need least.

    For most people that is 7, the Chapter 3 practice project.

    Why

    A new unit has four empty projects, 6–9. Through the course they fill up: 6 with Chapters 1, 2 and 4, 7 with Chapter 3, 8 with Chapter 5 and 9 with Chapter 6.

  2. Reusing one? Back it up first

    Back it up with the EP sample tool, as in Chapter 0 · Set up your K.O. II, Part 4.

    Check: A new backup file appears in your browser’s downloads.

    Your recordings stay in the sound library either way.

    Why

    You can restore single projects from a backup.

  3. Open the project

    Press MAIN. If music is playing, press PLAY to stop it. Then hold MAIN, hold the project’s pad as well, and release both.

    Check: While you hold both, the display shows P and the project’s number.

  4. Set the tempo to 128 BPM

    Press TEMPO and turn X until the display shows 128. Press MAIN.

    Check: The display shows 128 before you press MAIN.

    Why

    At 128 BPM one bar lasts 1.875 seconds and a four-bar loop 7.5 seconds.

  5. Set the grid to 16

    Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 16, not 1, 4 or 16T.

    Check: The display shows 16: 16 steps per bar.

  6. Quantize on, swing alone

    Press − to choose quantize: it snaps recorded notes to the grid. Leave Y (swing) alone. Press MAIN.

    Check: The MAIN icon is lit again.

  7. Listen: is the project empty?

    Press PLAY. Silence means an empty project: press PLAY again to stop and go on.

    Check: Nothing plays: the project is empty.

    Music? Press PLAY to stop, and erase nothing yet. Your Chapter 1–2, 5 or 6 track means MAIN + the pad switched scene, not project: go back to step 3.

  8. Old music? Clear the patterns

    Only for the reused project’s own old music, and only after a backup.

    Erases the current pattern of every group.

    Hold ERASE and A until PTN blinks, then do the same with B, C and D.

    SHIFT + B restores only the last group. The backup has the rest.

    Check: PTN blinks for each group, and PLAY plays nothing.

    In project 7, that is your Chapter 3 voice kit or bounced beat. Press PLAY again to stop.

    Why

    ERASE + a group erases its current pattern.

  9. Reused project 7? Empty D1 to D5

    With playback stopped, press SOUND, tap D and tap D1. Hold SOUND, type 0, 0, 0 and release SOUND. Repeat for D2 to D5.

    Check: D1 to D5 are now empty and play nothing.

    Your Chapter 3 recordings stay in the sound library. Pad settings stay too: Chapter 3 left A4 reversed and B1 stretched to one bar. A pad later plays backwards, stretched or cut short? Reset it in SOUND EDIT, as in Chapter 3, Parts 5 and 8.

    Why

    In a reused project 7, D1 to D5 still hold your Chapter 3 recordings, so they don’t blink in SAMPLE and can’t record hands-free. Sample number 000 leaves a pad empty.

  10. Write the project and V1 to V5

    On your paper, write the project number, then V1 to V5 with space beside each for a sample number.

  11. Write the scene names

    Below that, write DROP A, DROP B, PEAK, GROOVE, INTRO, BREAK and BUILD, each with space for its scene number.

Checkpoint: PLAY plays nothing, TEMPO shows 128 and TIMING shows 16.

If it doesn’t: PLAY still plays music after step 7? MAIN + the pad switched scene, not project. Hold MAIN first, then the project’s pad until the display shows P and its number, and repeat steps 3 and 7. Erased the wrong project? Press SHIFT + B at once while the umbrella is lit, or restore your backup. Not sure which project to give up? See help “My project is not empty”.

Part 2 · Get your five vocals onto D1–D5

Goal: your five vocals on pads D1 to D5, sampled as in Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample.

  1. Plan your five vocals

    Decide what V1 to V5 will be. Keep V1 and V5 under about 1.9 seconds, one bar, and V3 tiny.

    See “Sound roles” above. Your own voice is perfect: sing, hum, speak or whisper. Someone else’s recording? Read the rights note in Part 14 first.

  2. Open SAMPLE on group D

    Press MAIN and tap D. Press SAMPLE.

    Check: The lights blink while you are in SAMPLE.

  3. Choose the source

    Press − / + to choose the source.

    Check: The display shows your source: mic, in or usb.

    mic: the built-in microphone. in: the 3.5 mm input, for a phone’s headphone output on a cable. usb: a phone or computer on USB-C, with the K.O. II chosen as its audio output.

    Why

    Mono is fine for a voice.

  4. Set the input level

    While you sing or play the vocal, turn X (input level) up until it is loud but clean, never distorted.

    Check: The lights keep blinking: you are still in SAMPLE.

  5. Set the threshold low

    Turn Y (threshold) low, so recording starts as soon as you make a sound.

    Check: The lights still blink: nothing records until you hold a pad.

  6. D1: record V1

    Get ready, then press and hold D1. Perform V1, and release right after the last word.

    Check: The pad stops blinking and stays lit: it now has a sample.

    Need both hands? In SAMPLE, SHIFT + an empty, blinking pad starts hands-free recording, and pressing SAMPLE stops it.

  7. Listen to D1

    Press MAIN and tap D1 to hear it.

    Check: D1 plays V1, perhaps with a little silence around it.

    A little silence around it is fine: Part 3 trims it.

  8. D2 to D5: record V2 to V5

    Press SAMPLE again, with D still selected. Record V2 on D2, V3 on D3, V4 on D4 and V5 on D5 the same way.

    Check: D2 to D5 stop blinking and stay lit.

  9. Listen to D2 to D5

    In MAIN, tap each one to hear it.

    Check: D2 to D5 play V2 to V5.

    Not working?

    A bad take? Record that pad again. The pad gets the new take, and the old one stays in the sound library.

  10. Audio files? Transfer them

    Connect the K.O. II to a computer by USB-C, open teenage.engineering/apps/ep-sample-tool and transfer the five files.

    Check: The tool shows your K.O. II, and each file gets a sample number.

    Recorded on the K.O. II? Tick this step and move on.

  11. Load each file onto its pad

    For each vocal, press SOUND, tap D and tap its pad. Hold SOUND, type its sample number and release SOUND.

    Check: D1 to D5 now play V1 to V5.

    D1 for V1 and so on.

    Not working?

    Not sure of a number? Select the pad in SOUND and press − / + until you hear the vocal. SHIFT + − / + jumps ten numbers.

  12. Vocals already on pads?

    In your Chapter 3 project, for example? First write down each vocal’s sample number.

  13. Read each number, then come back

    In that project, press SOUND, select the pad and read the display. Then hold MAIN and this project’s pad to come back.

    Check: The display shows each pad’s sample number.

  14. Load D1 to D5 by number

    Load the vocals onto D1 to D5 by number, as for audio files above.

    Check: D1 to D5 play V1 to V5 in this project.

    Why

    Loading or pasting a sound only changes which sample a pad plays. The sample stays in the library.

  15. Or copy a sound inside one project

    Paste only onto an empty pad, so no vocal is lost.

    In SOUND, select the source pad and press SHIFT + C, then select an empty pad and press SHIFT + D.

    Check: The empty pad now plays the source pad’s sound.

    Why

    Copying a sound saves looking up its number.

  16. Write down the sample numbers

    Press SOUND, tap D, then tap D1. Write the number beside V1. Do the same for D2 to D5.

    Check: The display shows a number.

    Why

    The number on the display is the sample on that pad. With it you can always put a vocal back: select the pad, hold SOUND, type the number and release.

Checkpoint: D1 to D5 play V1 to V5, and their numbers are on your paper.

If it doesn’t: recording never starts? Lower Y (threshold), raise X and check the source on the display. A pad is silent? Record it again, or load the sample by number. No vocals yet? Load five factory melodic sounds (500–599) onto D1 to D5 as stand-ins, and swap your vocals in later by number.

Part 3 · Trim the vocals and make them play in full

Goal: each vocal starts on its first sound and plays to the end.

A brief press of SHIFT + SOUND opens sound edit, which is safe. Never hold SHIFT + SOUND on the trim page: that crops the file on disk, for good. Ordinary trimming can always be changed back.

  1. D1: open the trim page

    Press SOUND, tap D and tap D1. Briefly press SHIFT + SOUND to open sound edit, then press − / + to the trim page.

    Check: The display shows TRI.

    The six pages show as SND, TRI, ENV, TIM, MID and GRP: sound, trim, envelope, time, midi and mute group. Stop at TRI.

  2. Trim the start of V1

    Turn X (start) a little at a time, tapping D1 between turns, until playback begins right before the first sound. Don’t cut into the first word.

    Check: D1 now starts right on its first sound.

  3. Trim the end of V1

    Stay on trim. Turn Y (length) down until playback ends right after the last word. Press MAIN when it sounds right.

    Check: D1 stops right after the last word.

    For smaller steps, hold SHIFT while you turn, and keep your fingers off SOUND while SHIFT is down.

  4. Trim D2 to D5

    Trim D2 to D5 the same way.

    Check: Each D pad starts on its first sound and ends cleanly.

    V2: one word, about half a second. V3: one syllable or breath, about 0.2 seconds. V4: long and smooth. V5: the phrase only.

  5. D1: set oneshot

    Select D1 in SOUND, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND and press − / + to reach the sound page. Turn X until the display shows oneshot.

    Check: D1 now plays the whole vocal from one tap.

    Why

    Oneshot plays the whole sample from a single tap, one at a time.

  6. D2 to D5: oneshot too

    Repeat for D2 to D5.

    Check: D2 to D5 each play the whole vocal from one tap.

  7. Optional: reverse V4

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Select D4, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, press + until the display shows tim, then turn X until it shows rev. Tap D4.

    Don’t like it? Turn X back to where it was.

    Check: D4 plays backwards.

    Check its trim again afterwards.

    Why

    Reversed, V4 swells in instead of fading out.

  8. Set the vocal levels

    Press SOUND once: plain SOUND, not sound edit. Tap each D pad and set its X (amplitude). Leave Y (pitch) alone.

    Check: D1 and D5 clear, D2 a little quieter, D3 and D4 low.

    Why

    You balance against the drums in Part 9.

Checkpoint: one short tap on each D pad plays the whole vocal, with no silence in front.

If it doesn’t: stops early? Check oneshot on the sound page, then trim Y. Starts late? Turn trim X a little further. Higher or lower than before? You turned Y in plain SOUND: turn it back.

Part 4 · Choose the drum kit and the bass

Goal: four factory drum sounds on A1 to A4 and one factory bass on B1.

  1. A1: load sample 001

    Press SOUND, tap A, tap A1. Hold SOUND, type 1 on the pads and release SOUND to load sample 001. Tap A1.

    Check: A1 plays a factory kick.

    Want a different kick? Press − / + within 1–99 and choose a short, punchy one. SHIFT + − / + jumps ten.

  2. A2: a clap or snare

    Tap A2. To reach the snares, 100–199, hold SOUND, type 1, 0, 0 and release. Choose a clap or snare.

    Check: A2 plays a clap or snare.

    No clap you like there? Try percussion, 300–399.

  3. A3: a closed hat

    Tap A3 and choose a short closed hat from 200–299.

    Check: A3 plays a short closed hat.

  4. A4: an open hat

    Tap A4 and choose an open hat, 200–299, or a shaker from the percussion, 300–399. Turn X down below the kick and clap.

    Check: A4 plays an open hat, quieter than the kick and clap.

  5. B1: choose a bass

    Tap B, then B1. Hold SOUND, type 4, 0, 0 and release, then press − / + within 400–499 for a short, round bass.

    Check: B1 plays a short, round bass.

  6. Turn the bass down

    Turn X down so it sits under the kick.

    Check: B1 now sounds quieter than the kick.

  7. Play the kit

    Press MAIN. Tap A and play A1, A2, A3 and A4. Tap B and play B1.

    Check: You hear kick, clap, closed hat, open hat and bass.

Checkpoint: kick, clap, two hats and bass play, and D1 to D5 still play your vocals.

If it doesn’t: a vocal plays on an A or B pad? You loaded it with D selected. Tap the right group, select the pad and load the factory number again. Your vocal is safe: put it back on its D pad by number.

Part 5 · Write the four-on-the-floor bar

Goal: one bar of house drums in group A.

  1. Go to 1.1.1 with group A

    Press MAIN, tap A and check that playback is stopped. Hold SHIFT and press − until the display shows 1.1.1.

    Check: The display shows 1.1.1, the first step of the pattern.

  2. A1: the first kick

    Hold RECORD, tap A1, then release RECORD.

    Check: A1 lights at 1.1.1.

  3. A1: a kick on every beat

    Press + until the display shows 1.2.1 and write A1 the same way. Repeat at 1.3.1 and 1.4.1.

    Check: A1 lights at each of the four positions.

    Why

    A kick on every beat is the “four on the floor”.

  4. A2: claps on 2 and 4

    Write A2 at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1, beats 2 and 4.

    Check: A2 lights at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1.

  5. A3: off-beat hats

    Write A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3, the “and” between the beats. Then press PLAY, listen to a few loops and press PLAY to stop.

    Check: You hear boom, tss, clap, tss on repeat.

Checkpoint: boom, tss, clap, tss on repeat: a kick on every beat, claps on 2 and 4, hats in between.

If it doesn’t: a sound plays only while you tap it? Hold RECORD while you tap. An extra hit? Go to it and briefly press ERASE + that pad, or press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit. The display shows a sample number, not a position? You are still in SOUND: press MAIN.

Part 6 · Make the drums four bars long

Goal: a four-bar drum loop with small changes at the end of bars 2 and 4.

  1. Double A to two bars

    With A selected and playback stopped, tap RECORD to arm it. Hold SHIFT and press + once.

    Check: The display shows Ln.2: bar 2 plays bar 1’s notes.

  2. Double again to four bars

    Still armed, hold SHIFT and press + once more: four bars. Tap RECORD to disarm.

    Check: The display shows Ln.4: the pattern is four bars long.

  3. A3: two hat accents

    Disarmed and stopped, press SHIFT + + to jump to bar 2, then + until the display shows 2.2.4, and write A3. Do the same at 4.2.4.

    Check: A3 lights at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4.

  4. A4: open hats instead of closed

    Press ERASE + A3 briefly: a long hold clears every closed hat.

    Removes only those two A3 notes.

    Write A4 at 2.4.3 and 4.4.3. At both, briefly press ERASE + A3.

    Wrong note? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: At 2.4.3 and 4.4.3 A4 lights and A3 does not.

  5. Play the drum loop

    Press PLAY and listen to two passes, about 15 seconds, then stop.

    Check: Four bars repeat, with open hats ending bars 2 and 4.

Checkpoint: the groove runs four bars, and the open hats ending bars 2 and 4 pull you back to bar 1.

If it doesn’t: the loop repeats after one bar? Arm RECORD first, then press SHIFT + +. Without RECORD armed, it jumps a bar instead. Closed and open hat together? Briefly erase A3 at that position.

Part 7 · Add the off-beat bassline

Goal: a four-bar bassline in group B that bounces between the kicks.

  1. B1: off-beat bass in bar 1

    Press MAIN and tap B, with playback stopped. Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Write B1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3.

    Check: B1 lights at all four positions.

    Why

    The classic house bass: off the beat, between the kicks.

  2. Double B to four bars

    Tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + twice, for four bars. Tap RECORD to disarm.

    Check: The display shows Ln.4: all four bars play the same bass.

  3. B1: a gap at the end of bar 4

    Press briefly: a long hold clears the whole bassline.

    Removes only the B1 note at 4.4.3.

    Go to 4.4.3: SHIFT + + jumps to bar 4, then + moves step by step. Briefly press ERASE + B1.

    Wrong note? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: At 4.4.3 B1 no longer lights.

    Why

    The gap at the end of bar 4 helps the loop turn around.

  4. Checkpoint: drums and bass

    Press PLAY and listen to the drums and bass together for two passes, then stop.

    Check: The bass bounces between kicks, with a gap before the loop restarts.

  5. Optional: open sidechain

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SHIFT + FX for output settings, then + to reach sidechain.

    Check: The display shows OUT, then S.Ch after +.

    Why

    Sidechain makes the bass duck each time the kick hits.

  6. Optional: kick in, bass out

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold A and tap pad 1: the kick sets off the duck. Hold SHIFT and tap B: the bass group ducks.

    Check: During playback, the bass now ducks at each kick.

    Why

    Holding a group and tapping a pad picks one sound.

  7. Optional: shape the duck

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Turn X to set how long the duck lasts and Y its shape, then press MAIN.

    Check: The display shows LEN as you turn X, ShP for Y.

    Why

    LEN is the length of each duck and ShP its shape.

Checkpoint: the bass bounces between the kicks for four bars, with a short gap before the loop restarts.

If it doesn’t: the bass blurs into the kick? Select B1 in SOUND, briefly press SHIFT + SOUND, go to trim and turn Y (length) down a little. Bass only in bar 1? Check that B was selected, then double it again with RECORD armed.

Part 8 · Place the vocals in the drop

Goal: a four-bar vocal pattern in group D with the hook, answer, chops and texture.

  1. Make D four bars long

    Press MAIN and tap D, with playback stopped. Tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + twice, then tap RECORD to disarm.

    Check: The display shows Ln.4: four bars, like A and B.

  2. D1: the hook at 1.1.1

    Hold SHIFT and press − until 1.1.1. Hold RECORD, tap D1, then release.

    Check: D1 lights at 1.1.1.

    Why

    The main hook, once every four bars.

  3. D2: the answer at 3.2.3

    Go to 3.2.3: SHIFT + + to bar 3, then + step by step. Write D2, the answer.

    Check: D2 lights at 3.2.3.

  4. D3: two tiny chops

    Write D3 at 2.4.4 and 4.2.4: two tiny chops right before a beat.

    Check: D3 lights at 2.4.4 and 4.2.4.

  5. D4: the texture under the hook

    Write D4 at 1.1.1, under the hook. Then press PLAY for two passes and stop.

    Check: Hook and texture start bar 1, over the drums and bass.

Checkpoint: over the drums and bass, bar 1 has the hook and texture, bar 2 ends with a chop, bar 3 has the answer and bar 4 another chop.

If it doesn’t: a vocal plays only when you tap it? Write it again while holding RECORD. A vocal stops early? Set it to oneshot (Part 3). Hook and texture fight? Go to 1.1.1, briefly press ERASE + D4, then write D4 at 3.1.1 instead.

Part 9 · Mix, add space and keep DROP A

Goal: a balanced main drop, saved as the scene DROP A.

  1. Balance the mix

    Press PLAY, then SOUND. While the loop plays, select one pad at a time and adjust its X. Press MAIN when done.

    Check: Kick and bass lead, and the hook is clear.

    Kick and bass strongest, the clap a little under the kick, the hats low. D1 clear but under the kick, D2 a bit lower, D3 and D4 low.

    Why

    Always set a pad’s volume in SOUND.

  2. Add reverb to the vocals

    Don’t hold RECORD while you move the fader, or FX while you press a group button.

    With the loop still playing, tap D, press FX and press − / + until you reach reverb. Raise the fader a little.

    Check: A little space opens around the vocals.

    Why

    In FX, the fader sets how much of D goes to the effect. Holding RECORD records the fader move, and holding FX solos the group.

  3. Set the reverb

    Turn X low for a short reverb. Tap A and keep its fader low, and leave B at none. Press MAIN.

    Check: The vocals get a short tail, and the bass has no reverb.

  4. Checkpoint: listen to the drop

    Listen to four passes at a comfortable volume, then press PLAY to stop.

    Check: Steady kick, bouncing bass and a clear hook.

  5. Write down DROP A

    Hold MAIN and read the scene number on the display. Write it beside DROP A.

    Check: While you hold MAIN, the display shows a scene number.

  6. Commit, and name the copy DROP B

    Release MAIN and press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN again and write the new number beside DROP B.

    Check: The number has changed.

    Why

    Commit keeps DROP A and moves you into a copy.

Checkpoint: a steady kick, a bouncing bass and a clear hook, with DROP A and DROP B on your paper.

If it doesn’t: words unclear or muddy? Lower the hats and the texture, and use less reverb on D, none on B. Same number after SHIFT + MAIN? Hold MAIN and select the copy, the new number, before you edit. A level moves by itself every loop? You recorded a fader move: press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit, or erase it with ERASE + FADER + pad.

Part 10 · Grow it: DROP B and PEAK

Goal: two bigger versions of the drop, each a copy of the scene before.

  1. D5: the second phrase in DROP B

    In DROP B, with playback stopped, press MAIN and tap D. Go to 2.1.1 and write D5.

    Check: D5 lights at 2.1.1.

  2. Commit, and name the copy PEAK

    Press SHIFT + MAIN once. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside PEAK.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not DROP B’s.

  3. D3 and D5: more for PEAK

    In PEAK, with D selected, write D3 at 1.4.4 and 3.4.4, and D5 at 4.1.1.

    Check: D3 lights at 1.4.4 and 3.4.4, D5 at 4.1.1.

  4. Listen to DROP A, DROP B and PEAK

    Hold MAIN and use − / + to select DROP A. Press PLAY for one pass, then stop. Do the same for DROP B, then PEAK.

    Check: Each scene sounds fuller than the last, and DROP A has no V5.

Checkpoint: each scene sounds a little fuller than the one before, and DROP A has no V5.

If it doesn’t: DROP A has V5 too? You wrote it before committing: in DROP A, go to 2.1.1 and briefly press ERASE + D5. A number plays the wrong section? Step through the scenes with MAIN + − / +, listen and correct your paper.

Part 11 · Strip it down: GROOVE and INTRO

Goal: two lighter scenes, made by clearing whole pads.

With playback stopped, hold ERASE + a pad until TRK blinks: every note of that pad in this scene is gone. The sample stays. Hold MAIN first to check you are in the right scene. SHIFT + B undoes while the umbrella is lit.

  1. Commit, and name the copy GROOVE

    In PEAK, with playback stopped, press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside GROOVE.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not PEAK’s.

  2. GROOVE: clear D3, D4 and D5

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be GROOVE.

    Clears every note of D3, D4 and D5 in GROOVE.

    Tap D. Hold ERASE + D3 until TRK blinks, then release. Do the same with D4 and D5.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: drums, bass, hook and answer, without D3–D5. Stop.

  3. Commit, and name the copy INTRO

    Press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside INTRO.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not GROOVE’s.

  4. INTRO: clear the bass and vocals

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be INTRO.

    Clears every note of B1, D1 and D2 in INTRO.

    In INTRO, tap B and hold ERASE + B1 until TRK blinks. Tap D and do the same with D1 and D2.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: INTRO plays drums only, no bass or vocals. Stop.

  5. Listen to INTRO, GROOVE and PEAK

    Hold MAIN with − / + to select each scene, and play one pass each of INTRO, GROOVE and PEAK.

    Check: INTRO: drums only. GROOVE: drums, bass, hook, answer. PEAK: everything.

Checkpoint: INTRO plays drums only, GROOVE drums, bass, hook and answer, and PEAK everything.

If it doesn’t: PEAK or GROOVE lost notes? You cleared in the wrong scene: press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit, or write them again from “Your scenes” below. TRK never appeared, or only one note went? Stop playback first, and hold until TRK blinks.

Part 12 · Breakdown and a live-played build

Goal: a sparse BREAK, then a BUILD where you play the kick back in live.

  1. Commit, and name the copy BREAK

    In INTRO, with playback stopped, press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside BREAK.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not INTRO’s.

  2. BREAK: clear the kick and clap

    Hold MAIN first: the number must be BREAK.

    Clears every note of A1 and A2 in BREAK.

    In BREAK, tap A. Hold ERASE + A1 until TRK blinks, then do the same with A2.

    Wrong pad? Press SHIFT + B at once, while the umbrella is lit.

    Check: Press PLAY: the hats still play, with no kick or clap. Stop.

  3. D1 and D4: voice in BREAK

    Tap D, go to 1.1.1, then hold RECORD, tap D1 and release. Hold RECORD again, tap D4 and release. Go to 3.1.1 and write D4 again.

    Check: D1 and D4 light at 1.1.1, D4 again at 3.1.1.

  4. Listen to BREAK

    Press PLAY for one pass and stop.

    Check: Hats, the hook and the texture, with lots of space around them.

  5. Commit, and name the copy BUILD

    Press SHIFT + MAIN. Hold MAIN and write the new number beside BUILD.

    Check: Holding MAIN shows a new number, not BREAK’s.

  6. Set the grid to 4

    Press TIMING and turn X until the display shows 4: one step per beat. Press MAIN.

    Check: The display shows 4: one step per beat.

    Why

    Quantize stays on, so every tap you record lands exactly on a beat.

  7. Practise the kick on every beat

    Tap A and press PLAY. Without touching RECORD, tap A1 on every beat along with the hats, counting 1, 2, 3, 4.

    Check: Each tap plays a kick over the hats; nothing records.

    Press PLAY to stop when it feels steady.

  8. Arm recording with a count-in

    Now record it. With playback stopped, tap and release RECORD, then press PLAY.

    Check: After a four-beat count-in, recording starts and a metronome clicks.

    TEMPO Y sets its volume.

  9. A1: 16 kicks, one per beat

    Tap A1 on every beat through all four bars, 16 taps. Then press RECORD.

    Check: Recording stops and the loop keeps playing.

    Listen, then press PLAY to stop.

  10. Set the grid back to 16

    Press TIMING and turn X back to 16. Press MAIN. − / + now moves four steps per beat again.

    Check: The display shows 16 again: 16 steps per bar.

  11. A2: a clap roll in bar 4

    With A selected and playback stopped, write A2 at 4.3.1, 4.3.3, 4.4.1 and 4.4.3. Press PLAY for one pass and stop.

    Check: A roll of four claps ends bar 4.

    Why

    The roll leads the ear into the drop.

Checkpoint: BREAK has no kick, and BUILD brings it back on every beat, with a clap roll at the end.

If it doesn’t: a kick missing or doubled? Press SHIFT + B while the umbrella is lit and record again. Claps on the beats, not between? TIMING was still 4: set 16, erase them and write them again. BREAK has a kick too? You recorded before committing: in BREAK, hold ERASE + A1 until TRK blinks.

Part 13 · Arrange 32 positions: 128 bars, 4:00

Goal: a song list that plays the whole track in order, by itself.

  1. Read the song map

    Read the song map below, with your paper beside you.

    Why

    A song position lasts as long as the longest pattern in its scene. Every scene here is four bars, so 32 positions × 4 = 128 bars.

  2. Check every scene is four bars

    Hold MAIN and select INTRO. With playback stopped, tap A, B and D in turn and press SHIFT + + from 1.1.1.

    Check: Each display reaches bar 4.

    Group C stays one empty bar, which is fine.

    Why

    Every scene is a copy of DROP A, so the lengths are the same everywhere.

  3. Open the song list

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold MAIN and press ENTER to open the song list.

    Check: The display shows the current song position.

    Why

    Each position holds one scene.

  4. Set position 1 to INTRO

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold SHIFT and press − / + until position 1 holds INTRO’s scene number.

    Check: Position 1 now holds INTRO.

  5. Add position 2: INTRO

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Hold SHIFT and press A to add position 2. Still holding SHIFT, press − / + to set it to INTRO.

    Check: Position 2 now holds INTRO too.

    Set every position yourself: don’t assume a new one copies the last.

  6. Positions 3 to 12

    Set positions 3 and 4 to INTRO, 5–8 to GROOVE and 9–12 to DROP A.

    Check: Twelve positions so far, matching the song map.

  7. Positions 13 to 24

    Carry on: positions 13–16 DROP B, 17–19 BREAK, 20 BUILD, 21–24 PEAK.

    Check: 24 positions so far, matching the song map.

  8. Positions 25 to 32

    Finish with 25–28 GROOVE and 29–32 INTRO again, now a drums-only outro for DJs.

    Check: 32 positions in all, and the last four hold INTRO.

    That makes 31 presses of SHIFT + A in total.

  9. Check all 32 positions

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Release SHIFT and step from position 1 to 32 with − / +, checking each scene number against the song map.

    Check: All 32 positions match the song map.

  10. Checkpoint: play the song

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Press SHIFT + PLAY to play the song from the start, and listen to the whole track.

    Check: The sections play in order and stop after about 4:00.

Checkpoint: INTRO, GROOVE, DROP A, DROP B, BREAK, BUILD, PEAK, GROOVE, INTRO, then it stops after about 4:00.

If it doesn’t: too short or too long? Compare each position with the song map. SHIFT + C cuts an extra one, and SHIFT + A adds a missing one at the end: then re-check every position from the gap onwards. Wrong scene on a position? Select it and hold SHIFT + − / +. Keeps looping? Song loop is on: press SHIFT + ERASE, type 4, 2, 0, press ENTER, then MAIN.

Part 14 · Quality gate before you share it

Goal: test the track, record it and back it up before you share it.

  1. Dance test

    Needs OS 2.0 or newer

    Play the whole track with SHIFT + PLAY and stand up.

    Check: DROP A, DROP B and PEAK hit harder than INTRO and GROOVE.

  2. Low-end test

    Play the track at low volume.

    Check: The kick and bass are still clear.

    Not working?

    Shorten B1 with trim Y, or lower the vocals’ X in SOUND.

  3. Voice test

    Play the track again and listen to the D1 hook.

    Check: You understand every word of the D1 hook.

    Not working?

    Lower D3 and D4 first.

  4. Contrast test

    Play BREAK, then DROP A and PEAK, and compare. Hold MAIN with − / + to switch.

    Check: BREAK is clearly emptier, and PEAK is the biggest moment.

    Not working?

    Take more out of BREAK, or add one more V3 chop to PEAK.

  5. Record one full pass

    Needs OS 2.5 or newer

    Connect the K.O. II by USB-C to a computer or phone and record its USB audio output.

    Check: Your recording app plays back the full 4-minute track.

    You can also record the headphone output with an external recorder.

    Why

    Record a full pass before you change anything else: commit copies scenes inside the K.O. II but exports no audio. USB audio out sends the sound straight into your recording app.

  6. Back up, and check the rights

    Back up the project with the EP sample tool on a computer over USB-C. Keep your paper with the backup.

    Check: A new backup file appears in your browser’s downloads.

    Using someone else’s voice or recording? Get permission before you release the track. Sampling it doesn’t give you the rights.

  7. Your vocal house moves

    Read the seven moves below, then try one from memory.

    • Load a sample by number: SOUND, tap the pad, hold SOUND, type the number, release.
    • Trim: briefly SHIFT + SOUND, − / + to the trim page; X start, Y length.
    • Play to the end: on the sound page, turn X to oneshot.
    • Reverb on a group: tap it, FX, − / + to reverb, raise the fader.
    • Grow and strip: SHIFT + MAIN, then hold ERASE + the pad until TRK blinks to clear one sound.
    • Play it in: tap RECORD, press PLAY, play after the count-in.
    • Song list: hold MAIN and press ENTER; SHIFT + A adds a position, SHIFT + − / + picks its scene.

Checkpoint: you have a recording of the full track, a backup and your paper list.

If it doesn’t: the computer gets no audio? In your recording app, choose the K.O. II as the input. Some USB cables only charge: try one that carries data.

Quick fixes

ProblemFix
The voice sounds like percussionUse the longer phrases (V1, V5), lower V3, and keep vocals off the kick positions
The words are unclearLower the hats and V4 first, trim the tails, and keep only a little reverb on D
A vocal is cut shortSet oneshot on the sound page, then check trim Y
The track feels emptyBring back V1 or add one V5 phrase, not more drums
The track feels crowdedRemove a V3 chop or the V4 texture
No dancefloor energyCheck the kick, the bass and the contrast between BREAK and the drops before adding sounds

Going further

Resample and chop: resample records what the K.O. II is playing, your BREAK for example, as one new sample. Auto chop slices a vocal into even pieces, but resets the pads of the group you chop into, so use empty group C, never D. Both are in Chapter 3 · Sample, chop & resample.

Play it live: scene switching, punch-in FX and KEYS with scale and arpeggio, in Chapter 4 · Play & perform.

Per-pad copy and paste: hold a pad and press SHIFT + C to copy its notes, then hold another pad and press SHIFT + D to paste them.
